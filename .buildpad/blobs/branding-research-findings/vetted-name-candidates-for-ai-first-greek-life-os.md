# Name clearance for the Greek-life AI OS: the short-abstract-word lane is heavily mined, and only a handful of candidates survive

**Bottom line: there is no pristine, conflict-free name in your chosen lane — every one of the 29 candidates checked has *some* existing software/app footprint. But the risk is not uniform.** The decisive filter is whether a name carries a **live registered trademark in software/computer classes** or an **active, funded AI competitor on the exact word** — those are the real disqualifiers, versus mere usage in unrelated or niche industries. On that filter, most of your previously-proposed short list should be dropped: **Hearth, Loft, Cadre, and Vela all carry live registered software trademarks**, and **Chaptr collides with an AI startup literally named CHAPTR** (plus it's an off-strategy "chapter" descriptor). Of everything checked, the genuinely-clearer survivors are **Signet, Lodge, Crest, Sextant, Charter, and Sconce** — each has no registered exact-word software mark and no direct AI competitor, but each also has caveats, and **every one still requires a formal USPTO/TESS clearance before you commit**. The honest strategic takeaway: the "short warm real word" lane you (correctly) chose is exactly the lane every AI SaaS startup is fishing in, so you may get a cleaner, more ownable mark from a slight coinage or two-word compound than from another common dictionary word.

---

## How to read the risk tiers

I sorted every candidate into three tiers by the *kind* of conflict, because a registered trademark is a legal blocker while niche unrelated usage is mostly a marketing nuisance:

- **DROP (registered software trademark, or a prominent/funded direct AI competitor on the exact name).** Real legal/strategic collision.
- **HIGH RISK (an active AI or communication/productivity product uses the exact word, but no confirmed registration).** Confusion and future-trademark risk; avoid unless you love it and counsel clears it.
- **CLEARER (software usage exists only in niche/enterprise/unrelated fields, and the exact-word software trademark is dead, abandoned, or never found).** The pool worth taking to formal clearance.

## 4. Final ranked shortlist

All six below still require a **formal USPTO/TESS trademark search (ideally with counsel)** before you commit — this list clears web/app-store/aggregator scanning, not legal clearance. Ranked by combined trademark clarity, absence of AI competitor, brand fit to "an AI-first OS for Greek life," and texting/audience fit.

1. **Signet** — *Top pick.* A signet ring is a fraternal-identity object, and "signet = seal of authenticity/trust" maps cleanly onto the differentiator (an AI that gives *trusted, sourced* answers from your chapter's own records). No registered software word mark found; collisions are jewelry (unrelated) and a small email-security SaaS. Clean to say and text. **Formal USPTO search required** [justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/905/08/signet-90508261.html) [withsignet](https://withsignet.com/about).

2. **Lodge** — Strongest heritage fit: a "lodge" *is* a fraternal chapter-house, warm and home-base without being the banned literal descriptor "frat/Greek/chapter." No registered software word mark; collisions are real-estate and hotel-PMS software (adjacent, not AI, not Greek). One-syllable, instantly textable. **Formal USPTO search required** [apple](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lodge-inc/id1471156745) [lodgegate](https://lodgegate.com/en/).

3. **Crest** — Fraternity crests are core identity iconography, and "reach the crest" adds aspiration. Exact-word CREST mark is expired and no AI player exists — but the space is crowded with small generic "Crest Software" vendors, so it's the least *ownable* of the top three. **Formal USPTO search required** [crestsoft](https://www.crestsoft.com/) [justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/715/62/crest-71562961.html).

4. **Sextant** — Best *pure trademark* position (only a dead mark) and a strong wayfinding/"guide-your-chapter" metaphor in the torch vein. Ranked below the top three only because of the **"sex-" prefix awkwardness for a college Greek audience** and low word recognition. **Formal USPTO search required** [trademarkia](https://www.trademarkia.com/sextant-76557259).

5. **Sconce** — Distinctive, likely-available domains, and a genuine light/illumination metaphor (your "torch" instinct) that no software competitor holds. Held back by low recognition and non-obvious spelling — most members won't know what it means. Viable if you're willing to build meaning into it. **Formal USPTO search required** [pdsvision](https://pdsvision.com/press/sconce-becomes-pdsvision/).

6. **Charter** — Credible governance/founding-document meaning that resonates with national orgs and treasurers ("chapter charter"), and no standalone software word mark. The problem is ownability: Charter Communications dominates the word, so `.com` and search are effectively lost. Keep only if the governance framing outweighs the SEO/domain penalty. **Formal USPTO search required** [softwaresuggest](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/charter).

**Recommended path:** take **Signet, Lodge, and Crest** to formal USPTO/TESS clearance first — they combine the cleanest conflict profile with the best texting/recognition fit. If all three fail formal clearance, the round-one pattern strongly suggests your next-best move is not another common dictionary word but a **slight coinage or two-word compound** (which is where an ownable, registrable mark actually lives in a lane this crowded).

## Where more research would most change these conclusions

Two gaps are worth closing before you commit design work. First, **a formal USPTO/TESS search plus live domain/app-store availability check on your two or three finalists** — my scan reads web pages, aggregators, and USPTO-report/Justia/Trademarkia records, which surface *live* application status inconsistently and cannot confirm the *absence* of a mark; a "no registration found" here is not legal clearance. Second, **a 5-minute reaction test with 8–10 of your own chapter members** (say it aloud, text it, first-impression meaning) — this is the fastest way to catch problems my desk research can only flag secondhand, such as whether "Sextant" draws jokes, whether "Sconce" reads as a real name, or whether "Signet" lands as premium or obscure with the actual Discord-native audience you need to adopt it.
