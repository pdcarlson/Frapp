# AI answer & entry-point UX patterns, applied to Signet's dark warm UI

**The dominant industry pattern is now settled and directly transferable to Signet: one consistent, sparkle-marked "Ask" entry point that lives in the app chrome (not a corner chatbot bubble), an AI answer rendered as a visually distinct card, and inline citation chips placed at the end of each claim that expand to a hover/click popover and deep-link into the underlying source.** Every leading product Signet should emulate — Perplexity, Notion AI, Slack AI, Intercom Fin, Glean — converges on this shape, differing mainly in whether sources are external (favicons) or internal (message/doc references). For Signet, whose sources are internal (chat, docs, meeting notes), the closest models are **Slack AI** (citations that jump to the original message) [slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/31739993134867-Search-with-AI-in-Slack) and **Notion AI Q&A** (cites only your workspace pages, refuses to fabricate) [notion](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/understanding-how-q-and-a-finds-answers-can-help-you-get-better-results) . My concrete recommendation for Signet: **one global "Ask" entry point rendered identically on all six surfaces** (not six per-surface entry points), a citation-chip treaty built on the Radix accent scale you already specced, and a **single gated "coming soon" stub** — the one entry point visible now, everything else (inline AI affordances, citation rendering) fully hidden until the backend ships.

---

## Part 1 — How the leading products actually build these UIs

### Answer presentation: a distinct card, not a plain message

Every product visually separates the AI answer from surrounding content. **Perplexity** reveals answers progressively in phases ("Searching → Reading → Writing"), with sources appearing *before* the answer text streams in [blakecrosley](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/perplexity) , and pairs the answer with a favicon stack and a visible source count ("**10 sources**") in the action bar [aiuxplayground](https://aiuxplayground.com/teardowns/perplexity/citations/) . **Notion AI Q&A** renders as a chat interface that synthesizes workspace content into a written answer plus navigable page links [notion](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/get-answers-about-content-faster-with-q-and-a) . **Slack AI** places answers *at the top of search results* rather than in the message feed [slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/31739993134867-Search-with-AI-in-Slack) . **Glean** renders answers in a chat UI with inline citation markers plus a "View sources" section [glean](https://docs.glean.com/user-guide/assistant/glean-chat/glean-chat-citations/glean-citations) . **Copilot Chat** returns text with interactive elements (code blocks, buttons, file trees) inside a dedicated chat panel [github](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/using-github-copilot/asking-github-copilot-questions-in-your-ide) . NN/g's guidance reinforces this: **style AI output and citations differently from the main UI** so users can tell what is generated [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/explainable-ai/) .

### Citations: inline chips at the claim, expanding to a source popover

The universal pattern is a **small clickable marker at the end of the specific claim it supports**, which reveals a preview on hover and navigates on click:

| Product | Inline citation form | Click behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Perplexity | Rounded **domain "pill" chips** with "+N" for multi-source claims [aiuxplayground](https://aiuxplayground.com/teardowns/perplexity/citations/) | Popover with favicon/domain/headline/excerpt + 1/N pagination; opens source in new tab; "10 sources" opens right-side panel [aiuxplayground](https://aiuxplayground.com/teardowns/perplexity/citations/) |
| Slack AI | Citations referencing source **messages/files** [slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/31739993134867-Search-with-AI-in-Slack) | Hover previews a snippet; click **jumps to the original message** [slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/28310650165907-Security-for-AI-features-in-Slack) |
| Glean | Inline markers + **"View sources"** section [glean](https://docs.glean.com/user-guide/assistant/glean-chat/glean-chat-citations/glean-citations) | Hover previews; click **opens the item in its native app** (permission-scoped); deep-links to the exact passage [glean](https://docs.glean.com/user-guide/assistant/glean-chat/glean-chat-citations/deep-linked-citations) |
| ChatGPT | Inline citations + a **"Sources" panel** with publisher logos [openai](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search) | Hover to learn more; click opens the source [openai](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search) |
| Intercom Fin | **Inline source links** in the chat message (degrade to "[1],[2]" on old SDKs) [intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/11433030-conversational-fin-experience) | Click behavior not documented; links to help-center content |
| Copilot Chat | **"Used n references" dropdown** at top + "References" link below [github](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/using-github-copilot/asking-github-copilot-questions-in-your-ide) | Expands the reference list; code-match references link into the editor [github](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/completions/code-referencing) |

Component-level guidance from design specialists: render citations as **semantic text elements** (visible number, not a background image), visually distinct from body text so they don't interrupt reading, with the popover **capping excerpts around 200 characters** [aydesign](https://www.aydesign.ai/blog/ai-citation-source-ui-patterns-2026) ; support **hover for preview and click for the full source** [shapeof](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/citations) ; and NN/g stresses citations must be **visible, prominent, and placed directly next to the claim** they support, never buried in a footer [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/explainable-ai/) . A reusable "InlineCitation" pill that shows source info on hover is now a standard component [ai-sdk](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/inline-citation) .

### Confidence and "I don't know": refuse rather than fabricate, then ask to clarify

The strongest products **decline to answer when retrieval is weak**. Perplexity won't generate an answer until retrieval succeeds and treats weak results as an explicit failure state [datastudios](https://www.datastudios.org/post/how-to-use-perplexity-ai-for-effective-research-with-real-time-sources-file-uploads-and-citation-t) . Notion Q&A is positioned as "not a generic chatbot — **if it can't find an answer, it won't make one up**" [notion](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/use-notion-ai-to-give-teams-perfect-memory-and-save-time) . Intercom Fin runs a **certainty parameter**: if output doesn't clear its confidence threshold, Fin generates a **disambiguation response that expresses uncertainty and asks for clarification** rather than hallucinating [intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/9929230-the-fin-ai-engine) . Glean shows **1–3 clarifying questions with clickable options and a "Skip" button** when a request is under-specified, and explicitly notes that *absence of citations is not evidence of a hallucination* [glean](https://docs.glean.com/user-guide/assistant/glean-chat/clarifying-questions) . Copilot's no-result state is a plain "no results please refine your input and try again" [github](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode-copilot-release/issues/6135) . NN/g's rule for genuinely low-confidence output: use **first-person uncertainty language ("I'm not completely sure, but…")** and encourage verification [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-hallucinations/) . Critically, all "empty" citation states should be **made explicit** — a missing/broken citation must show, not silently vanish, to preserve trust [shapeof](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/citations) .

### Entry-point placement and differentiation: in the chrome, sparkle-marked, labeled

The winners embed AI in the existing product chrome rather than a floating widget:
- **Notion AI**: a **sparkle icon bottom-right of every page**, plus "Search all sources with AI" under the search bar, plus inline `/AI` slash blocks and a highlight → "Ask AI" selection action [notion](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/everything-you-can-do-with-notion-ai) .
- **Slack AI**: an **AI icon in the upper-right of every conversation**, AI answers in the top search bar, and per-thread/channel "Summarize" buttons plus a sidebar "Recap" [slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/25076892548883-Guide-to-AI-features-in-Slack) .
- **Copilot Chat**: a chat icon in the editor title bar, **inline chat via Cmd/Ctrl+I directly in the editor**, and `/` slash commands [github](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/using-github-copilot/asking-github-copilot-questions-in-your-ide) .
- **Glean**: a message-bubble icon in the left nav, plus extension sidebar and a floating desktop quick-chat [glean](https://docs.glean.com/user-guide/assistant/glean-chat) .

On differentiation: Google's own research confirms **users reliably read a single sparkle as "AI"** [design.google](https://design.google/library/ai-sparkle-icon-research-pozos-schmidt) — but the same research warns the meaning is ambiguous, so NN/g insists AI affordances carry a **text label, not an icon alone** [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/prompt-controls-genai/) . NN/g's field study is a direct warning against the corner-chatbot approach: users routinely **never noticed chat icons that were small, unlabeled, or blended into the background**, and inconsistent placement across pages prevented a reliable mental model [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/site-ai-chatbot/) . Their positive guidance: **keep the AI accessible across all pages, consolidate it with existing chat/search, and never rely on a floating button as the only access path** [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-chatbots-design-guidelines/) . The recommended integrated pattern is an "inline assistant" living inside the object (field, block, card) with a small AI icon, explicitly to avoid the "corner chat bubble" feel  .

### Follow-ups and threading: persistent input + suggested-question buttons

Perplexity keeps an **"Ask follow-up" input beneath the answer** and offers **related-question chips** that run as follow-ups within a persistent "thread/session" holding the original question, all follow-ups, responses, and sources [perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10354769-what-is-a-thread) . Notion Q&A supports back-and-forth follow-ups "like conversing with a colleague" [notion](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/understanding-how-q-and-a-finds-answers-can-help-you-get-better-results) . ChatGPT shows optional **"quick follow-up" chips** under replies [reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1mwjgyb/setting_show_follow_up_suggestions_in_chats/) . Slack AI, notably, has **no persistent thread** — follow-up means re-querying the search bar [slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/31739993134867-Search-with-AI-in-Slack) . NN/g's rule: offer suggested prompts **as buttons, not text** [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-chatbots-design-guidelines/) .

### Staleness: essentially nobody ships a user-facing "this answer is out of date" indicator

This is the clearest gap in the landscape, and it should lower Signet's priority on the feature. Freshness is handled at the *data-sync layer*, not surfaced to end users: Glean indexes "in real time" (~15 min for Slack/Jira/Confluence) but shows no staleness label [glean](https://docs.glean.com/user-guide/about/end-user-quick-start-guide) ; Intercom shows a "last synced" timestamp only in the **admin** knowledge-source view, not in the Fin answer [intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/9440354-knowledge-sources-to-power-ai-agents-and-self-serve-support) ; ChatGPT's cached offline search warns it isn't "current at time of response" but adds no visual badge [openai](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001203-offline-web-search-for-chatgpt-workspaces) . The only user-facing staleness affordance found anywhere is **Perplexity's "Out of date" option in the thumbs-down feedback menu** — a reporting mechanism, not a proactive badge [aiuxplayground](https://aiuxplayground.com/teardowns/perplexity/citations/) . **Conclusion: do not build staleness badges for v1.** The mature pattern is to keep sources live/re-queried and let click-through to the (current) source be the freshness guarantee.

## Part 2 — Applied recommendation for Signet

### Entry point: one global "Ask" affordance, rendered identically on all six surfaces

**Recommendation: a single global entry point, not per-surface entry points.** The evidence favors this on three grounds. First, NN/g's field data shows scattered, inconsistent AI placement destroys the user's "where do I look" mental model [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/site-ai-chatbot/) , while a consolidated, always-accessible entry point is the explicit guideline [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-chatbots-design-guidelines/) . Second, for a solo founder shipping AI as a fast-follow, one entry point is one component to build, gate, and later wire to a backend — versus six. Third, your tagline "Ask your chapter *anything*" is inherently cross-surface (answers span chat + docs + meeting notes), so scoping the entry point to a single surface would contradict the product promise.

Concrete pattern:
- **Placement**: a persistent **"Ask" pill in the top app bar, trailing position, on every one of the six surfaces** (chat, events, points/tasks, study hours, dues, backwork). On web, additionally bind **Cmd/Ctrl+K** to open the same surface. This mirrors Notion's global sparkle [notion](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/everything-you-can-do-with-notion-ai) and Slack's persistent AI icon [slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/25076892548883-Guide-to-AI-features-in-Slack) .
- **Affordance spec**: pill, height 36–40px, `radius: 20px` (fully rounded), background `--accent-3`, border `1px solid --accent-6`, **sparkle icon in `--accent-11` + the text label "Ask"** in `--accent-11` at 15–16px. Never icon-only — Google/NN/g both require the label [design.google](https://design.google/library/ai-sparkle-icon-research-pozos-schmidt) [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/prompt-controls-genai/) . This is the *one* place the chapter accent appears in the chrome as a filled affordance, which makes it read as "the AI thing" without a bolted-on sidebar.
- **Behavior**: opens a **full-height bottom sheet (mobile) / centered command panel (web)** — the "Ask sheet." Because it's launched from a specific surface, seed a **scope chip** at the top of the sheet: e.g., opened from Backwork → chip reads "Across all chapter records" with a suggested narrower "Backwork files only" toggle. This borrows Notion's source-picker ("All sources" dropdown) [notion](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/everything-you-can-do-with-notion-ai) and Perplexity's scoping without requiring six separate UIs.
- **Do not** add per-surface inline AI buttons (no "summarize this thread," no `/ai` blocks) in v1. They multiply the surface area, need their own backend wiring, and their absence is unnoticeable — reserve them for a later phase.

### Citation / source-chip visual treaty (dark warm base + single accent)

Because Signet's sources are **internal** (a chat message, a backwork file, a meeting note) rather than web pages with favicons, the chip should encode **source type**, and clicking should **deep-link inside the app** (jump to the message / open the file / open the note) — the Slack [slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/28310650165907-Security-for-AI-features-in-Slack) and Glean [glean](https://docs.glean.com/user-guide/assistant/glean-chat/glean-chat-citations/glean-citations) model, not the Perplexity favicon model.

Component spec (all tokens from your existing Radix/shadcn scale):

| Component | Spec |
|---|---|
| **Inline citation chip** | Pill, `padding: 2px 8px`, `radius: 8px`, `background: --accent-3`, `text: --accent-11`, 13px. Content = a small **source-type glyph** (chat bubble / document / meeting) + a number. Placed at the end of the sentence it supports (NN/g) [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/explainable-ai/) . Multi-source → single chip with "+N". |
| **On hover (web) / tap (mobile)** | Opens a **popover** on `--popover` (#232326), `radius: 12px`, `border: 1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.08)`. Contents: source-type icon, source title (e.g. author + channel, or filename), **excerpt capped ~200 chars with ellipsis** [aydesign](https://www.aydesign.ai/blog/ai-citation-source-ui-patterns-2026) , timestamp, and a primary **"Open" button** (`--primary` = `--accent-9`, foreground `--accent-contrast`). |
| **On click "Open"** | Deep-links into the app: chat citation → scrolls to and highlights the message; backwork citation → opens the file; meeting-note citation → opens the note at the passage. Permission-scoped: never cite a source the member's role can't open (Glean's rule) [glean](https://docs.glean.com/security/security-principles) . |
| **Sources footer** | Below the answer, a `--muted-foreground` row: "Sources (N)" with stacked source-type icons; tap expands a vertical list of the same popover cards. Mirrors Perplexity's "10 sources" and Glean's "View sources" [aiuxplayground](https://aiuxplayground.com/teardowns/perplexity/citations/) [glean](https://docs.glean.com/user-guide/assistant/glean-chat/glean-chat-citations/glean-citations) . |
| **Empty/broken source state** | If a cited message was deleted or the member lost access, render the chip as **disabled/struck with a tooltip "Source no longer available"** — never silently drop it [shapeof](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/citations) . |

### AI answer card and confidence states

- **Answer card**: distinct surface — `background: --accent-2` (barely-tinted), `border-left: 3px solid --accent-6` or a header row, `radius: 20px` (your spec's max, reserved for chat bubbles and AI cards). Header: sparkle in `--accent-11` + "Signet" label + the scope chip. Body text 16px `--foreground` (your readability floor). This makes the answer unmistakably not a normal chat bubble.
- **"I don't know" state (mandatory)**: adopt Notion's refuse-don't-fabricate posture [notion](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/use-notion-ai-to-give-teams-perfect-memory-and-save-time) . When retrieval returns nothing, render an explicit empty state: *"I couldn't find anything in your chapter's records about that."* — with 2–3 suggested rephrasings as **buttons** [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-chatbots-design-guidelines/) . For under-specified questions, borrow Fin/Glean **clarifying-question chips with a "Skip"** [glean](https://docs.glean.com/user-guide/assistant/glean-chat/clarifying-questions) [intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/9929230-the-fin-ai-engine) . Place the "verify against sources" disclaimer near the input, not in a footer (NN/g) [nngroup](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/explainable-ai/) .
- **Follow-ups**: persistent "Ask a follow-up" input pinned to the bottom of the Ask sheet + up to 3 suggested-question buttons; keep the thread within the sheet (Perplexity's session model) [perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10354769-what-is-a-thread) .
- **Staleness**: skip proactive badges (industry norm). Re-query on each open so citations point to current records; optionally show the answer's generation timestamp in `--muted-foreground`.

### The "stub" state: what ships visible-but-gated vs. fully hidden now

The single-entry-point recommendation makes the stub cheap and clean. **Show exactly one gated affordance; hide everything else.**

**SHOW now (gated):** the global **"Ask" pill** on all six surfaces, with a small `--accent`-tinted **"Soon" badge**. Tapping it must **not** be a dead disabled button — the anti-pattern that frustrates users [smashing](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2021/08/frustrating-design-patterns-disabled-buttons/) . Instead it opens the **real Ask sheet shell** in a "coming soon" state: the sheet header, scope chip, and a teaser body — *"Ask your chapter anything. Search across your chat, files, and meeting notes — arriving soon."* — plus an optional **"Notify me when it's ready"** toggle. This follows the established coming-soon guidance: label the feature clearly, change the CTA to "Notify me," and lead with what users *will* unlock rather than what they can't do ^[ux.stackexchange](https://ux.stackexchange.com/questions/144834/how-to-display-features-that-are-coming-soon "usability - How to display features that are \"coming soon\" - User ...") [pixxen](https://pixxen.com/blog/saas-empty-state-design/) . This teaches the entry point's location and the product promise with zero misleading behavior.

**HIDE completely now:** every other AI affordance — inline `/ai` blocks, per-thread "summarize" buttons, citation chips, source popovers, the answer card. SAP Fiori's rule applies directly: **if a control can't be enabled yet and has no in-context enablement path, hide it rather than disable it** [sap](https://www.sap.com/design-system/fiori-design-web/v1-96/foundations/best-practices/ui-elements/ui-element-states) [jakobnielsen](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/inactive-buttons) . These components have no meaning without a live answer, and rendering them empty would confuse.

**Rework avoidance:** build the **Ask sheet as a real, permanent component now** — top bar pill, sheet shell, scope chip, tokens, animations — and slot the "coming soon" teaser into its body. When the RAG backend ships, you **replace only the sheet body** with the live answer stream + citation chips (built during the fast-follow), leaving the entry point, sheet chrome, and design tokens untouched. Because there is only one entry point and one sheet, the migration from stub → live is a single-component swap, not a six-surface refactor.

## Where more research would most strengthen this

Two areas. **First, empirical citation-chip legibility across real chapter accent colors**: the chip treaty assumes `--accent-3`/`--accent-11` pairs read cleanly, but many fraternity colors are dark navy/maroon — running 20–30 real chapter hexes through `generateRadixColors` and eyeballing chip contrast on `#0a0a0a` would confirm the chip is legible for every tenant before locking the spec. **Second, the internal-source deep-link interaction** (chat citation → scroll-and-highlight the original message) has no direct precedent documented at component level — Slack and Glean confirm the *behavior* exists but not the exact scroll/highlight/animation treatment, so this is the one piece you'll need to design from first principles rather than copy.
