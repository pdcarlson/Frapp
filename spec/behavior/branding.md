# Chapter Branding

Behavior and boundaries for per-chapter branding. Visual design tokens (palette, typography, radii) are owned by the UI spec; this file covers what branding *means* and what the API enforces. Editing of branding happens in Chapter settings → Chapter on web (the Accent section sits under the chapter mark) — see [`settings/customization.md`](settings/customization.md) — and, for the accent alone, in You → Chapter settings → Chapter accent on mobile (§ Accent Color below).

## Logo

- Chapters can upload a logo image. Types, extensions, and size follow the shared `image` kind in `@repo/validation` (`packages/validation/src/upload-allowlists.ts`: `isAllowedUploadMime`, `isAllowedUploadExtension`, `MAX_UPLOAD_BYTES`) — the same allowlist and cap as avatars and every other image-upload surface. This spec does not copy those lists; the kind is the source of truth. The private `branding` bucket enforces the same MIME list and `file_size_limit` on the PUT itself.
- The logo is displayed as the chapter mark (§ Chapter mark below): the web nav's chapter tile and, on mobile, Chat home's title row, beside the chapter name (`ChapterMark`, drawn by `(app)/(tabs)/index.tsx` through `ScreenShell`; it rode in the tab navigator's header until no screen had one, [#2485](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2485)) — there is no Home screen; chat is home. It is also embedded in exported PDF reports, and mobile chapter setup previews it once the founder adds one (#3351). Those are its only surfaces. The member directory and the onboarding tutorial welcome screen were once listed too and never built; the owner dropped them on 2026-09-29 ([#2891](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2891)), because the nav tile already carries the mark on every screen and the rebuilt directory is dense rows with no place for one. PDF export is a renderer limit, not an upload restriction: the PDF library can embed only a subset of the `image` kind, and an unreadable logo is skipped with a warning rather than failing the export — see [`reports.md`](reports.md) § PDF Formatting.
- Logo is stored in Supabase Storage under `chapters/{chapter_id}/branding/logo-{uuid}.{ext}`, a fresh key per upload (#2592). The key used to be `logo.{ext}`, fixed per extension, and a signed upload URL can't overwrite an existing object without an upsert chosen at mint time, so replacing a PNG with another PNG failed at the mint. Upserting onto a fixed key would have swapped the displayed bytes at PUT time, before any confirm and so before any audit row; a unique key keeps confirm the only step that changes the logo. Logos stored under the old key keep working until they are replaced.
- **Confirm proves the upload and cleans up after it.** `POST /v1/chapters/current/logo` refuses a `storage_path` with no object in the chapter's branding folder (a 400). After the column update lands, it re-reads which logo is current and deletes the logo it replaced, plus any upload that was minted and PUT but never confirmed and is more than a day old. It never deletes the current logo or a recent upload: nothing serializes two confirms, and a recent upload may be another officer's replacement still on its way to its own confirm. So when two officers replace the logo at once, whichever confirm lands last leaves the chapter on an object that exists, and the other upload stays until a later confirm finds it past the grace period. Deleting before the update would leave the chapter pointing at a deleted logo whenever the update failed. A failed delete, or a failed re-read, is logged and doesn't fail the confirm; with unique keys a stranded object costs storage but blocks nothing. One narrow case is left: a confirm that re-sends the exact path another confirm is replacing, in the same instant, can have that object deleted under it.
- The `branding` bucket is **private**, so `logo_path` is not addressable by a client on its own. `GET /v1/chapters/current` returns a signed **`logo_url`** alongside it; that is the only supported way for a client to render the logo. When signing fails the field is `null` — the logo is decoration, and an unreachable asset must not fail the chapter read.
- Without a logo, what stands in for it is the chapter mark's text (§ Chapter mark below).

## Chapter mark

The chapter mark is what stands for a chapter wherever the product shows its identity in a small space: the web nav's 28px chapter tile, and Chat home's title row on mobile, beside the chapter name. Mobile chapter setup also previews it while the officer creates the chapter (#3351), and mobile's join confirm card shows the text mark of the chapter an invite is for, before the member joins (#3352; the API resolves it with `chapterTextMark`, so letters a chapter or its organization hides never leave the server, and the card shows no logo). Web `/join`'s cards for the invites emailed to the member's address (#3776) show the same server-resolved text mark in the 28px tile, also without a logo. The welcome message onboarding posts to `#general` is not a mark surface and never shows the letters; [`onboarding.md`](onboarding.md) owns its wording (#3347; corrected 2026-11-04, it used the mark from #2876 until then). In precedence order:

1. **The uploaded logo.**
2. **The short name** (`branding.short_name`), a few characters the chapter chooses: "FIJI", say. Capped at `CHAPTER_SHORT_NAME_MAX_LENGTH` in `@repo/validation`, which the tile's width sets. The API's DTO enforces the cap, and the inputs that write a short name carry it as `maxLength`. No zod schema restates it, because class-validator and zod count some emoji differently. A client reads `branding` as served and skips a value of the wrong type rather than refusing the payload: a read that refused one stored value would blank the nav for every member (#2844).
3. **The Greek letters** (`branding.greek_letters`), **unless the chapter turned them off** (`branding.show_greek_letters: false`).
4. **Initials of the chapter name**, only on a surface that always fills its slot (the web tile, mobile setup's preview, the join confirm card, and web `/join`'s invite cards). Mobile's title row shows nothing instead, since the name is right beside it.

**Why letters can be turned off.** Some organizations don't display their Greek letters at all. By custom, chapters of Phi Gamma Delta (FIJI) don't, and the beta chapter, Tau Nu, is one. The onboarding autofill fills the letters from `chapter_directory` for every chapter it knows, so without the switch that chapter's nav showed "ΦΓΔ" (reported by the owner, 2026-09-29). The positioning promise to serve "the full Greek spectrum" ([`../product/positioning.md`](../product/positioning.md)) means a chapter chooses what represents it.

**The switch hides; it doesn't delete.** The stored letters stay (Settings still edits them), and turning the switch back on shows them again. Absent means shown, so every chapter created before the setting keeps its letters. Only an explicit `false` hides them.

**One resolver.** `resolveChapterMark` and `chapterTextMark` in `@repo/validation` (`chapter-mark.ts`) own this order, and every surface reads the mark through them. They don't know which organizations never display their letters: the surfaces that show a live chapter's mark set the switch off by name before calling them (listed under *Where it's set* below), so a new such surface has to do the same until one shared helper owns it ([#3459](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3459)). `chapter-mark-renderers.spec.ts` beside them guards it two ways: a file may touch `greek_letters` only if its `ALLOWED` map names it with the reason, and no allowed file may print the field as a JSX child expression, a template interpolation or a string concatenation. A new file that shows the letters fails the first check. The second is a pattern match, so a print it doesn't recognize (through a prop, or an expression wrapped across lines) still needs catching in review of those files.

**Where it's set.** Settings → Chapter → Chapter mark ([`settings/README.md`](settings/README.md#chapter-tab-taborg)) uploads the logo and sets the short name and the switch. Both first-officer wizards take the short name and the switch beside the autofilled letters (web's identity step, mobile's confirm step under `Edit details`). For an organization that doesn't display its letters (`orgHidesGreekLetters` in `@repo/org-archetypes`, Phi Gamma Delta), no directory result, wizard or Settings screen draws the letters or the switch. Both wizards keep them hidden for a chapter picked from that organization's directory row even after the founder edits the name. A typed name that names such an organization, alone or with more ("Phi Gamma Delta Tau Nu", "FIJI at RPI"), turns the switch off and fills an empty short name; moving the name to another organization undoes both, and a short name the founder typed is never replaced (`orgMarkFieldsFor`). The switch is set off and stays off on the next mark save, which also corrects a chapter created before the profile turned them off; the stored letters stay stored. Until that save, the surfaces that show a live chapter's mark hide them anyway by the chapter's name, matched the same way (`orgHidingGreekLettersIn`, 2026-11-05): web's nav crest and Settings, mobile's Chat home title row (`useChapterBranding`), and the join confirm card and web `/join`'s invite cards, whose `mark_text` the API resolves on the server (`InviteService.preview` and `GET /v1/invites/mine`, through the shared `InviteService.describe`). Web's wizard still keeps the last organization's short name and switch when the founder picks a different directory row ([#3466](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3466)). Both also take a logo once the new chapter is active, because the logo routes are chapter-scoped: web uploads it after create, and mobile's live step offers it as an optional, skippable picker (#3351). Mobile's setup previews the text mark on a tint of the chosen seed with the foreground label, because the palette that yields the shell's step-11 fill is generated on the server at create.

**Contrast.** A text mark on web is `accent-text` on `accent-subtle`, a pair the accent engine holds to AA for every seed ([`accent-engine.md` §8](../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md#8-validation)). On mobile it is the accent chip pairing: the step-11 accent fill under the fixed `gold.onHouse` label. Both surfaces are dark-only. A logo is drawn as uploaded on a neutral tile, never recoloured, and the Settings card previews the nav's own tile so an officer sees how it reads before it ships.

## Accent Color

> This section describes the stored accent. Signet derives a 12-step scale from it as its seed — see
> [`../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md`](../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md) — and that scale is
> what the web dashboard shell paints: it applies no token from the per-surface re-validation below.
> Which call sites still re-validate (`resolveChapterAccentColor`) is owned by
> [`accent-engine.md` § 6](../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md#6-implementation-status) and is not
> restated here. `apps/landing` is not a consumer: it resolves no chapter and reads no accent.

- Chapters can set a custom accent color (hex string, e.g. `#8B0000` for crimson). It is stored on the `chapters` table in two places, and **`branding.colors.accent` is authoritative** (#795). The `accent_color` column is a mirror the API maintains on every write path — onboarding, the config PATCH, and the direct column update from Settings, which also writes the value back into `branding.colors.accent` on every hex save. An explicit `accent_color: null` is the gap: it takes the early-return branch in `ChapterService.update` and nulls the column without touching the jsonb. Before this, the onboarding wizard wrote only the jsonb, so the column kept its default and every surface reading it showed Royal Blue for a chapter that had chosen otherwise.
- The accent color is applied to: the places [foundations.md](../ui/design-system/foundations.md) §6 "Where the accent goes" lists: primary buttons, controls a member has switched on, focus, links, the chapter's own mark, and where the member is (active tab and nav indicators) — for that chapter's members only. Since 2026-11-05 names (the viewer's own included, which was the chat self-bubble until 2026-09-29, #2873, then the accent text), the mention picker's highlighted suggestion, hovers and labels are neutral. Mentions themselves are not accent-derived: the in-message `@Name` chip is the fixed `--mention-chip` pair ([`components.md`](../ui/design-system/components.md) § In-body mention chip) and the unread-mention badge the fixed `--mention` token, the same for every chapter. On mobile that means everything interactive or highlighted inside the `(tabs)` group: the active tab tint, primary buttons, the chat send button, focus outlines, back arrows, inline actions (Retry, Unblock, mark all read), the viewer's own reactions and selected options. A finished task's checkbox is neutral, like every done state. The owner settled this on 2026-11-04, after the beta chapter's phone showed its cream accent and house gold side by side on one screen: house gold stays only where there is no chapter (the `(auth)` group, § Brand Boundaries), on Ask, the product's own assistant, whose gold is fixed on web too, and on the Chat home bell's count badge, which matches the web bell's so that a red accent never draws an inbox count beside the mention-red channel badges (`apps/mobile/components/chat/notifications-bell.tsx`, #3369). Mobile paints these through `useChapterBranding` (`apps/mobile/lib/chapter-branding.ts`), never the `gold.*` color tokens. Apart from those exceptions, the one `gold.*` token left inside a chapter is `gold.onHouse`, the dark label ink drawn on a step-11 fill (a switched-on control's check or thumb), because it is ink, not a color the chapter chose.
- **Picking the accent.** Both mobile surfaces that set it, the create-chapter wizard and Chapter settings → Chapter accent, use one swatch picker (`apps/mobile/components/chapter/accent-picker.tsx`): the organization's official colors first, then a fixed row of common colors (`ACCENT_PRESETS` in `@repo/hooks/chapter-identity`), with a typed hex behind "Custom color". The owner asked for this because "nobody wants to type a hex". An organization's colors live in `ORG_PROFILES` (`packages/org-archetypes/src/org-profiles.ts`), keyed by organization name and never by Greek letters, because some organizations don't display theirs (§ Chapter mark). Settings looks them up by the chapter's name, which is the organization's name for a chapter created from the directory, so a chapter renamed since, or created by hand under another name, sees only the common colors ([#3372](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3372)). A color that is none of the swatches on screen, such as the one the chapter held when the picker opened, shows as its own selected swatch, so the picker never holds a color it doesn't show. A swatch paints the seed itself, not the shade the engine derives from it, because it shows the color being chosen; a member sees the derived accent once the save lands. Chapter settings → Chapter accent saves through `PATCH /v1/chapters/current`, so only a member holding `CHAPTER_PROFILE_PERMISSIONS` can open it. Web's wizard and the Chapter settings Accent section draw the same two groups as buttons (`apps/web/components/settings/accent-swatches.tsx`, added 2026-11-05), and keep the browser's color input and the hex field beside them for a color that is neither.
- **A directory match starts on the organization's color.** Both wizards prefill the accent with the organization's first official color (`orgDefaultAccent`) and fall back to `chapter_directory.default_colors.accent` only for an organization `ORG_PROFILES` doesn't hold. The same profile switches the Greek letters off for an organization that doesn't display its letters (FIJI), since the directory row carries the letters either way, and fills in its short name when the field is empty (`orgMarkFieldsFor`, on both). The directory's rows carry one placeholder gold, `#C9A56F` (45 of the first seed's 50 rows; every row since the Wikipedia seed of 2026-11-06, whose lists carry no colors), which is how the beta chapter was created cream rather than FIJI purple (2026-11-04). `ORG_PROFILES` holds the organizations of the first seed; the ones the Wikipedia seed added start on the placeholder until it holds them too.
- Two different "defaults" are in play, and they are not interchangeable:
  - **Stored default** — the `chapters.accent_color` column defaults to Frapp's Royal Blue `#2563EB`, so that is what a chapter holds until an accent is chosen. Chapters onboarded before #795 kept it regardless of what they picked; `20260814120000_backfill_chapter_accent_color_from_branding.sql` repairs those rows.
  - **Render fallback** — what a client paints when the stored accent is absent, malformed, or illegible on the surface at hand. Each caller passes its own surface and its own fallback accent — the values are the caller's theme's, and are not restated here. The call sites that still re-validate ([`accent-engine.md` § 6](../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md#6-implementation-status)) pass Signet house gold; `resolveChapterAccentColor` has no default of its own (the legacy bronze one went in #3227). [`../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md`](../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md) owns the resolution order.
- **The stored accent is format-validated, not contrast-gated.** Both `branding.colors.accent` and the `accent_color` mirror must be `#RRGGBB`; neither is rejected for failing 4.5:1 on save. The accent is the Signet engine's *seed* ([`../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md`](../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md) §1) — the engine derives a scale, guarantees AA for its **text** roles, and holds its solid `accent-primary` fill and that fill's hover shade to 3:1 against every surface they paint on ([§8](../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md#8-validation) owns both and how). The fill floor holds for palettes written since #2541, and the hover floor for those written since #2586; [`accent-engine.md` § 4](../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md#4-caching-and-persistence) covers stored ones. So a dark seed saves as entered (and so does a vivid mid-tone whose hover would not stand out), and the engine lightens the fill it paints rather than rejecting the colour. Which colour the fill ends up as is owned by [`accent-engine.md` § 2](../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md#2-role-map).
  - A save-time gate also does not survive contact with the data: 49 of the 50 chapters in the first `supabase/seed/chapter_directory.csv` had an accent below 4.5:1 on the light surface, `#C9A56F` alone accounting for 45 of them at 2.16:1, because fraternity colors are so often light golds, silvers, and whites.
  - It cannot apply to only one of the two stores either. They are one logical value written through three paths, so gating the column while the seed stays open let a chapter be created holding an accent it could never re-save — the Settings form resends the stored value and got a 400 telling the officer to choose a darker color they had never chosen. This supersedes #600, which assumed the two stores were different kinds of thing.
  - Contrast is checked where it is observable instead: at generation for the engine's roles (above), and per surface at the call sites that still paint the stored accent directly (below). An accent can pass on one surface and fail on another; crimson (`#8B0000`) is the worked example: 10.0:1 on white, 1.7:1 on the native dark card.
- The call sites that paint the stored accent directly ([`accent-engine.md` § 6](../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md#6-implementation-status) owns which) **re-validate it per surface** rather than trusting it. `resolveChapterAccentColor` (`apps/web/components/settings/resolve-chapter-accent.ts`) takes the background and the mode's own fallback accent, and substitutes the fallback when the chapter's accent fails. In the Settings editor that substitution is preview-only: a failing color surfaces an inline warning rather than hard-failing the edit, and the save stores the color entered, not the substitute (#2543). It is stored as `#RRGGBB`, the same parse the preview uses, so a 3-digit shorthand or a padded hex is stored as the color it previewed (`#08e` as `#0088EE`); a draft that isn't a hex color, an empty one included, disables Save and says what to enter.
- Note this bites the stored default too: `#2563EB` is 5.2:1 on white but 3.2:1 on the dark card, so at a call site that re-validates, an uncustomized chapter renders the fallback token in dark mode rather than Royal Blue. That is the intended outcome — legibility wins over exactness.

## Brand Boundaries

- Chapter branding applies **only within the chapter context** — when a user is viewing that chapter's data.
- The **Frapp brand** — the product mark and wordmark, the splash screen, the landing site, the docs site — is **not** affected by chapter branding. The dashboard *shell* is a separate question and is not exempt: `useChapterTheme` is mounted once by `DashboardShell` "so branding applies shell-wide", writing the engine's accent roles onto `:root` semantic tokens. It is the mark that must never take the chapter accent, not the chrome around it.
- On mobile the boundary is the route group: the `(app)` group (the tabs and every screen pushed over them) is chapter context and carries chapter branding; the `(auth)` group sits outside it and stays Frapp-branded. The reason is the absence of a resolved chapter rather than the absence of a session: `(auth)` also holds `join`, `terms` and `welcome`, which `lib/auth-gate.ts` routes *authenticated* members to (the Terms prompt even when a chapter would resolve, since it comes before anything a member can reach), plus `chapter-picker` and `create-chapter`, which they reach deliberately rather than by the gate.
- Switching the active chapter re-resolves branding in place. Chapter identity comes from the `active_chapter_id` token claim (see [`multi-tenancy.md`](multi-tenancy.md)), so no restart or cache reset is involved.
