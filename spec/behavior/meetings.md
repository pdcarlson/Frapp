# Meetings

Frapp turns a chapter meeting into minutes and suggested tasks, as a paid-tier feature. The minutes come from notes one or more members took, from an uploaded recording, or from a recording made in the app, and one draft reads all of them ([Several note-takers](#several-note-takers)). Posted minutes and transcripts land in the [AI corpus](ai.md), giving the chapter searchable institutional memory of what was discussed and decided.

The order the three ways are built in, and why, is [ADR-28](../architecture/adr/adr-28.md) decision 6.

## Meeting type metadata

When an officer starts minutes, by any of the three ways under [Workflow](#workflow), they select a **meeting type**. Standards is in the table because it is a kind of meeting a chapter holds, and it is the one type none of the three ways offers ([Standards meetings](#standards-meetings)).

| Type        | Example                                          |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Chapter     | Full chapter meeting (most common).              |
| Exec        | Officer / exec board meeting.                    |
| Committee   | Committee or working-group meeting.              |
| Standards   | Standards board / member-conduct hearing.        |
| Other       | Anything else (free-form label optional).        |

The type is captured as metadata on the meeting and persists through transcription. It is **not** a separate template; rather, the AI summarizer uses it to vary tone and emphasis:

- **Chapter**: balanced minutes — attendance, announcements, decisions, action items.
- **Exec**: decisions and action items emphasized; lower-priority on the full discussion log.
- **Committee**: focus on action items and follow-ups; lighter on attendance.
- **Standards**: never drafted. Its minutes are written by hand, and nothing about it is recorded or sent to a model. See [Standards meetings](#standards-meetings).
- **Other**: balanced minutes (defaults to Chapter behavior).

## Template

All meeting summaries use a **single global template** with toggleable sections per chapter. Available sections:

- Summary / Overview (default ON)
- Announcements (default ON)
- Decisions (default ON)
- Action items with owner + due date (default ON)
- Notes (always on: no switch turns them off)
  - Changed 2026-11-17 by pdcarlson (#5410): this was "Discussion log (default OFF — verbose)". Paul, after a 7-minute meeting came out as one summary sentence: "I'd rather have just a blank wall of text ... than this high-level summary." The notes are the body of the minutes, so every chapter's minutes carry them.
- Attendees (default ON)
- Officer reports (default OFF)
- Old business (default OFF)
- New business (default OFF)

> Changed 2026-11-17 by pdcarlson (#5381): what was settled and who owes what come right after the summary, then the discussion, then the record. The list began with Attendees and put Decisions after Announcements.

> Changed 2026-11-18 by pdcarlson: Announcements moved up to sit under the summary, so the order reads summary, announcements, decisions, action items, then the notes. Paul, on the posted minutes: "Maybe it should be summary, announcements, decisions, and then action items."

Chapters toggle sections from chapter settings ([`settings/README.md`](settings/README.md)), on the Meetings page's Rules. The template is the same across all meeting types — type only affects AI summarization behavior. The sections always show in the order above. A section switched off keeps what minutes already wrote in it, and those minutes still show it.

> **Added 2026-11-12 (#4312).** Announcements joined the list: the Chapter type's minutes above name announcements, and there was no section to hold them. The list and its defaults are `MEETING_SECTIONS` in `@repo/validation`.

Per-meeting-type template variants are deferred to v2+ pending real usage signal that chapters want them.

## Workflow

There are three ways to start, and they end in the same review screen.

| Way in | What the officer does | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notes | Types, pastes or dictates rough notes after the meeting. | Nothing new, which is why it is built first. |
| Uploaded recording | Uploads an audio file on web, for example a phone's voice memo. Built 2026-11-12: [Uploading a recording](#uploading-a-recording). | A transcription provider. |
| In-app recorder | Records on the phone during the meeting. Built 2026-11-12: [Recording in the app](#recording-in-the-app). | A store build at native version 0.12.0 or later, the first with microphone access, and a transcription provider. |

1. An officer with the meeting permission starts minutes, selects the meeting type, and links the meeting's event. The event's check-ins fill the Attendees section without any AI. A meeting with no event gets one when minutes are started, with the audience the officer picks, because the event is what says who the minutes are for ([Who can read minutes](#who-can-read-minutes)).
2. For a recording, the audio is transcribed on the server by a transcription provider behind Frapp's own interface. The vendor is chosen by a bake-off on a real meeting ([#712](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/712)). Transcription is its own step: a general model is not asked to listen.
3. The summarizer drafts the templated minutes from the notes or the transcript, using the meeting type as context. It is given the roster's names, who holds each role, and who checked in, so "the treasurer will send the invoice" resolves to a person.
4. The officer reviews and edits the draft before posting. With a recording, each drafted line links to its moment in the audio, so checking a line takes seconds.
5. Once the minutes are posted, the same screen lists suggested tasks, under the rules in [Suggested tasks](#suggested-tasks).
6. On post, the minutes are saved to the meeting and its audience is notified. The officer can also drop a link to them in a channel. The link opens only for the audience, and the minutes' text is never copied into a chat message: a chat message is read by everyone in its channel, and is a chat source for AI under the channel's rules, not the meeting's.
7. Posted minutes, and the transcript when there is one, are indexed for AI Q&A (see [`ai.md`](ai.md)). An answer draws on them only for a member in the meeting's audience; a `chapter_id` match alone is not enough.

> **Changed 2026-11-11 (Paul, [ADR-28](../architecture/adr/adr-28.md) decisions 6 and 7).** This section used to describe one path: record, transcribe "via a Whisper-equivalent service", summarize. It also posted Standards summaries to the vault and indexed every transcript, which contradicted the vault's exclusion from AI ([#3295](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3295)). Notes come first because they need no recording and no new app build. Step 6 used to post Chapter, Exec and Committee minutes into one `#meetings` channel, which made an Exec meeting's minutes readable by every member who could open it, and through AI by anyone. They now belong to the meeting and its audience ([Who can read minutes](#who-can-read-minutes)). That audience rule is the drafting session's answer to #3295's access question, built on the event rule that already existed, and is Paul's to change.

## Who can read minutes

A meeting's **audience** is the members its event invites, by the event's own rule ([`events.md` § Event Creation](events.md#event-creation), "Attendance by role"): a member whose answer is "not invited" can't find the event, and can't read its minutes or its transcript. A member who holds `events:update` sees every event, and so every meeting's minutes.

- Minutes and a transcript are readable by the audience, in the app and through AI alike. There is no second, wider rule for AI.
- An Exec meeting is an event that invites the exec roles. Its minutes are then exec-only without any setting on the minutes themselves.
- Changing who the event invites changes who can read the minutes, from that moment.
- **A draft is its author's.** Until minutes are posted, only the officer who started them and holders of `meetings:edit` can read them. Posting is what shows them to the audience. The author is held to the event's audience like anyone else: an author the event no longer shows itself to can't read, change, post or delete the minutes until it does.
- **Minutes outlive their event.** Deleting an event keeps its minutes, which then have no audience: only holders of `meetings:edit` can read them, and they still say the event's name and date. Removing them takes `meetings:delete`.
- **Minutes are always linked to an event.** Every way of starting them links one (the Meetings page's New minutes, the event sheet, the phone), and the server refuses minutes with neither an event nor a new one to make. Minutes whose event was deleted show **Link an event** to the `meetings:edit` holders who can read them: it offers the recent events that have no minutes, or making one after the fact by the rule under [Writing minutes by hand](#writing-minutes-by-hand), and linking one gives the minutes that event's audience. Added 2026-11-13 (#4935, Paul: "Minutes should be linked to an event by default. If meeting notes are started and not linked to an event, we should ask the user to add a linked event or backlog one to link it to.")

### Finding minutes on the phone

Asked by Paul, 2026-11-17 (#5290): "it should probably have better grouping, you know, by like type of event ... if it's a recurring event ... group it by recurring event ... maybe some more tags could be cool." The phone's Minutes list groups and tags minutes from what they already hold, so nobody files or tags anything ([`screens.md` § Minutes](../ui/mobile/screens.md#minutes) owns the layout). `GET /v1/meetings` gives each row its event's series (the series' first event's id, as the Events list keys a series) and repeat rule when the reader can see that event, how many decisions and action items the minutes hold, and whether a recording was used (a transcript on posted minutes, a recording that hasn't failed on a draft). A repeating event with two or more minutes folds into one Series row that opens its own list. Tags an officer types (Budget, Formal) are a separate idea (#5291). The web's Meetings page doesn't group yet (#5481).

### Where minutes live

Settled 2026-11-12 with the meetings schema (#4312, [#3290](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3290)'s storage question). Minutes are their own record (`meetings`), at most one per event, not the event's notes field. The notes field stays the event's own notes ([`events.md` § Event Creation](events.md#event-creation)): while the meetings module is on, the event sheet labels it "Notes" and shows the event's minutes beside it. Starting minutes copies the event's notes into the Summary once, so nothing typed there is typed twice; after that the two are separate. A chapter with the module off keeps using the notes field as before.

## Writing minutes by hand

The Notes way in, without the AI draft: an officer writes the minutes themselves. The AI draft ([ADR-28](../architecture/adr/adr-28.md) decision 6) fills the same sections later, and the officer reviews it on the same page.

- **Start from the event.** The event sheet's Minutes row offers Write minutes to a holder of `meetings:record`, which starts them in one tap with the type the event's name suggests ("Exec meeting" opens on Exec), and Open minutes to anyone who can read them. The Meetings page's New minutes asks one thing at a time ([#5219](https://github.com/frapplive/Frapp/issues/5219), Paul 2026-11-16): it lists the events of the last 120 days, newest first, five at a time behind "Show older meetings", with the one on now (or else the newest one that has started and has no minutes) already picked, so a calendar meeting is one press of Start minutes. The type is guessed from the event's name and stated in one line ("Filed as Chapter. Change"); the Standards note shows only when changing it.
- **A meeting that isn't on the calendar** gets an event when its minutes start. New minutes asks its name (blank means "Chapter meeting") and the day it met; the times are optional behind "Add times", and who reads the minutes is one line (Everyone) until changed to some roles. That event requires nobody, earns no points and sends no new-event push, so writing up a meeting after the fact never pings the chapter or changes anyone's attendance record.
  - Its times feed only the check-in window (and the week ring through it) and the order events list in, so with none typed (#5219): a meeting today starts now, rounded down to five minutes, so members can check in while it runs; an earlier day starts when the chapter's last event of the same name started, or 7 PM; and with no end it runs an hour. Link an event asks the same fields, except that its minutes are already written, so today there is read like any other day rather than as starting now.
  - Changed 2026-11-13 (#4935): making that event needed `events:create` as well as `meetings:record`. (`meetings:edit` alone still doesn't make one: linking orphaned minutes to a new event needs `meetings:record` or `events:create` too.) Every seeded tier holds both, so only a custom role that took `events:create` away was stopped, and it then could not start minutes for a meeting that wasn't on the calendar at all. Starting minutes is now enough, because this event can't do what `events:create` guards.
- **Writing.** The minutes page is the document and its editor, as a shared doc is: the author's draft opens ready to type, and posted minutes open to read, with Copy link as an icon and Edit and Delete (in red, behind a confirmation) in the ⋯ menu for whoever may change them (Paul, 2026-11-18: "your primary options should not be giant buttons"). The meeting's name and date, the save state and Post stay in a bar pinned to the top as the page scrolls, and Delete sits in its More menu. A draft saves itself a moment after typing stops, as a shared doc does, with "Saving…" and "Saved" in the bar and no Save button; edits to posted minutes keep Save and Cancel, since members already read them (Paul, 2026-11-16). Decisions and action items are boxes that grow to fit what is written. Meeting type sits under the sections. Attendees come from the event's check-ins (present, late, excused) and aren't typed. When nobody checked in, someone who can edit events (`events:update`, the same rule as the event's own attendance list) marks who was there by hand from the Attendees section: pick members, Present, Late or Excused. That fills in the event's attendance for anyone not marked yet, so the minutes and the event agree; like every officer mark it adds no points ([events.md § Marking attendance by hand](events.md#marking-attendance-by-hand); Paul, 2026-11-16). A decision may carry a vote count (for, against, abstaining), only when the meeting took one. An action item names someone only when the meeting did, and takes a due date. Empty decision and action rows are dropped on save.
- **Start from the phone's +** (#5287). The + panel's New minutes picks the meeting as [Recording in the app](#recording-in-the-app) describes, shows it with Change, and Record starts them recording where the phone can record, with "Type notes instead" under it, which opens them ready for notes (elsewhere Start minutes does). It is offered to every holder of `meetings:record` with the module on, as the event's Write minutes is.
- **Writing on the phone** (#5288; Paul, 2026-11-17: "typed notes become the minutes' Summary"). The phone opens minutes an officer may edit as a note: the event's name, chips (Draft, when, the type, how many checked in, Everyone or Invited only), then a box to type in on the page. Where the chapter drafts from notes the box is the officer's notes ([Drafting from notes](#drafting-from-notes)). Where it doesn't, the box is the minutes' Summary section, saved as the web's draft saves (once typing rests, naming the version it was typed over, so a save over someone else's newer one is refused and offers theirs with nothing lost), and Post works from the phone, saving what is waiting first. A line under it says decisions and action items are added on the web, until the phone has a section editor (#4412). The other sections, written on the web, read under the box.
- **The live-meeting card.** While an event's check-in window is open and it has no minutes, its creator, when they hold `meetings:record`, sees a card at the foot of the web's left nav (not in the 56px rail, which has no room for it, nor on the phone, where the event's own Write minutes row is the way in): "{event} is on", with Start minutes (one tap, as Write minutes) and Not now, which hides it for that event in that browser. Only the creator, not every officer who records minutes (Paul's pick, 2026-11-16, #5219), so one meeting doesn't nudge the whole exec board.
- **Two people editing.** A save carries the version it was made on. If someone saved since, the save is refused, the officer's typing stays on screen, and the page offers to load the other version. Nothing is overwritten silently.
- **Posting** asks nothing first (Paul, 2026-11-16: "Should not need confirmation"). The page turns into the read view the moment Post is pressed and the post lands behind it; a failure puts the editor back with every word and says so. It shows the minutes to the audience and tells each member of it once, except the officer who posted: "Minutes posted", "The minutes from {event} are up." in the `events` notification category. Posting again tells nobody, and edits after posting tell nobody.
- **Who changes them.** The author edits their own minutes, draft or posted, while the event still shows itself to them; a holder of `meetings:edit` edits anyone's. The author deletes their own unposted draft; a holder of `meetings:delete` deletes any minutes.
- **On for every chapter.** The `meetings` module was held off for the beta in `BETA_OFF_MODULES` until the AI build-out reached `main`; it left that list on 2026-11-12 (Paul), so a chapter keeps minutes unless it switches the module off. Drafting from notes still needs AI on (below).

## Drafting from notes

The officer who takes the minutes pastes or types what they wrote down during the meeting and asks for a draft ([ADR-28](../architecture/adr/adr-28.md) decision 6). The draft lands in the same sections as hand-written minutes, for them to fix and post. Web first (#4312); the recording path reuses the same draft, and so does the phone (#4415): an officer who may edit a draft types notes on the minutes page itself, uses the draft and posts, and changes a section by hand on the web, because the section editor has no phone twin yet. Where drafting isn't offered the phone still offers Write minutes, and what is typed is the Summary ([Writing on the phone](#writing-minutes-by-hand)).
  - Changed 2026-11-17 (#5288): the phone offered Write minutes on an event only where drafting was offered, so a chapter without it could not start minutes on a phone at all.

- **Where it is.** A "Your notes" box sits above the sections of a draft the officer may edit. Since #4935 it saves as the officer types, and other members can take notes too ([Several note-takers](#several-note-takers)). It shows only to a holder of `meetings:record`, when the server has AI on and the president has turned meeting notes on ([`ai.md` § Consent](ai.md#consent)). An officer who has not turned Frapp AI on sees the box with one plain line saying so ("Turn on Frapp AI to draft from your notes."), and asking opens Meet Frapp AI first.
- **Asking** needs the right to edit these minutes as well. Typing in the sections is saved first. If the sections already hold something, the page asks before the draft replaces them. Attendees are never touched.
- **While it is written,** the sections wait and can't be typed in, Post waits, and the box offers Stop. Stopping keeps the notes, in the box and stored, and nothing is put into the minutes.
- **It never overwrites a save.** The draft goes into the minutes only if they are still at the version it was asked on. If someone saved meanwhile, the draft waits and the officer chooses Use the draft, which asks before replacing what is written and goes onto the newest version by the same rule. While a draft is being put in, the page never shows it as waiting.
- **Only what the notes say.** Every decision and action item must come with the line of the notes it was drawn from, word for word: one line, never two joined, and enough of it to be that line (a single word or number backs nothing). An item whose line isn't in the notes is dropped, and the officer is told how many were. A vote count is kept only when its numbers are in that line. An owner is kept only when that line names exactly one member, by name or by a role only one member holds; a line that names two members names no owner. Otherwise the item shows "Nobody named". A due date is kept only when that line says when (a number, a weekday, "tomorrow"). An award of points ("give Jake 50 points") is never an action item, and isn't counted as dropped. A quote that misses its line only in how it is written (letter case, spacing, curly quotes, end punctuation) is put back to that line word for word and kept, when exactly one line matches (Paul, 2026-11-16: a long transcript lost real decisions this way).
- **Notes come topic by topic, with detail** (Paul, 2026-11-17: "break down the discussion into what topics were discussed ... even when the meetings are short, there's still high amounts of detail"; Granola and Wispr Flow are the model). The summary is a short opening paragraph. The notes are an outline: each topic on a line of its own, its points under it as "- " bullets, a sub-point two spaces deeper. Every idea, proposal, concern, reason, number, name, date and plan said goes in, with who said it when the sources say, and where the topic landed; a short meeting still gets several points per topic. Web and phone draw the outline as a bold topic over its bullets; text with no bullets, typed by hand or older, reads line by line as before. Decisions and action items keep their word-for-word rule; the notes don't need quotes. The summary and the notes are written by a pass of their own (`minutes.notes`), on the strongest model, which reads every source whole, never in parts, beside the draft and the editor's pass; when it fails, the draft's own summary and notes stand (Paul, 2026-11-17: "I would rather do a good job first and figure out how to optimize tokens later").
  - Changed 2026-11-17 by pdcarlson (#5410): the discussion log was "one line per topic" and the drafter's rules allowed no headings.
- **Long meetings are drafted in parts, then edited as one.** Notes or a transcript from one source longer than about twenty-five minutes of talk (24,000 characters) are cut into parts, each drafted on its own, side by side. A cut falls at a line break, else at the end of a sentence, else between words, because a transcript is often one line the whole meeting long (Paul's 4-hour recording was a single 130,000-character line). Each part, or a shorter draft's one pass, lists every candidate decision and action item with the words it came from. Every draft then gets an editor's pass, short or long and from one note-taker or several, because a procedural motion reads as a decision in an hour's notes as much as in four. It reads every candidate from the whole meeting, with each part's account of it, and writes the minutes: one decision per outcome, stated with its substance (the amount, the conditions, who), the motion to discuss, the amendment and the vote that settled one matter merged into it, and every procedural step dropped. The discussion log and summary are written for the whole meeting, never mentioning parts. The editor never writes a quote: each item it keeps names the candidates it stands on and takes its words from the first, so every item is still checked word for word against the notes. Whatever stops the editor's pass (a reply that doesn't parse, a refusal, the spend cap reached on that call, an error), the first pass's items stand in meeting order, each once, with procedure dropped, because they are already paid for and checked. An item the editor keeps that names no candidate has no words in the notes, so it is left out and counted like any other. If any part can't be drafted, the whole draft fails as a one-pass draft would, so a draft missing an hour of the meeting never reads as complete. Several note-takers are never cut into parts.
- **A decision is an outcome, never procedure** (Paul, 2026-11-17, on a draft that listed "voted to move on to new business" and "voted to discuss the afterparty": "Voting to discuss is not a decision. What matters is the body of the discussion."). Moving to an agenda item, opening or closing the floor or nominations, approving the agenda, a motion to discuss or consider something, calling the question, a recess and adjourning are left out of decisions; tabling, postponing and sending to a committee are outcomes and stay. An election is one decision per office. Every draft is told to list every substantive decision and action item, not only the main ones (Paul, 2026-11-16), and the discussion log gives each topic what was raised, the positions and where it landed.
- **Honest gaps.** What the notes leave unsaid is listed under "To fill in" (who is doing something, a vote with no count), not guessed. "To fill in" sits above the minutes, the first thing to do after a draft, and each question takes an answer. "Update the draft" adds the answers to the end of the author's notes, one line per question with its answer, and drafts again without asking, so the answers are quoted like any other line and, as the author's own notes, outrank the other sources (Paul, 2026-11-16). Once a draft has filled the minutes, the notes box folds away under them behind Show. Each drafted decision and action item shows the line it came from until the officer rewrites it.
- **Notes are text, not instructions.** A line in the notes addressed to the AI is not followed.
- **The noise stays out** (Paul and Shawn, 2026-11-18, after a Cabinet Meeting draft quoted a member calling someone "the number one pain in the ass to deal with"). Minutes are the chapter's record, read by every member and kept, so the draft and the notes leave out side conversations (banter, tangents, talk that wasn't the meeting's business), insults, venting about a named person, gossip and anything about someone's private life. A real problem with someone's work that was meeting business is written plainly and neutrally as it affects the chapter, without the remark, and swearing becomes plain words. Trimming is for noise: no decision, assignment, number, date, amount or plan is dropped to make the minutes shorter. The transcript is kept whole, as recorded, for reading and for Ask. Filtering in the draft was Paul's pick over hiding lines by hand after posting. A name the transcript misheard ("Alex Bogus", "Olton") is written as the member it plainly is when only one member on the roster sounds like it (Paul, 2026-11-18); the transcription is also given the roster's names to listen for.
- **Nothing is posted.** The draft is an unposted draft like any other. Posting is the officer's own step, and action items stay as written for the officer; turning them into suggested tasks is [Suggested tasks](#suggested-tasks).
- **When it can't be done,** the box says why in plain words and keeps the notes: the chapter's AI limit was reached, drafting is unavailable, the provider declined, or the draft didn't come through and can be asked for again.
- **Standards.** A Standards meeting has no minutes in Frapp, so there is nothing to draft into. Minutes whose type is changed to Standards are refused by the server before any text leaves ([Standards meetings](#standards-meetings)).
- **How long the notes are kept.** The notes are kept only while the minutes are an unposted draft, so "Draft again" works and the box is as the officer left it. With them is what the officer checks the draft against: the "To fill in" list, and each drafted decision and action item beside the line it came from. The drafted sections themselves are kept apart from the minutes only while a draft waits to be used. They are deleted when the minutes are posted or deleted, when the officer clears them, when the officer deletes their account, and 30 days after the draft was asked for whatever else happened.

> **Added 2026-11-12 (#4312, the drafting session's, Paul's to change).** Keeping the notes and the check list while the minutes are a draft (which [ADR-28](../architecture/adr/adr-28.md) decision 8 did not foresee, and is noted there), the 30-day limit, and replacing the sections rather than merging into them were not stated before. The conservative choice in each case: the shortest keep that still lets the officer ask again, and a replace the page asks about first.

## Several note-takers

Several members take notes on the same meeting, each in their own notes, and the officer who started the minutes asks for one draft that reads all of them and the recording ([#4935](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/4935)). Asked by Paul, 2026-11-13: "allow multiple people to take meeting notes at a time and AI should merge them depending on the authority of the users involved and the recording itself." Decided by Paul the same day, from the four questions on the issue. Built on the API, the web and the phone. On the phone the author's notes box on the minutes page says what else the draft reads and lists Notes disagreed, and a note-taker opens their own notes from the event's **Take notes** row.

- **Who takes notes.** Anyone the minutes' event shows itself to, on unposted minutes of a type Frapp drafts, who is one of: the officer who started the minutes; a holder of `meetings:edit`; an officer with `meetings:record`, who joins by writing; or any member of the meeting's audience the author or a `meetings:edit` holder added. An added member is told once ("Take notes", in the `events` category), and the event sheet then offers them **Take notes**.
- **Each writes in their own notes,** saved as they type, on the web or, for a note-taker, the phone. The author's notes on the phone are saved as they type too (#5288), and also kept on the phone until a draft is asked for, so a back swipe with no signal loses nothing. An emptied box is saved only before anything is drafted, so a transcript's own draft never reads notes the officer deleted; once a draft exists, empty notes would clear it with them, which only Clear notes does. A save that fails says so under the box and tries again as they type. A note-taker sees only their own. The author and `meetings:edit` holders see every note-taker's notes, read-only, under **Notes for this meeting**, with the recording. A note-taker never sees the draft: a draft stays its author's until it is posted ([Who can read minutes](#who-can-read-minutes)).
- **One draft reads every source.** Asking for a draft (the author or a `meetings:edit` holder, as before) reads each note-taker's notes and the recording's transcript, each named in the prompt by whose it is. Everything in [Drafting from notes](#drafting-from-notes) holds for every source: every decision and action item quotes one line of one source word for word, its vote, owner and day only as that line gives them; gaps go under "To fill in"; no source's text is ever an instruction. Each drafted item shows whose line it is ("Sam Abernathy", "Recording") beside the line.
- **The recording is its own source.** Its transcript is no longer put into the uploader's notes ([Uploading a recording](#uploading-a-recording)); the draft reads it directly. A phone from before this change still puts it into its notes, and the draft then reads it once, as those notes.
- **When sources disagree** about the same decision or action item (a vote count, who owns an action, what was decided), the model doesn't choose: it lists each source's version, and Frapp picks one by a fixed order, in code, so nothing written in the notes can change it:
  1. the recording, where it covers the moment (a recording that missed the vote settles nothing about the vote);
  2. the minutes' author;
  3. holders of `meetings:edit`, and between two of them, the higher role tier;
  4. the note-taker in the higher role tier, by the chapter's own tier order ([`rbac.md` § Tiers and overrides](rbac.md#tiers-and-overrides));
  5. still tied: nothing goes into the minutes, and the author picks.
- **Nothing is dropped silently.** A version whose line isn't in its notes is counted as left out, and never lets a source that settles less win: when it came from the recording or a higher rank, nothing goes into the minutes and "To fill in" says to check it. The first twelve disagreements are listed; any more are settled the same way and "To fill in" says how many weren't listed. Every listed disagreement is under **Notes disagreed**, with each version, whose it is and its line, and how it was settled. The author can put another version in with one tap, or pick one for a tie; that replaces the drafted item when it still reads as drafted, and is added beside it when the officer has rewritten it.
- **A note-taker's own words go to a provider only with their own yes** on Frapp AI ([`ai.md` § Consent](ai.md#consent)). Notes from a member who hasn't said yes, or who can no longer take notes on these minutes, are left out of the draft, and the author's list says whose notes wait on Frapp AI. This is checked when the draft is asked for and again when it is written, so a No said in between is kept. Clearing notes, or removing a note-taker, stops a draft still being written from them.
- **Standards.** Notes can't be taken on Standards minutes, by the same server check as a draft.
- **How long notes are kept.** By the notes rule, for each person: deleted when the minutes are posted or deleted, when the note-taker clears them, is removed as a note-taker, or deletes their account, and 30 days after they were started whatever else happened. A draft keeps what it was written from until it goes, and goes when any note-taker it read clears their notes, is removed or deletes their account.

> **Decided 2026-11-13 (Paul, #4935).** The four questions on the issue were answered with the recommended option each: who takes notes, own notes only until the draft, the order above, and the notes rule per person. The build session's own choices, Paul's to change in review: the recording became a source of its own instead of text in the uploader's notes; a note-taker's notes wait on their own yes; `meetings:edit` holders sit between the author and role tier; a member someone added stays a note-taker with empty notes after clearing them, while anyone else who clears theirs stops being one. If event agendas ([#4934](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/4934)) land, the draft follows the agenda's order; the prompt already takes one.

## Uploading a recording

The officer who recorded the meeting on their phone uploads the file on web and comes back to a transcript the draft reads beside everyone's notes ([Several note-takers](#several-note-takers); until #4935 it landed in "Your notes"), ready for [Drafting from notes](#drafting-from-notes) ([ADR-28](../architecture/adr/adr-28.md) decisions 1 and 7). Built 2026-11-12 ([#712](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/712)); web only, off unless the server has AI on, a transcription provider is set up and the president has turned meeting notes on.

- **Where it is.** One row at the top of the "Your notes" box, for the same officers who see the box. It always says what the recording is doing and offers the one thing to do about it.
- **Before anything is sent,** the upload step shows the [Recording notice](#recording-notice): the script that should have been read out, what happens to the file, and the Standards rule. The officer picks the file and ticks "Everyone in the room was told this meeting was being recorded, and nobody objected." The server refuses an upload without that tick, and keeps when it was given.
- **What it takes.** M4A, MP3, WAV, AAC, OGG or FLAC, up to 200 MB and 2 hours, and at least 10 seconds: what a phone's recorder makes of a chapter meeting. A provider with tighter limits narrows them, and the step says the real ones. The file is checked the moment it is picked, so a wrong file never starts uploading.
- **The upload is resumable** and goes straight from the browser to a private bucket (`meeting-audio`), signed for that one file. A dropped connection picks up where it left off. The row shows the percent sent and asks the officer to keep the page open; closing it loses the upload, and the row then says it was interrupted.
- **Transcribing takes minutes and needs nobody watching.** It is an AI run (`meeting_transcription`), so the row survives a reload or a closed tab, and Stop removes the recording. The provider is given the roster's names so it spells them right, and nothing else about the chapter.
- **The transcript is a source of its own.** One line per speaker turn, opened by "Speaker 1", "Speaker 2" in the order each voice is first heard ([Voices](#voices)). The draft reads it beside every note-taker's notes, and it settles a disagreement where it covers the moment ([Several note-takers](#several-note-takers)). The officer reads it under Notes for this meeting.
  - Changed 2026-11-13 (#4935): the transcript used to land in the officer's notes (empty notes were filled; otherwise the row offered "Add to your notes"), and the officer fixed names there before asking for a draft. With several note-takers it would then have read as the uploader's notes and ranked as theirs.
- **The audio is kept for playback until the minutes are posted** (Paul, 2026-11-17, picking "Keep audio until minutes are posted"): the officer plays it back from the minutes while checking the draft (`GET /v1/meetings/{id}/recording` carries `audio_url`, a link signed for an hour, only while the transcript is ready). Posting removes the recording's row, and the sweep every half hour deletes the file no row names any more. It is deleted at once when the transcription fails for good and when the officer removes or replaces the recording, and the sweep also takes what those missed (an upload nobody finished within six hours, a meeting deleted mid-transcription). The 30-day limit below ends it in any case. A provider that keeps a copy is told to delete it once the transcript is read.
  - Changed 2026-11-17 by pdcarlson (#5381): this line used to read "The audio is deleted as soon as it has been used: when the transcript is written, ..." The Privacy Policy changed with it (`legal.md` § Acceptance record).
- **The members who were at the meeting are told,** once per meeting, when transcription starts: a notification saying a recording was uploaded to write the minutes, that it is deleted once the minutes are posted, and to tell the secretary if they object. "Who was there" is the event's check-ins; with no check-ins it is the members the event invites.
- **When it can't be done,** the row says why in plain words and what to do next: the file couldn't be read as audio, no speech was heard, it is too long, the chapter's AI limit was reached, or the transcript didn't come through. In every case the audio is already deleted.
- **Standards.** A recording can't be uploaded for a Standards meeting, by the same server check as a draft, made before the upload is signed and again before the audio is sent to the provider ([Standards meetings](#standards-meetings)).
- **What it costs** is the recording's length at the provider's price per hour, held against the chapter's and the member's AI caps before the provider is called and settled at the length the provider measured ([`billing.md`](billing.md)).
- **How long the transcript is kept.** While the minutes are a draft, by the notes' rule: removed when the minutes are deleted, when the officer removes the recording (Clear notes on the web removes it with the notes), when the uploader deletes their account (which also takes any draft asked for while their recording was the meeting's, since its notes may hold the transcript; #4504), and after 30 days at most. Posting copies it onto the minutes, where it stays until they are deleted ([Retention](#retention)). Neither the audio, its link, the file's name nor the transcript is ever written to a log, an error report or an analytics event.

> **Added 2026-11-12 (#712, the build session's, Paul's to change).** Four things here were not decided before and took the conservative choice. (1) The audio is deleted when the transcript is written, not when the minutes are posted as [Retention](#retention) used to say: nothing built plays it back, so keeping it would hold a recording of a room for no use. Linking each drafted line to its moment in the audio (Workflow step 4) is therefore not built, and would need the audio kept. (2) The transcript is treated as the officer's notes, kept only while the minutes are a draft, and is not indexed for AI Q&A (Workflow step 7 is not built for transcripts). (3) The provider is AssemblyAI in the model catalog, because it is the only candidate that takes a whole meeting with speaker labels in one request; Google's transcription model is built as the challenger and caps a request at 30 minutes. The vendor is still Paul's pick after a bake-off on a real meeting, and no key exists yet. (4) Whether the tick and the notification are enough in a state that requires everyone's agreement is counsel's question ([#3297](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3297)); the wording is working copy. The notes box's limit rose from 40,000 to 160,000 characters so a two-hour transcript fits.
> - Changed 2026-11-17 by pdcarlson: (1) and (2) no longer hold. The audio is kept for playback until posting (#5381), and the transcript is kept with the posted minutes and indexed for Ask (#5410).

## Recording in the app

The secretary opens the meeting's draft minutes on their phone, sets the phone on the table and taps Record. One or two hours later they tap Stop. The recording uploads, is transcribed the same way an uploaded file is, and the draft reads the transcript beside everyone's notes ([Uploading a recording](#uploading-a-recording) owns everything from the upload on: transcription, the attendees' notification, deletion, cost, Standards). Built 2026-11-12 for iOS and Android; it needs a store build at native version 0.12.0 or later, and is offered only where the upload is: AI on, a transcription provider set up, meeting notes on, and a draft the officer may edit.

It is used once a week by one person, in a chapter room with weak wifi, with the phone locked for most of it. So the rules are about not losing two hours of a meeting:

- **The minutes screen is the recorder** (Paul, 2026-11-17, on the real screen: "Show me, not tell me", "I don't want a pop-up sheet for everything", buttons "pushed down to the bottom"). Voice Memos, Otter and Granola are the model. One thing is pinned at the bottom: the recorder's controls while it records or needs the officer, else Post once the minutes have words, else Draft minutes once there is something to draft from (notes typed here, another note-taker's, or a transcript, so a draft that failed can be asked for again), else Record (with a microphone). A refusal's Open Settings never takes Post's place. While recording the recorder is a bar pinned to the bottom (#5288, Granola and Voice Memos the model): pause (carry on while paused) and stop as round icons at its ends that never trade places, and between them a pulsing dot, the room's live input level and the running clock; red while recording, amber while paused. The page above stays the officer's note, and the keyboard can stay up while it records: the bar rides above it, and a tap on pause or stop doesn't close it. After Stop a card ticks off Recorded, Uploaded (with its real percent), Transcribed and Drafted, so it is plain nothing was lost; the step under way says what it is doing (Saving, Uploading, Transcribing, Drafting) beside a spinner, never the recording's level bars, and takes its milestone name once done (Paul, 2026-11-18); Draft minutes waits for the transcript, the draft is asked for by itself once the transcript is in and the minutes are empty, and lands on the page for review with Post at the bottom. The officer's notes are a box on the page, not a sheet, saved as they type; the recording's play control sits under them, and its transcript opens on its own screen (Paul, 2026-11-18: an hour of lines under the minutes buried them). On the web it opens in its own tab, beside the draft, from **Notes for this meeting** and from posted minutes. There is no Record again: a meeting with a transcript offers none. While the phone holds a recording for this meeting the recorder is there whatever the server says: with no connection, with transcription turned off since, or with the minutes posted from another device, the officer can still stop, upload or delete it.
  - Changed 2026-11-17 (#5288): while recording, the page showed a large running clock over moving bars, with two 72pt round controls at the bottom; the bar gives the page back to the notes.
  - Changed 2026-11-17 by pdcarlson (#5381): Record used to be one row above Draft minutes with words for buttons (Pause, Resume, Stop, Record again), notes in a sheet and the transcript in another.
- **From the event.** The event's Minutes row reads Record minutes where the phone can record and holds no recording now: it starts the minutes and opens them asking to record (the notice first). Elsewhere it reads Write minutes, as before.
- **From anywhere, through the +** (#5287; Paul, 2026-11-17: "If I go into Granola, like it's literally 2 buttons, like a plus and it just starts recording"). The tab bar's + panel's New minutes leads with Record, the meeting named on a row above it, and "Type notes instead" under it (Paul, 2026-11-17: "replace it for just the record button") ([`navigation.md` § Quick actions](../ui/mobile/navigation.md#quick-actions); one entry rather than a separate red row, Paul 2026-11-17: "Why have 2 options?"), offered where this row's Record is. It picks the meeting by itself: an event the member can see whose check-in window is open, of two the one whose start is nearest now (so the meeting about to start beats the one just ended, and a short meeting beats an all-day event); otherwise a new meeting, "Chapter meeting" today starting now, made by the rule under [Writing minutes by hand](#writing-minutes-by-hand). An event whose name reads as a hearing (Standards, hearing, J-Board) is never picked or listed. The meeting sits on a row of the sheet and of the notice with Change, which lists the meeting on now and the last two weeks' meetings without minutes, and a new one. When the meeting on now already has someone else's minutes (or the member's own posted ones), New minutes says so under the meeting and asks the officer to pick another, or to take notes from the event ([Several note-takers](#several-note-takers)), rather than starting a second meeting beside it. The member's own draft on it is reopened, and recorded straight away only when it has no recording yet: one uploading, transcribing or ready opens the minutes as they are. Start recording starts the minutes (or opens the draft), opens them and starts recording, so the officer lands on the minutes already recording. Before any minutes exist the phone learns whether a recording can be written out, why the member can't send one, and the limits from `GET /v1/meeting-recorder`, under the same guards as a meeting's recording. With transcription not set up, New minutes offers no Record; recording then is #5289.
- **The notice comes first, every time.** Record opens the [Recording notice](#recording-notice) as a sheet: the rule, the script to read aloud, what happens to the audio, the Standards rule, and one tick, "Everyone in the room has been told this meeting is being recorded, and nobody objected." Only Start recording with the box ticked opens the microphone, and the tick does not carry over to the next meeting. An officer who has not answered the AI question is asked it before the notice. The upload later carries the tick to the server as the upload step's does.
- **Before the microphone opens** the phone checks three things and says which one failed: microphone permission (with a way to Settings), on Android permission to show the notification that keeps a recording alive with the screen off, and free space for a whole meeting (about 120 MB).
- **While recording** the page shows a red dot, "Recording" and a running clock, with the pause and stop icons. Stop asks first. On every other screen a pill at the top of the window says "Recording 1:02:33"; a tap opens the minutes. Recording continues with the screen locked and with Frapp in the background (iOS's audio background mode; on Android a foreground service with a system notification).
- **A phone call, Siri or another app taking the microphone** pauses the recording. The row and the pill say "Paused", not "Recording", and the row says why. iOS hands the microphone back by itself after most interruptions and recording then carries on; when it doesn't, the phone asks again every few seconds, and the officer can tap the carry-on icon. A pause the officer made themselves stays a pause through a call: the phone does not start recording again when the call ends. Nothing recorded before the interruption is lost.
- **The recording stops by itself** at the longest the server takes (2 hours), and when the phone has under 25 MB free. Either way what was recorded is kept and the row says why it stopped. It does not upload by itself then: the officer taps Upload.
- **Stop uploads at once,** through the same signed, resumable upload as the web's, in pieces, with the real percent on the row and on the pill. The officer is asked to keep Frapp open. A dropped connection is retried, then the row says the upload didn't finish; Try again carries on from what was already sent. Cancel stops it and keeps the file.
- **The recording stays on the phone until the server has it.** It is deleted from the phone the moment the server confirms the upload, when the officer taps Delete (which asks first), and otherwise after 7 days. Nothing else removes it: not a failed upload, not signing out, not closing the app.
- **If the app is closed or killed while recording,** what was written up to that moment is still a playable file. On the next launch the pill says a recording hasn't been uploaded, and the row says one was kept and how long it is, and offers Upload or Delete. The same on a launch after a failed upload.
- **One recording at a time for each member.** While the phone holds the officer's recording for one meeting, another meeting's row points to it instead of offering Record.
- **A recording belongs to the member who made it, in the chapter they made it in.** Another member signing in on the same phone, or the same member in another chapter, is not shown it, can't upload it, and doesn't remove it by recording a meeting of their own. It waits for its owner to sign back in, and goes when its 7 days pass.
- **The transcript is a source of its own** on the phone as on the web: the draft reads it beside everyone's notes. Clearing the notes removes the recording.
  - Changed 2026-11-16 (#4935): the transcript used to go into "Your notes" on the phone (into an empty box by itself, behind "Add the transcript" when the officer had already typed).
- **Standards.** The notice says never to record a Standards hearing, and the server refuses the upload for one before anything is stored, as it does on the web. The row then says so and keeps the file for the officer to delete.
- **Nothing about a recording is logged.** No audio, file path, link, file name or transcript goes to a log, an error report or an analytics event, and what the phone writes down to find the file again is where it is, whose it is (the member and the chapter), the meeting and its event's name, when it started, how long it is and why it stopped: nothing said in the meeting and no upload signature.
- **Expo Go and the web build of the app have no recorder.** The row then shows only what was uploaded elsewhere.

> **Added 2026-11-12 (the build session's, Paul's to change).** Choices made here that nobody had decided. (1) The file is one channel of AAC at 64 kbps in an ADTS container (`.aac`), about 58 MB for two hours. ADTS rather than the usual `.m4a` because an `.m4a` cut off by a crash can't be read at all. (2) A recording left on a phone is deleted after 7 days. (3) An existing audio file can't be picked on the phone: that needs a second native module (`expo-document-picker`, [#3401](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3401)), so uploading a file stays on the web. (4) A killed app's upload starts again from the beginning, because the upload's signature is not written to the phone's storage. (5) Android's recording notification reads "Recording audio", the audio library's fixed wording. (6) The microphone permission text in `app.json` is a draft for Paul to approve before a store build. (7) On Android the two-hour stop is checked by the app's clock, which Android may hold while the screen is off; a meeting that ran past two hours is still uploaded and transcribed, declared to the server as two hours. For the same reason a recording stopped from Android's notification is measured by its file's size, not by the clock. None of this has run on a device: the list of what to check on one is in the PR that added this section.

## Suggested tasks

Posted minutes produce suggested tasks, as [`ai.md` § Suggestions](ai.md#suggestions) describes for every proposal. Web first (#4313); the phone reads the same list (#4415; [`../ui/mobile/screens.md`](../ui/mobile/screens.md), the Suggested row). Both show one suggestion at a time (web since Paul, 2026-11-18: "one at a time at the top"), in the order of the minutes; the phone has no Create all.

- **When.** Posting minutes turns each action item into a suggested task. A draft suggests nothing: its action items are still being written, and a draft is its author's. Hand-written minutes are treated exactly as drafted ones are.
- **No model is asked.** By the time minutes post, an action item already holds what a task needs: its words, an owner only when one was named, a day only when one was said. A drafted item got those from the notes under the checks in [Drafting from notes](#drafting-from-notes), and the officer read it before posting. So a suggested task is the action item as posted, and nothing leaves Frapp to make it.
- **The officer creates them, not the AI.** Each suggested task shows its title, its owner and its due date, and an officer creates it (the button reads **Create task**, not Accept, which read as "this is mine"; Paul, 2026-11-18), changes it first, or dismisses it. Creating makes the task under that officer's own permission, as the task form would, and its assignee is told as for any task. Nothing is a task until then.
- **Where.** At the top of the posted minutes, one card with "1 of 3" and previous and next, and in a Suggested list at the top of the Tasks board, each card naming the minutes it came from with a link back to them. While one is being created the others stay in reach. Only someone who may manage tasks and may read those minutes sees either. With nothing waiting, neither place shows anything. Changed 2026-11-18 (Paul: suggested tasks "should just be a small piece at the top"): on web's Tasks board the list sits behind a one-line strip, "3 suggested tasks from the minutes of …", whose Review opens it in place.
- **An owner only when one was named, or suggested where the officer can see it.** The minutes' own owner and day are filled in as said. Where they said none, the web's card suggests one beside each field, marked as a suggestion: the member the item's own words name, when exactly one member fits (a full name, or a first name only one member has; a verb that opens the line, such as "Mark the date", is not a name), and a day a week after the meeting, when that day hasn't passed (Paul, 2026-11-18: "suggested person assigned ... suggested due date ... one less thing to be entering in"). Otherwise it shows "Who?" with the shared Who picker, the one the Tasks page uses, so a suggestion can go to a member, several, a role or group, a copy each, or up for grabs for one of them ([`tasks.md`](tasks.md)). A suggestion is saved only from the card that showed it, by pressing Create task; Create task asks for whichever is still missing, in place.
  - Changed 2026-11-18 (Paul): this used to fill in nothing the minutes hadn't said, on the grounds that a wrong owner is worse than a blank one. A suggestion the officer sees on the card before creating it keeps that protection.
- **Two members with the same first name are the officer's pick,** never a guess.
- **A suggested task never carries points.** "Give Jake 50 points" spoken in a meeting is not an instruction. The officer adds points by hand, on the task.
- **Creating several.** Create all sits under the card and takes only the ones whose minutes or officer already said who and when, never one that leans on a suggestion, since it creates cards the officer may not have brought up.
- **Action items follow their tasks.** On posted minutes, an action item an officer made a task shows a checkbox, ticked once the task is done; a task for everyone is done once every copy is, and its item reads "N of M done". At a glance at the top counts the action items and how many are done. Only the done mark and those counts reach the page, never the task itself: anyone who may read the minutes sees them, an exception to [`tasks.md` § Visibility](tasks.md#visibility) recorded there. An item keeps its box through edits to its capitals, spacing or punctuation; reworded, it is a new item and loses it. An item created as up for grabs has no box until someone claims it and the box learns to follow the claim.
- **Each action item is suggested once.** Editing posted minutes follows them: a new action item is suggested, a removed one's waiting suggestion is taken back, and a changed owner or day replaces the waiting suggestion. An item an officer already accepted or dismissed is never suggested again, and neither is one whose capitals, spacing or punctuation were tidied. Rewording an item makes it a new item.
- **Who is told.** The officer who posted the minutes gets one notification for all of them, and only if they could accept one. Edits after posting tell nobody.
- **Off unless** the server has AI on and the president has meeting notes on ([`ai.md` § Consent](ai.md#consent)). No member's answer on the AI sheet is asked for, because nothing is sent to a provider.

> **Changed 2026-11-12 (#4313, the drafting session's, Paul's to change).** This section used to put the review on the draft's screen and show each task beside the line of the officer's notes it came from. Suggestions are now made when the minutes post, from the posted action items, with no second model call: the action items are already structured and already read by the officer, and the notes are private to their author and deleted at posting ([Drafting from notes](#drafting-from-notes)), so a line of them is not copied onto a suggestion other officers read. The line a suggested task shows is the action item in the minutes. Matching an item by its words, so that rewording one after it was decided suggests it again, is also this session's choice.

## Standards meetings

A Standards meeting (a standards board or member-conduct hearing) is never recorded, never transcribed and never sent to a model.

- **None of the three ways offers the type.** Notes are covered as much as recordings: an officer can't paste hearing notes into the drafter and pick Standards.
- **The server refuses it too.** A request to draft minutes for a meeting typed Standards is rejected before any text is sent anywhere. The check does not live only in the app. Hand-written minutes can't be typed Standards either: `POST /v1/meetings` answers 400 `meeting.standards_refused`, and the table's own check refuses the type.
- **An officer writes Standards minutes by hand,** and they are stored in the [vault](vault.md), which the AI never reads. Until the vault exists they are kept outside Frapp.
- **Filing a hearing under another type to get a draft breaks this rule,** and the product can't detect it. The notes screen says so in one line where the type is picked, as the hand-written minutes' type picker already does.

## Recording notice

A meeting is recorded only after the room has been told.

- **The officer reads a short script aloud before recording starts,** for example: "This meeting is being recorded and transcribed by Frapp. Tell the secretary if you object."
- **Frapp posts a notice to the meeting's attendees.** For an uploaded recording that is a notification when the upload finishes, and the officer confirms in the upload step that the script was read ([Uploading a recording](#uploading-a-recording)). For a recording made in the app the officer confirms before the microphone opens, and the same notification goes out when the phone's upload finishes ([Recording in the app](#recording-in-the-app)).
- **The recording phone shows a visible indicator for as long as it records:** the row on the meeting's minutes, a pill at the top of every other screen in the app, and outside the app the system's own mark (iOS's microphone indicator, Android's recording notification).
- **Anyone present may object, and the officer then doesn't record, or pauses.**

Apple requires explicit consent and a visible indication for any recording (App Review Guideline 2.5.14). Several states require every person present to agree, so a chapter in one of them should confirm the script with counsel first.

## Voices

Frapp stores no voiceprint and never recognises a member by voice. Speaker labels belong to one meeting ("Speaker 2"), and an officer maps a label to a name by hand. Nothing about a voice is kept or reused across meetings.

## Permissions

- Starting minutes, by notes, upload or recording, requires the `meetings:record` permission. Default seeds: President, Secretary, exec roles. It is on the Exec board tier, so every role in the chapter's top tier holds it ([`rbac.md`](rbac.md)).
- Taking notes on someone else's unposted minutes takes `meetings:record`, or being added by their author or a `meetings:edit` holder ([Several note-takers](#several-note-takers)). Adding and removing note-takers is the author's and `meetings:edit` holders'; a note-taker can remove themselves.
- Linking minutes whose event was deleted to another event takes `meetings:edit` ([Who can read minutes](#who-can-read-minutes)).
- Editing someone else's minutes requires `meetings:edit` (default: President). The officer who started them always edits their own.
- Deleting minutes requires `meetings:delete` (default: President only). The officer who started them may delete their own unposted draft.
- Reading needs no meetings permission: the audience rule above decides it.

## Retention

- **Raw audio is kept until the minutes are posted, and never more than 30 days,** for the officer to play back; it is deleted at once when its transcription fails for good ([Uploading a recording](#uploading-a-recording)). Audio can't be anonymised, so a member deleting their account removes the recordings they uploaded.
  - Changed in #712 by the build session, 2026-11-12, pending Paul: this line used to read "deleted when the minutes are posted. A recording whose minutes are never posted is deleted after 30 days."
  - Changed back 2026-11-17 by pdcarlson (#5381): Paul picked "Keep audio until minutes are posted" so it can be played back from the minutes.
- **A recording's transcript is kept with the posted minutes** (Paul, 2026-11-17: "it should store the transcript ... and we should be able to query that transcript with the AI. That's half the leverage"). Posting copies it onto the minutes (`meetings.transcript`) before the recording's row goes; the posted minutes show it under Transcript, readable by the meeting's audience, and it is indexed for Ask with them ([Workflow](#workflow) step 7). It goes when the minutes are deleted. While the minutes are a draft it stays on the recording's row, and goes with it as the officer's notes do, never more than 30 days.
  - Changed 2026-11-17 by pdcarlson (#5410): this line used to read "kept as the officer's notes are: only while the minutes are unposted, and never more than 30 days." The Privacy Policy changed with it (`legal.md` § Acceptance record).
- **Transcripts and posted minutes** follow the chapter's general [data retention](data-retention.md) policy. When a member deletes their account, their name on a speaker label becomes "Deleted User", as it does on their chat messages.
- **An officer's notes for a draft** are kept only while the minutes are unposted, and never more than 30 days ([Drafting from notes](#drafting-from-notes)).
- **Each note-taker's notes** follow the same rule, per person, and also go when the note-taker clears them, is removed or deletes their account ([Several note-takers](#several-note-takers)).
- **Standards minutes** in the vault follow the vault retention rules.
