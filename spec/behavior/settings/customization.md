# Settings: Customization Tabs

Behavior rules for the customization-heavy settings tabs (Accent, Roles, Fields, Dues) and the workflows behind two module pages. The settings rail, the Org tab, the module pages, and cross-cutting audit rules live in [`README.md`](README.md). Saves in these tabs write `chapter_audit_log` rows mirrored to `#chapter-audit`. On the Roles tab that covers creating, editing, recolouring, reordering or deleting any role in the Manage roles section, every permission-matrix toggle, a presidency transfer, the default invite role and a presidency claim. Which verbs and diffs the role routes write, and which writes skip a save that changes nothing, is in the README's [audit rules](README.md#audit-rules).

## Accent (a section of Chapter, `?tab=theme`)

Accent is a section of the Chapter row, under the chapter mark, since #3827 (2026-11-11); `?tab=theme` opens Chapter.


- A chapter sets **one colour**: the accent seed. Every accent role is derived from it, and the neutral ladder — backgrounds, borders, sidebar, text — is fixed rather than branded ([`../../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md`](../../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md) §1 and §5). A second **dark** colour existed until the #920 slice-9 cutover, which deleted the legacy engine it fed; #541 tracked building a picker for it and is obsolete. Accent-color rules, brand boundaries, and logo upload (the shared `image` kind in `@repo/validation`, not an Accent-tab-specific allowlist) are canon in [`../branding.md`](../branding.md).
- Saving recomputes the chapter's `theme_palette` **server-side** and the client refetches and re-applies the accent roles immediately — no full reload.
- **WCAG:** which accent roles are contrast-guaranteed is [`../../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md`](../../ui/design-system/accent-engine.md) §8's, and seed validation is [`../branding.md`](../branding.md) § Accent Color's. The tab's inline warnings, and their copy, are in [`writing.md` § Settings (dashboard)](../../ui/design-system/writing.md#settings-dashboard); what the preview fallback does is in [`../branding.md` § Accent Color](../branding.md#accent-color). Derivation is archetype-agnostic.

## Roles Tab

**Tiers, then roles, then switches** (#3990, 2026-11-12). Paul asked for "toggles, not the matrix", set per tier: "under roles you would have tiers, and then within each tier you would have all the different roles". The tab is three pages addressed by the URL (layout: [`../../ui/web-dashboard/README.md`](../../ui/web-dashboard/README.md)). Its history: four sub-tabs (Pack, Matrix, Custom, Live roles) until [#2146](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2146), then board `4e`'s live roles × permissions matrix until #3990 deleted it. `/roles` still redirects here.

- **The tier list** shows the chapter's tiers in their order with the roles in each and how many members hold each role, then the roles outside the tiers. Here an officer adds, renames, moves up or down and deletes a tier (only an empty one), adds a role into a tier (it starts with the tier's permissions), moves a role to another tier, and deletes a role. A search box finds a role by name in a chapter with many.
- **A tier's permissions** are one switch per permission, grouped by module, each saved on click. A switch changes every role in the tier; a role set apart from its tier keeps its difference ([`../rbac.md`](../rbac.md)).
- **A role's page** carries its name, color, tier, position in its list and @mention switch (saved together, and the save never sends `permissions`, so a switch flipped since the page opened is not rolled back), then its permissions. A role in a tier shows the tier's answer beside the role's own, Off, Same as tier or On (#4072): Same as tier follows the tier when it changes, and Off or On keeps the role's answer whatever the tier does later. A role outside the tiers has one column of switches. A "View as" button previews the dashboard as the role (#4072; [`../../ui/web-dashboard/README.md`](../../ui/web-dashboard/README.md)). The President's role holds everything and every switch is locked: the wildcard moves only through presidency transfer.
- **Rank rules show before they refuse.** Anything the API would refuse under the rank rules (a tier or role at or above the caller's own, or switching on a permission the caller lacks) is disabled with the reason. The API stays the authority.
- **On the phone** (#4074, 2026-11-12), More → Admin → Chapter settings → Roles shows the same tiers and roles, a tier's switches, and a role's answers, under the same rank rules (one copy, `@repo/hooks/role-tiers`). A role in a tier lists each permission's answer (On or Off), and a row set apart from its tier says so; a tap opens Same as tier, On and Off in a sheet. Adding, renaming, reordering and deleting tiers and roles, a role's name, color and @mention switch, View as, and presidency transfer stay on web. Layout: [`../../ui/mobile/navigation.md` § Chapter settings](../../ui/mobile/navigation.md#chapter-settings).
- **Presidency transfer** is shown only to the caller holding everything, and the orphan-president claim banner to anyone who reaches the tab.
- **The role pack** has no view and no label of its own (2026-11-06: the header label went, because which pack seeded the roles is a setup detail officers don't act on). Assigning members to roles happens in the member directory, not here.
- **This tab does not require `chapter-config:view`.** The pages read the roles and the tiers, neither of which is chapter config. A `roles:manage` holder reaches it through the nav's Settings row, which admits that permission on its own, so gating the tab on the config read would send them to a config error. That is exactly what happened while the nav had a Roles row of its own. Only the default-invite-role control below degrades when the config read fails.
- **Default invite role** (a chapter-level setting about roles rather than a view of one): nominates the role new invites use when the sender does not pick one. Writes `chapters.default_invite_role_id` through the config PATCH, so it needs `chapter-config:manage` and is audit-logged like the other audited settings saves. An explicit **No role** option clears it, returning invites to the seeded Everyone role (once Member, [`../rbac.md` § The Everyone role](../rbac.md#the-everyone-role)), and a default naming Everyone reads as No role. A role id that does not belong to this chapter is rejected with `400`, and deleting the role clears the setting (`on delete set null`) rather than leaving it dangling. Full resolution and validation rules: [`../rbac.md`](../rbac.md) → Default Invite Role.
- **One role system** ([#4075](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/4075), 2026-11-11). Every role the chapter has is a column of the matrix: seeded, created on this tab, or imported from Discord. Board `4e` pin 1 asked for custom roles to append as columns; they now do, because they are ordinary roles. Until #4075 a separate Custom roles section edited `chapter_custom_roles` (label, rank, capabilities, `core`), a second table bridged into enforcement through `members.custom_role_ids`; Paul approved removing that section on 2026-11-10, and migration `20261111210000` turned each custom role into a role with the same permissions and moved its assignments into `role_ids`. What it did and what is left: [`../rbac.md` § Custom roles folded into roles](../rbac.md#custom-roles-folded-into-roles). The former standalone `/roles` page still redirects to `/settings?tab=roles`.

## Fields Tab

- An editable table over `chapter_custom_fields`. Each field has: label, type (`text` / `number` / `decimal` / `phone` / `select` / `boolean`), `required`, **visibility** (`self` / `chapter` / `exec` / `president`), and a `sensitive` flag.
- Add-field configuration is type-specific (text → max length; select → options list; the remaining types → none). A `select` field must declare a non-empty options list before it can be saved (enforced on both the shared validation boundary and the server).
- Unlike the Workflows/Dues tabs, which write through the config blob, custom fields are individually-addressable rows and use **dedicated CRUD endpoints** (`GET/POST /custom-fields`, `PATCH/DELETE /custom-fields/:id`); reads require `chapter-config:view` and writes require `chapter-config:manage`. Every write **through these endpoints** appends a `chapter_audit_log` row (mirrored to `#chapter-audit`) like the other audited settings saves — actions `chapter_custom_field_created` / `chapter_custom_field_updated` / `chapter_custom_field_deleted`; the onboarding seed below is the one write that does not. A duplicate `(chapter_id, key)` is rejected (`409`). `key` and `type` are immutable after creation (changing `type` would orphan stored member values); every field is deletable (there is no `core` concept).
- **A new chapter is seeded from the archetype at onboarding.** `ChapterOnboardingService` inserts one `chapter_custom_fields` row per `CUSTOM_FIELDS_SEED` entry, so the Fields tab opens populated instead of empty. `key` is derived from the seed label as a lowercase slug (`T-shirt size` → `t_shirt_size`), falling back to the slugified seed id when a label yields no slug or two labels collide, so the derivation is deterministic. Every candidate row is validated with the same `CreateCustomFieldSchema` the `POST /custom-fields` contract uses, so a seeded row is always one an officer could also have created by hand; an entry that fails it (a `select` with no choices) or that duplicates another entry's id is skipped and logged rather than inserted. The seed write is **best-effort and idempotent** (skip-existing on `(chapter_id, key)`): a failure leaves an empty tab an officer can fill in by hand rather than costing them the chapter.
- **The onboarding seed is the one write that does not audit.** It appends no `chapter_audit_log` rows, matching the default roles and channels seeded alongside it and keeping a brand-new chapter's `#chapter-audit` clear — nobody has acted yet. This is the sole exception to the every-write-audits rule above; do not "fix" it by routing the seed through the CRUD service, which would open every new chapter with eight audit messages.
- **A field added without an explicit `sort` is appended, not prepended.** `POST /custom-fields` defaults `sort` to `max(sort) + 1` for the chapter (0 when it has no fields yet); the Fields tab sends no `sort`, so this is the path every hand-added field takes. Fields render by `sort` then `created_at`, so a fixed default of `0` would interleave hand-added fields with the onboarding-seeded ones rather than placing them at the bottom.
- **Custom-field options lists are deep-cloned per chapter.** Editing one chapter's options must never mutate another chapter's options (no sharing of a seed reference). This is honored by construction: each chapter's row owns its own `options` jsonb, the service `structuredClone`s the payload on write, and the archetype seed (`CUSTOM_FIELDS_SEED` in `@repo/org-archetypes`) is already deep-cloned by `buildChapterConfigFromArchetype` when a chapter is provisioned.
- Visibility semantics are enforced when rendering the member directory — see [`../members.md`](../members.md) for the server-side enforcement rule.

## Workflows

- **No Workflows tab since [#4073](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/4073).** It listed all nine `WORKFLOWS_SEED` switches, and seven of them were read by nothing ([#3439](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3439)). The two that are read moved onto their module's page: `wf_hours_receipt` is Service hours' **Ask for proof**, and `wf_dues_grace` is Dues' grace period. The other seven are shown nowhere; the API still stores and returns them until each is wired or dropped from the seed.
- Workflows are rows over `chapter_workflows`. Each enabled workflow may configure a numeric threshold, stored as `{ enabled, threshold }` on the workflow row.
- The **catalog** (key, label, default `enabled`, default `threshold`, `units`) is sourced from `WORKFLOWS_SEED` in `@repo/org-archetypes`; `chapter_workflows` holds only the per-chapter overrides. The merged view (catalog presentation overlaid with chapter `enabled`/`threshold`) is read via `GET /chapters/:id/config` (`workflows[]`) and written via `PATCH /chapters/:id/config` (`workflows: [{ key, enabled, threshold? }]`). No tab component writes the table directly — the config endpoint is the only path, and it audit-logs the change (mirrored to `#chapter-audit`) like the other audited settings saves.
- The PATCH validates each `key` against the catalog (an unknown key is ignored, never written) and persists only changed rows. `threshold` **guard-parses**: the input holds a text draft that takes only a nonnegative integer, so a negative or decimal keystroke preserves the previous value, while the field can be emptied mid-edit. An enabled workflow's empty threshold is refused at save, under the field, and `NaN` is never stored. The threshold input is shown only while its workflow is enabled (#3050).
- **Runtime enforcement (wired keys).** Workflow toggles are chapter policy, not just presentation. Domain services read the merged state (seed default overlaid with the chapter's `chapter_workflows` row) via `ChapterWorkflowsService`. A read failure falls back to the **seed default** and logs a warning — during an outage a chapter whose explicit setting differs from the seed is temporarily governed by the seed. Because enforcement follows the merged state, a chapter that never saved a workflow is governed by the seed defaults it sees displayed. Wired so far:
  - **`wf_hours_receipt`** — when enabled, `POST /service-entries` rejects a submission without `proof_path` (400 with an actionable message; whitespace-only proof does not count). Approval of pre-existing proof-less entries stays at the reviewer's discretion. The web submit form marks the proof field required for viewers who can read chapter config (`chapter-config:view`, i.e. officers); regular members cannot read config, get a neutral label, and rely on the server's enforcement message.
  - **`wf_dues_grace`** — when enabled, `GET /invoices/overdue` counts an OPEN invoice as overdue only once `due_date` plus the grace period has passed. The period is the workflow's merged `threshold` (Dues page; seed default 7 days), clamped to at most 365. Disabled → no grace (overdue the day after `due_date`). `chapter_dues_config.grace_days` is deliberately **not** consulted: a fallback to it would apply only until the chapter first saves the grace period (any `wf_dues_grace` save materializes the displayed threshold into the row, permanently shadowing `grace_days`), and policy that depends on save history is not policy — one authoritative knob instead (reconciling the duplicate field is tracked in the backlog).
  - The remaining catalog keys are unwired and not shown; wiring each is tracked as its own issue. Note for wiring `wf_budget_approval`: its seed threshold varies per archetype, so enforcement must use an archetype-aware default, not the flat catalog.

## Dues Tab

- Writes a singleton `chapter_dues_config` row per chapter, read via `GET /chapters/:id/config` (`dues`) and written via `PATCH /chapters/:id/config` (`dues: { … }`), which audit-logs the change (mirrored to `#chapter-audit`) like the other audited settings saves. No tab writes the table directly. An unconfigured chapter has no row yet; the config endpoint returns the table defaults.
- Cadence is one of `monthly` / `per_semester` / `per_quarter` (the stored values).
- Amounts are configured per member class: active member, new member, alumni.
- Conditional configuration: installments allowed (`installments_allowed` toggle + `installment_count`, an integer `>= 1` shown only while the toggle is on), grace period (`grace_days`), late fee (`late_fee_cents`), scholarship pool (`scholarship_pool_cents`). `grace_days` is **not yet consulted** by the overdue computation — the `wf_dues_grace` workflow threshold governs it (see § Workflows, runtime enforcement); reconciling the duplicate knob is tracked in the backlog.
- **All numeric inputs guard-parse.** A value is committed only when it parses to a finite number in range (amounts and days `>= 0`; `installment_count >= 1`). The API also caps them: the three dues amounts and the late fee at `INVOICE_AMOUNT_MAX_CENTS` (Stripe's per-charge maximum), and the rest at `INT4_MAX`, the column's own limit (#3045). Each input holds a text draft that a refused keystroke leaves as it was, while a field can be emptied mid-edit and keeps the transient `0` left by deleting a leading digit. The floors are checked at save, which refuses an empty or under-floor field by name rather than sending it; a hidden installment count (installments off) that is empty or under its floor saves as stored (#3050). Storing `NaN` is forbidden. Applies to every numeric input above.
  - **Money is typed in dollars and stored in cents** (since 2026-11-05; they were typed in cents, "85000" for $850). The three dues amounts, the late fee and the scholarship pool take a dollar amount with at most two decimals (`42.5`, `42.50` and `$42.50` all read as 4250 cents, rounded to the nearest cent so floating point never loses one) and show a stored amount as `850` or `42.50`. A third decimal, a sign or a letter is refused at the keystroke, and an emptied amount is refused at save as `<Field> needs an amount`.
  - **Counts stay whole numbers.** Grace days and installment count accept digits only; a negative or decimal keystroke keeps the previous value.
