# Tasks

A lightweight task management system for chapter operations.

## Task Lifecycle

- Admins with `tasks:manage` permission create tasks with: title, description (optional), assignee (single member, or a copy each for a group: § Tasks for everyone; or one task someone claims: § Up for grabs), due date, and optional point reward on completion.
- **Setting a due date never needs the year** (Paul, 2026-11-09, #3928). Almost every task is due in the next days or weeks, so a month and a day are enough and the year is filled in as the next time that date comes round, today included. A date further out, an annual chore set a year ahead, is still reachable on purpose, and a date picked outside the current year shows its year in the field so it can't be misread.
  - **Web** types it (`apps/web/components/tasks/due-date-field.tsx`, on the board's Add a task row and the chat composer's Task form): `Nov 14`, `11/14`, `Friday`, `tomorrow`, with a year only when one is meant (`Nov 14 2027`). `parseDateEntry` in `@repo/formatting` owns what is read. The field writes the held date out (#3937), `Today`, `Saturday`, `November 14`, `March 14, 2027`, by the rule of the shared web date field ([`components.md` § Time and date pickers](../ui/design-system/components.md#time-and-date-pickers)), so a click also opens a month to pick from.
  - **The `/task` command** reads its date with the same reader: whatever follows the `@member` is the date, or the date and then the points, so `/task "Sweep" @sam 11/14 5` and `… Nov 14 2027` work; `YYYY-MM-DD` still does ([`integrations.md`](chat/integrations.md)).
  - **Mobile's** New task sheet offers Today, Tomorrow, Friday (when it isn't one of those two) and Next week as chips, and the platform's date picker for any other day, which opens on the current year. Its field label shows the year for a date in another one. Mobile's task rows and cards still print a due date without its year ([#3420](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3420)).
- **A due date can carry a time of day** (Paul, 2026-11-10, #4003). It is optional: most chores are due by a day, and a task with no time behaves exactly as before. A time is stored as `tasks.due_at`, the exact moment, read in the creator's device zone (chapters keep no zone of their own), beside `due_date`, which stays the calendar day the creator saw so every reader that groups by day keeps working. The API refuses a `due_at` more than a zone's width away from `due_date`.
  - **Web** reads the time in the same due field, before or after the date, the way Todoist reads one: `Fri 5pm`, `11/14 9:30am`, `tomorrow noon`, `17:30`, or `5pm` alone for today (tomorrow once that time has passed). A time needs `am`/`pm`, a colon or `noon`, so a bare number is never one. The field shows it as `Friday · 5 PM`. `parseDateEntryWithTime` in `@repo/formatting` owns what is read; `/task` reads the same (`/task "Trash" @sam fri 9pm 5`). Every web task form (the board's Add a task row, a task's edit form, the chat + card's Task form, and the forms that accept a suggested task from meeting minutes or Ask) also carries the phone's quiet "Add a time" button under the due field (#4512, Shawn, 2026-11-12: a time typed into the date field went unnoticed). Pressing it gives the task a time where the phone starts (5 PM, or the next hour on a task due today after 5, and 11:59 PM from 11 PM on; today's date if none is picked yet) and shows the browser's time field beside a button that takes the time off. A time typed into the date field shows there too, and the date field writes the time in with the day. A new time already past is refused on submit with a line under the field, as on the phone; an edit keeps the task's own time even once it has passed, and accepting a suggestion keeps the time it was suggested with. `formatDueTimeLabel`, `suggestedDueTime` and `dueTimePassed` in `@repo/formatting` are shared by both apps.
  - **Mobile's** New task sheet has a quiet "Add a time" line under the due chips. Tapping it gives the task a time (5 PM, or the next hour on a task due today after 5, and 11:59 PM from 11 PM on) and shows the platform's time picker; a clear button takes it off. A time already past today is refused with a line saying so, since the task would be overdue as it was made.
  - Rows and cards name the time: "Due today at 5 PM", "Due Friday at 11:59 PM", and once it passes "Overdue by 40 minutes", "Overdue by 3 hours", then by the day. A finished task reads "Due Nov 14 at 5 PM". Within a day, timed tasks sort by time ahead of untimed ones, which are due by the end of the day.
- **A task can carry subtasks** (Paul, 2026-11-11, #4003): the steps of a chore, as a checklist inside the task, the way Apple Reminders, Todoist and Asana show a sub-task count. A step is a title and a done mark, nothing else: no assignee, due date or points of its own, since a chore with its own owner is its own task. At most 20 steps of up to 200 characters (`TASK_SUBTASKS_MAX`, `TASK_SUBTASK_TITLE_MAX_LENGTH` in `@repo/validation`), stored in `task_subtasks` in the order they were typed.
  - **Adding them.** The creator types steps when making the task: on web, under "Add subtasks" on the board's Add a task row and in the New task form; on mobile, the "Add a subtask" field under "Add a time", where Return adds the step and keeps the keyboard up. A half-typed step is sent with the task. An officer with `tasks:manage` can also add a step at the end of an unconfirmed task, or remove one, from the task's checklist: on web under the tile and in its edit form, on mobile on the task's screen (#4296, #5492). If the steps fail to save, the task is still made and the failure is logged; the officer adds them again rather than retrying into a duplicate task.
  - **Checking them off.** The assignee, or an officer with `tasks:manage`, ticks a step done or back (`PATCH /v1/tasks/:id/subtasks/:subtaskId`). The box flips at once and goes back if the server refuses. Steps don't move a task's status: finishing every step doesn't complete the task, and completing the task doesn't tick its steps, because the assignee still says when the chore is done. Once a task is confirmed its points are paid and its steps are frozen; the API refuses any change.
  - **Where they show.** Web board tiles show "2 of 5" beside the due date, which opens the checklist under the tile; web list rows and mobile rows say it after the due, with how the task repeats and its reference photos ("Due today · ↻ Weekly · 2 of 5 · 1 photo", `taskRowFacts`, on web's list rows since 2026-11-19) and the task's screen lists the steps to tick. Chat task cards show "2 of 5 subtasks" to anyone who can read the live task.
- **An officer can edit a task after it is made** (Paul, 2026-11-11, #4296): its title, description, assignee, due date and time, and points, through `PATCH /v1/tasks/:id` (`tasks:manage`). Any of them, at any status, until the task is confirmed. A confirmed task's points were paid on it as it stood, so it is a record and the API refuses the edit, and a confirm that lands while an edit is in flight wins (the write is a compare-and-set on `points_awarded = false`). An omitted field stays as it is; `null` clears the description, the time (the task is then due by the end of its day) or the points.
  - **A new assignee starts the task over.** It goes back to To do, with no completion and no send-back, since the old assignee's progress isn't the new one's, and the new assignee gets the assignment push (none when the officer assigns it to themselves). On a task already under way, web's forms say so under Assignee before Save, and the phone's task screen asks first in the platform alert as the member is picked ("Give it to Sam?", since #5514). This is also how a chapter whose only officer completed a task they assigned themselves gets out of it: nobody may confirm that one ([#1340](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/1340)), but it can be handed to someone else.
  - **A new due date or time, or a new assignee, re-arms the reminders.** The due-soon and overdue claims in `scheduled_notification_dispatches` are keyed on `due_date`, so a timed task moved within its own day would otherwise never be reminded again; the edit deletes the task's claims, and the sweeps send each reminder once more for the new due.
  - **The chat card stays as posted.** Its assignment snapshot (title, assignee, due date, points) is the record of what was assigned; its live status line reads the task, and its Start and Mark done follow the task's assignee now, not the snapshot's (`viewerIsCardAssignee` in `packages/hooks/src/task-card.ts`).
  - **Web**: an officer's tile has an Edit icon beside Delete. It turns the tile into the + card's task form, opened on the task, the way Todoist's board turns a card into its editor: Save and Cancel, with the task's live checklist in place of the steps typed for a new task. Save sends only the fields that changed.
    Changed 2026-11-18 (Paul, "the editing is terrible"): a row or tile opens the task in the docked task panel, where each field is a label and its value and a click on the value edits it in place and saves only that field, as Linear's and Asana's panels do. The panel's ⋯ menu holds Delete. The tile's Edit icon and the Save and Cancel form are gone. **Mobile**: an officer's task screen has Edit (a pencil) in its bottom bar, which opens the New task sheet's fields on the task, titled Edit task, and its checklist adds and removes steps (a sheet with Edit at the top right until #5492). Mobile's board shows only the viewer's own tasks, so on the phone an officer edits other members' tasks from their task card in chat (below), from the task screen an Activity row pushes (#5492; a completion waiting on their sign-off too since #5513, where Edit is in More beside Confirm and Send back), or from a task screen a push opens (Overdue also reaches the officer who assigned it; the Calendar opens only the viewer's own); mobile has no description field, on create or edit.
    Changed 2026-11-18 (#5514, Paul's rule for the phone's task screen: no pop-up sheets): on mobile the pencil turns the task screen into its own editor, in place, as Apple Reminders and Things 3 edit a to-do where it is. The title, due day and time (the platform's pickers), points (the New task sheet's "+" field), notes (the description, which the phone can now edit), what Mark done needs, the reference photos and who it is for become fields, and each saves itself through `PATCH /v1/tasks/:id` as it is left, as web's panel saves: a field sends only itself, and only when it changed. A group task's copy saves its title, notes, due, points and proof with `scope: "group"`, so every copy not yet confirmed changes; its Who hands over that one copy (no scope, the instance edit), and its photos, which change per copy only, are left out (§ Tasks for everyone). A field's saves go one after another, each measured against the one before it. The bar becomes one accent Done, which saves what is still being typed, waits on every save on its way and puts the screen back; a refused save is said on the screen in the API's words, with what was typed kept, and one that lands after Done is still said there until the member's next action. What doesn't save as the screen is left (offline, refused, not a number) is kept under `task-edit:<id>`, as the Edit task sheet's draft was (#4872), and the task's next edit opens on it. Not optimistic, as `useUpdateTask` isn't. A copy of a repeating task saves to that copy alone, as the phone's edit always did (web asks "This task only" or "All upcoming tasks"). (Changed 2026-11-19, #5119 step 3: the phone asks too, § Repeating tasks.) The New task sheet's edit mode is gone: a long press's Edit and a chat card's Edit push the task's screen in its edit mode, and Reassign there with Assign to's picker open.
  - **Edit wherever a task is shown** (#4512, Shawn, 2026-11-12: a task made from Activity couldn't be edited there). The task card, which is how chat shows a task on both apps and web's Activity panel shows one, offers the same Edit to a `tasks:manage` holder once its live task is read, until the task is confirmed (`canEditCardTask` in `packages/hooks/src/task-card.ts`, the board's rule). On web it is a pencil beside the card's status badge that turns the card into the board's edit form (Escape or Cancel puts it back); on mobile it is an Edit button after the card's actions that opens the task's screen in its edit mode (#5514; the board's Edit task sheet until then). Anyone else, and a confirmed task, gets no Edit. The phone's Activity opens a task's own screen instead, whose Edit is the same (#5492); a completion waiting on the viewer's sign-off too, since the screen has Confirm and Send back (#5513).
- **A task can carry photos and ask for proof** (Shawn, 2026-11-12, #4565; Paul, 2026-11-12, #4635), the way Asana and Todoist tasks carry attachments and TaskRabbit and Thumbtack ask for a photo of the finished job. See § Photos.
- Task statuses: TODO → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED. Tasks past their due date that are not COMPLETED are flagged as OVERDUE.
- **OVERDUE is derived, not stored.** `toDisplayStatus` synthesizes it on every read for a task whose stored status is TODO or IN_PROGRESS and whose `due_date` is before today (UTC), or, for a task with a time, whose `due_at` has passed (#4003). An optimistic write must therefore predict the _rendered_ status, or an overdue row visibly flips back on the next refetch.
- **Read paths carry `stored_status` alongside `status`.** Because TODO+past-due and IN_PROGRESS+past-due both render as OVERDUE with `completed_at: null`, the rendered value alone cannot tell a client which transition is legal — and the assignee transition table (`VALID_ASSIGNEE_TRANSITIONS` in `apps/api/src/application/services/task.service.ts`) is checked against the _stored_ status. So every read returns both: `status` (rendered, what to display and group by) and `stored_status` (persisted, what to compute the next action from). `POST /v1/tasks` returns the freshly created row and does **not** derive, so it carries no `stored_status`; the next read does. The field is additive — the server remains the only authority on whether a transition is accepted.

  Three rules follow for clients, and all three are load-bearing:

  1. **Badges and grouping read `status`; every action affordance reads `stored_status`.** Mixing the two on one row lets a task sit in the Overdue column offering an action its rendered status contradicts.
  2. **An optimistic write must patch _both_.** Patching only `status` leaves the affordance keyed to a stale value, so after a successful write the control does not change, the member acts again, and the second attempt is rejected — a failure reported for something that already worked.
  3. **A stored `OVERDUE` maps to the same affordance as `TODO`** (`VALID_ASSIGNEE_TRANSITIONS` accepts `OVERDUE → IN_PROGRESS`), and an _absent_ `stored_status` — a client talking to a pre-#1051 server — is ambiguous only when `status` is `OVERDUE`, since derivation can produce no other value. Offer nothing in that one case rather than guessing.

## Photos

Photos and proof (#4565, Shawn, 2026-11-12; reshaped by #4635, Paul, 2026-11-12), the way Asana and Todoist tasks carry attachments, TaskRabbit and Thumbtack ask for a photo of the finished job, and Google Classroom and Canvas set what an assignment's submission takes:

- **Reference photos.** Whoever creates or edits a task (`tasks:manage`) can attach up to **6** photos of what the job is ("this is the mess to clean"), view them, add more, remove some, or reorder them (no surface offers reordering yet). They show on the task wherever it appears to someone who can read it: the board tile and the task card in chat and Activity, on web and the phone (§ Photos' last paragraph). Sent as `reference_photo_paths` (an array) on `POST /v1/tasks`, and on `PATCH /v1/tasks/:id` as the set as it should stand: paths the task already holds stay, new ones are added, missing ones are removed, `[]` removes them all.
- **Proof kind** (`proof_kind`, Paul's decision A, 2026-11-12). The officer sets what Mark done needs, per task, on create and under More options on edit: **None** (default), **Photo** or **Link**, one segmented control. It replaced #4565's `photo_required` switch (migrated to `photo`). Changing it doesn't touch a completion already waiting on an officer.
- **Proof.** The assignee sends proof with `PATCH /v1/tasks/:id/status` and `COMPLETED`: `proof_photo_paths` (1 to 6 photos), `proof_link` (http or https only, stored as typed, shown as its domain, never fetched by the server), and optionally `completion_note`, a reply to the officer. A Photo task without a photo, or a Link task without a link, is refused with a **400** carrying the code `task.proof_required` (`TASK_PROOF_REQUIRED_CODE` in `@repo/validation`), as service hours refuse an entry without its receipt; the code is the contract a phone build matches, never the sentence. The assignee may always attach photos to a task that asks for none (the API takes them, and web's camera button beside Mark done offers it; the phone offers it only on a sent-back task so far: [#4708](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/4708)). The officer sees the proof on the waiting task, above Confirm and Send back: a row of photo thumbnails that opens a swipeable viewer, or a link chip. A completion's proof goes with the completion: taking it back, an officer sending it back, or handing the task to someone else deletes its proof photos and clears its link and reply, and the next Mark done sends its own.

**Storage reuses service-hours proof end to end** ([`service-hours.md` § Logging](service-hours.md#logging)): the private `tasks` bucket, under the server-minted prefix `chapters/{chapter_id}/tasks/{photo_id}/`. The client asks `POST /v1/tasks/photo-upload-url` for a signed upload URL per photo (any member who can see tasks: the assignee needs it for proof), uploads, then sends the returned `storage_path`s. Photos are rows of `task_photos` (`kind` `reference|proof`, `storage_path` unique, `position`), at most 6 per task and kind, which the API holds. The API refuses a path outside the active chapter's prefix, one naming no uploaded object, and one another photo already holds, so a photo belongs to one task and deleting it never takes another task's; the unique `storage_path` makes a racing second write fail too, and Mark done inserts its proof rows before it moves the task, so a lost race leaves the task as it was. A take-back or send-back reopens only the completion it read (its compare-and-set includes `completed_at`) and deletes only the proof rows it read, so it never clears a newer completion's. Photos only: kind `task_photo` in `@repo/validation` (JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, up to 25 MB each), enforced on the bucket's `allowed_mime_types` and `file_size_limit` because a signed upload URL can't pin a type. Clients check type, size and count before asking for URLs, and pick several photos at once. The upload-request and path checks are the same code service proof runs (`apps/api/src/application/services/chapter-upload.ts`).

**Reading.** Task reads and status writes carry `photos` (reference photos, then proof photos, each in order). Each photo is read through `GET /v1/tasks/:id/photos/:photoId/url`, for whoever may read the task (its assignee or a `tasks:manage` holder, and for its reference photos only, a member of the group following it, § Following a task), which returns a short-lived signed link. Photos are never public and never served across chapters.

**Deleting.** Removed reference photos, cleared proof, and every photo of a deleted task are deleted from storage after the write, best effort: the write stands if storage fails, and the object is left for the orphan sweep ([#2197](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2197)). So is a photo uploaded for a write that was then given up.

**Web** puts the reference photos and the proof control on the board's Add a task row and under More options in the task form (the chat + card's, and the edit form on the board and on cards). Mark done opens a sheet when the task asks for proof or was sent back; otherwise it marks the task done at once, with a camera button beside it to add photos anyway. **Mobile** does the same except for that camera button (#4635, which also brought #4565's photos to the phone). The New task sheet, and the task screen in its edit mode (#5514; the Edit task sheet until then), carry Photos (thumbnails with a remove ×, and Add photos, which picks several at once from the library, up to 6 in all) and the None / Photo / Link control, on the app's segmented control. New task's photos go up only when the task is created, so a sheet given up before then leaves nothing in the bucket; the task screen's go up as they are picked and save at once, as each of its fields does; one given up after a save that was refused leaves its uploads for the orphan sweep, as above (`lib/tasks/task-photos.ts`, the service-hours proof pipeline). The task screen, the board row ("2 photos" beside the due line, "Needs changes" ahead of it) and the chat task card show the reference photos and, to the assignee and officers, the officer's reason while it Needs changes, the proof (a row of thumbnails that opens a full-screen viewer to swipe through, or a chip showing the link's domain, which opens in the in-app browser) and the reply, above Confirm and Send back. Mark done (the board's box, the task screen's bar, the card's) opens a Mark done sheet when the task asks for a photo or a link or was sent back: photos or a link field, the reason, and "Reply to the officer" after a send-back. It checks what the server checks before it sends, the check web's dialog makes (`taskMarkDoneCheck` in `@repo/validation`); a direct Mark done the server still refuses (`task.proof_required` or `task.resubmit_needs_change`, because the task changed after the phone read it) opens the same sheet with the reason in it. Send back opens a sheet with the required reason (up to 200 characters, counted) and "Ask for a photo next time", hidden on a task that already asks for one. Phone builds installed before #4565 and #4635 can attach no proof and send no reason: on a task that needs proof their box reverts (a #4565 build says to mark it done on the web), and their Send back is refused for want of a reason.

## Assignee Actions

- The assignee can mark their task IN_PROGRESS (signals they are working on it) or COMPLETED (signals they are done).
- Marking a task COMPLETED does not immediately award points. An admin must **confirm** completion, except on a task that needs no sign-off (§ Admin Confirmation).
- **Marking a sent-back task done again needs something new** (#4635, decision B): fresh proof if the task asks for it, otherwise proof anyway or a short reply to the officer's reason (`completion_note`, up to 500 characters). A completion of a task with `sent_back_at` set that carries none of them is refused with a **400** and the stable code `task.resubmit_needs_change` (`TASK_RESUBMIT_NEEDS_CHANGE_CODE`). Before this, a member could tap Mark done again with nothing changed and it went straight back to the officer. Both apps ask for proof or a reply before they send (`taskMarkDoneCheck`). A phone build installed before #4635 still sends Mark done with nothing new: refused this way, its box reverts without saying why.
- **The assignee can take back their own completion until it is confirmed** (`COMPLETED → IN_PROGRESS`, clearing `completed_at`). A mis-tap or a second look shouldn't need an officer to reject it. The write is a compare-and-set on `status = COMPLETED`, the `completed_at` it read, and `points_awarded = false` (`reopenCompletion` in the task repository), so a confirm that lands first wins and the take-back is refused: a task an officer confirmed never goes back to in progress. The one exception is a completion that needed no sign-off (§ Admin Confirmation): it was signed off as it was marked done with nothing paid, stamped with its own `completed_at` (`taskSignedOffAutomatically` in `@repo/validation`), so the take-back matches `points_awarded = true` there and unconfirms it. Only the assignee: an admin moving someone else's completion back is a rejection, which notifies the assignee, and goes through the reject endpoint. The creator's "needs your confirmation" notification is not withdrawn; an officer who opens it finds the task in progress again. Each completion notifies again, so a member who toggles a task done and back repeatedly sends the creator a push each time; collapsing those is [#3654](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3654).

## Long press on the phone

(#5492, Paul, 2026-11-18: "I should have haptic touch options for tasks and events so that I can tap and hold on it and then maybe edit details, reassign, delete, et cetera. And some of those options obviously should be role gated, as well as whoever-created-the-task gated.")

A long press on a task row or card, on the board, the Calendar and Activity (where Snooze joins it on a row that waits on the member, and a task for everyone's one row offers the Calendar group row's Remove people, Duplicate and Delete for everyone; [`screens.md` § Inventory](../ui/mobile/screens.md#inventory), Activity), opens the task's actions in the phone's glass menu ([`patterns.md` § Long-press item menu](../ui/mobile/patterns.md#long-press-item-menu)). This section is the one statement of who gets which; the menu reads the task screen's own rule (`taskScreenControls`, `taskMenuKeys` in `apps/mobile/lib/tasks/task-screen.ts`), and each follows `task.controller.ts`:

- **Mark done**: the assignee or a `tasks:manage` holder, on a task not done whose status this build can move (`PATCH /v1/tasks/:id/status`). It is the board checkbox's move: Mark done's sheet first where the task asks for proof or was sent back, and Undo for the assignee alone.
- **Start**: the assignee only, on their own To do (`PATCH /v1/tasks/:id/status` to `IN_PROGRESS`, the assignee's move), as chat's task card offers it; the task screen's More has it too (#5492).
- **Mark not done**: the assignee only, until an officer confirms (an officer moving someone's completion back is Send back, § Admin Confirmation).
- **Confirm** and **Send back** (#5513): a `tasks:manage` holder. Confirm is on a completion waiting on their sign-off (`taskAwaitsReview` in `packages/hooks/src/task-review.ts`: marked done, not confirmed or paid, needing a sign-off for its points or proof, and someone else's, since the API refuses a self-confirm; `POST /v1/tasks/:id/confirm`), Send back on someone else's completion (`/reject`). They follow Mark done's place at the top. Confirm is offered only on a task that asked for no proof: the menu shows none, and a photo or link is seen on the task screen before it is confirmed, the To review row's own rule (#4635). Send back is wherever chat's task card offers it (`taskSendBackOpen` in `packages/hooks/src/task-card.ts`, the API's `isReopenable`): any completion not yet paid, or one signed off as it was marked because it needed no sign-off; on the officer's own, Mark not done does the same. It opens the sheet that takes the reason and "Ask for a photo next time". The task screen offers both: Confirm as its accent pill, Send back beside it (§ Admin Confirmation, [`screens.md` § Task](../ui/mobile/screens.md#task)).
- **Edit** and **Reassign**: `tasks:manage`, refused once confirmed (`PATCH /v1/tasks/:id`). Both push the task's screen in its edit mode (#5514), Reassign with Assign to's picker open over it. Reassign was left off a group's copy until #5514; it is on it now, handing over that one copy with the instance edit (a group edit refuses `assignee_id`).
- **Remove people**: `tasks:manage`, on a group's copy; it opens the task's screen in its select mode (§ Tasks for everyone).
- **Duplicate**: `tasks:manage` (`POST /v1/tasks`). A new task with the original's title, description, points, proof, steps and who, its day while still ahead and its time while still ahead; not its photos, nor anything that happened to it.
- **Delete**: `tasks:manage`, that copy alone; **Delete for everyone**: `tasks:manage`, on a group's copy, the only one that sends `scope=group`. Both are red, last, and ask first.
- **No creator rule.** Making a task takes `tasks:manage`, so its maker already holds it; a creator who has since lost the permission gets what any member gets.

So a member's own open task offers Mark done alone, a member's completion offers an officer Confirm (with no proof to see) and Send back first, and a task that isn't theirs nothing (no long press). A confirmed task offers an officer Duplicate and Delete, and on a group's copy Remove people and Delete for everyone too. A Calendar row standing for every copy of a group task the viewer gave offers only Remove people, Duplicate and Delete for everyone; a row for several separate tasks offers nothing.

## Repeating tasks

(#4295 step 4, Paul, 2026-11-11: "Repeat on a schedule, as event series do." Shawn picked one copy at a time on the calendar, 2026-11-15, #5119.)

- **The schedule** is the event rule format ([events.md § Event Creation](events.md#event-creation), its Recurring events bullet; `packages/validation/src/recurrence.ts`): `FREQ` daily, weekly or monthly, every 1 to 30, the weekdays or the day or nth weekday of the month, and the officer's zone (`TZID`). It ends after `COUNT` copies (2 to 120, the first included), on an `UNTIL` day, or **never**, which events don't allow (`parseTaskRepeatRule`). A copy keeps its time of day on that zone's wall clock across a DST change.
- **Each time is its own task** (a copy): its own due, status, proof, send-back, sign-off and points, confirmed through `confirm_task_completion` like any task, so points are paid per copy. A copy carries `series_id` and `occurrence_date`; reads add `series` (`recurrence_rule`, `next_due_date`, `ended`) so a client can say "Every Monday · next Nov 24". A build that knows nothing of series sees ordinary tasks.
- **One copy at a time.** Creating a task with a rule makes the series and its first copy in one transaction (`create_task_series`); the assignee gets the usual assignment push. Once the latest copy's due moment passes (its `due_at`, or the end of its day in the rule's zone), the 5-minute timed-task sweep makes the next copy, done or not (`materializeDueTaskSeries`), and pushes it the same way. Days the sweep missed while stopped are skipped rather than made at once, already overdue.
- **A missed copy stays missed**: overdue, reminded, and confirmed, sent back or deleted by an officer as any task is. It never blocks or carries into the next copy. A copy is a task, so it leaves Activity by Activity's rule like any other, and no future copy exists to fill the Archive.
- **The sweep is safe on every instance**: the write is a compare-and-set on the series' `next_due_date` under a row lock, and `tasks` holds one copy per `(series_id, occurrence_date)`. At most 200 series a run.
- **The series stops** when its rule runs out, when an officer ends it, or when its assignee leaves the chapter, which also tells the series' creator. Copies already made stay.
- **Scopes** on a copy of a live series, worded as the event editors do ("This task only" / "All upcoming tasks"):
  - `instance` (the default) edits or deletes that one copy.
  - `series` edits the template the next copies are made from and the **open copy**: the latest copy, while it is still to come (not past its due) and not marked done. A new rule counts from the latest copy's day, so its `COUNT` includes it; a new rule or time of day re-steps the next copy. A null rule ends the series after the latest copy. The open copy keeps its day: moving one copy to another day is `instance` (a `series` edit with another `due_date` is a 400), and the series keeps its schedule either way. Every check runs before anything is written. If the sweep makes a copy mid-edit, the edit is refused (409) to be retried. Done, overdue and earlier copies never change.
  - Deleting with `series` ends the series and deletes the open copy only if it is still to come and no one has started it (to do, no proof, no step checked). Done, confirmed and overdue copies stay. There is no "this and past".
- **Reference photos aren't copied**: one stored photo belongs to one task (§ Photos), so photos go on the copy they were added to. The checklist is copied, unchecked.
- **One assignee** per series. A task for everyone can't repeat yet (§ Tasks for everyone); taking turns through a group is still to define (#5119 Part 2).
- **On the web** (#5119 step 2), Repeat is the event editor's control with one more end, **Never**, where a new task starts (`repeat-draft.ts`, `noTaskRepeatDraft` / `taskRepeatRuleOf`; `apps/web/components/shared/repeat-options.tsx`). It sits under More options after Points in the task form, its pick in the summary line ("5 points · Every Monday"), and in the board's Add a task row. A task's rule is never a legacy token. Editing a copy of a live series shows its Repeat; a task that doesn't repeat has none on an edit (a series starts as a new task). Tiles say "↻ Every Monday · next Nov 24". Save on the copy still to come asks "This task only" / "All upcoming tasks" (`TASK_SERIES_SCOPE_COPY`, `taskSaveScope`), decided before any photo uploads. It doesn't ask for a move to another day (that copy only; days are compared on the device's calendar, so a time change seen from another zone isn't a move) or on an overdue or done copy (that copy only: All upcoming tasks wouldn't change it). A Repeat change saves to the series on its own; with other changes it is refused with a line to save it first, and from an overdue or done copy with a line to change it from the copy still to come. An All upcoming tasks save sends the time without the day. The chat and Activity cards' Edit does the same (`readEditableCardTask` carries `series`). Delete on a copy of a live series asks the same, with All upcoming tasks stopping the repeat. The panel's field-at-a-time saves never ask for reference photos alone, which go on the copy they were added to (`taskFieldSaveScope`, shared with the phone since step 3).
- **On the phone** (#5119 step 3), the New task sheet has a quiet **Repeat** row under Add a time, in the same frame, that opens the event sheet's Repeat control in place (`apps/mobile/components/repeat-options.tsx`, moved there from `components/events/` and given the task variant, `forTasks`: Ends adds Never, where a new task starts, and counts "times"). Closed, the row says "Every Monday" once it repeats. It is offered for one person's task wherever New task opens (the Tasks screen, the tab bar's quick actions, a Duplicate), except from a chat's + (one card) or for a suggestion (one task); once a group or Up for grabs is picked the row steps aside, with a line saying why if a repeat was already picked. A pick that can't be saved (an end date with no task after the first) says why under the row, open or closed, and holds Create. A row's status line says how often it comes after its due ("Due Monday · ↻ Weekly", `taskRepeatCadence`; a screen reader hears "repeats weekly"), and the task screen has a **Repeats** line under its facts ("Every Monday · next Nov 24"). The task screen's edit (#5514) adds Repeat under the time on a copy of a live series, saved with Done or as the screen goes (its chips change a step at a time), after the other fields (stopping the repeat first would leave their All upcoming tasks answer nothing to change), and only from the copy still to come; an overdue or done copy's says to change it from the next, and a pick held while the copy went overdue or done is dropped. Each field's save on the copy still to come asks "This task only" / "All upcoming tasks" in the platform alert, by the web panel's rule (`taskFieldSaveScope`, via `seriesScopeOf` in `lib/tasks/task-edit.ts`); Cancel keeps the draft unsaved, saves Done makes together share one question, and a draft saved as the screen goes saves to that copy alone without asking (an alert over another screen couldn't say which task). Delete, on the task screen and a row's long-press menu, asks the same with both answers red (`confirmTaskDelete`); All upcoming tasks on the task screen goes back to the board only when it takes the copy (still to come and untouched, `seriesDeleteTakesCopy`), and otherwise stays and says the repeat stopped. The alert is the one the event sheet asks (`lib/series-scope.ts`).

Picking every copy holds Remove and says to use Delete for everyone instead. Remove asks first in the platform alert ("Take 2 people out?"), since the copies can't be brought back, then leaves select mode and says "Took 2 people out of this task." ("They were already off this task." when every pick had left meanwhile; a pick that left or was confirmed on a refetch is dropped, never sent); if the copy on screen was one of them, the screen goes back to where the member came from.
- **Not yet.** A task for everyone (or a role, a group or several people) can't repeat, carry reference photos (one stored photo belongs to one task, § Photos) or post a chat card; the forms hide Repeat and Photos once Everyone is picked and say why, and the API refuses them. Proof still applies to each copy.
- **A role, a group, or several people** (Paul, 2026-11-18: a task for "everyone on a committee", or for a few people picked by hand). The same copy each goes to a narrower group:

### The Who picker

(Paul, 2026-11-18: "a select box next to each person… if it's a group, the groups appear somewhere… defaults to everyone in the chapter and then… more granular as you get further down the tree of roles".) One picker on both apps decides who a task goes to, its rules in `packages/hooks/src/task-who-picker.ts` and its value the `TaskWho` union in `@repo/validation` (one person, a copy each for a target, or someone in a target, § Up for grabs).

- **Two tabs.** People: every active member with a checkbox, the viewer first, then by name, with search. Roles and groups: the Everyone row (the chapter), then the roles under their tiers, each tier of two roles or more led by **Everyone in Exec board** (the tier picked whole, a copy each for everyone holding a role in it, `audience_tier_id`, #5473; a tier of one role is that role and gets no second row), then the chapter's groups, each a checkbox that ticks its active members, shown as all, some or none of them ticked. Search finds such a tier by its name. The web and the phone (#5613) show the same rows. A suggested task's picker offers no tier, since its payload has no tier field yet (#5614).
- **Ticks make the value.** One ticked person is a task for them. Every active member ticked is the chapter. A role, group or tier ticked alone is that role, group or tier. Any other set is those people. Ticking a role or group while the chapter is picked switches to that role or group; unticking one member of a picked role, group or chapter narrows it to the people left. A role or group with no active members can't be ticked.
- **Each or someone.** Once the value reaches more than one person, a switch picks **Each gets a copy** (§ Tasks for everyone) or **Someone takes it** (§ Up for grabs). The switch keeps its choice while the value passes through one person.
- **Alumni** aren't listed, so none can be ticked into a copy each or a posting ([`alumni.md`](alumni.md)). A single task for an alumnus, which the API still accepts, has no way in from the picker yet ([#5501](https://github.com/frapplive/Frapp/issues/5501)).

## Following a task

Option 1 of the options page: the group sees and tracks the task; sign-off stays with officers.)

- **From a group.** A task can be **from** a tier, a role or a chapter group: `followed_by` on `POST /v1/tasks` and `PATCH /v1/tasks/:id`, spelled as channel access lists spell an entry (`tier:<id>`, `role:<id>`, `group:<id>`; `TASK_FOLLOWER_COPY` and `parseTaskFollower` in `@repo/validation`). Null, the default, is a task from its creator alone. The creator, or an editor setting a new one, must be in it, so "From Exec board" is true; an edit that leaves the task's own follower as it is passes whoever sends it. One member's copy of a group task can't be given its own (a 400): it changes for the group. A repeating task can't have one yet ("A repeating task can't be from a group yet."), since its next copies are made from the series.

Not its proof or what was said about it: a follower's read has no `proof_link`, `completion_note` or `sent_back_reason` and only the reference photos, and `GET /v1/tasks/:id/photos/:photoId/url` won't sign a proof photo for them. Reads carry `followed_by` and `followed_by_name`, the tier's, role's or group's name as it is now (null once deleted).
- **On the phone** (#5613). New task (from the Tasks screen and the quick actions) and the task screen's edit mode hold a **From** row: "Just you", or a pick-one sheet of the tiers, roles and groups the officer is in, each kind under its own heading with how many are in it (`followerChoices` in `@repo/hooks`), and under it the same "Everyone in Exec board can see this task…" line. Only the officer's own: a Duplicate of a task from a group they aren't in opens on Just you. Not on a task up for grabs, a chat's + or a suggestion, whose routes carry none; a repeating task says "A repeating task can't be from a group yet." in its place. On a group's copy the edit goes with `scope: "group"`. Picking a tier whole in Assign to is § The Who picker's. - **On the web.** The add line's More and the task panel hold **From**: "Just you", then the tiers, roles and groups the officer is in, each under its own heading with how many are in it, and under it "Everyone in Exec board can see this task and how it's going. ## Up for grabs

(Paul, 2026-11-18: "someone in X", one task that a member of a committee or the chapter takes, which can offer points, and which an officer can hand to someone.)

- **One task, one taker.** A `tasks:manage` holder posts it with `POST /v1/open-tasks`: the task's own fields (title, description, due, points, proof, checklist) and who may claim it, the chapter (no scope field), a role (`role_id`), a chapter group (`group_id`), a tier's role holders (`tier_id`, #5473) or people (`user_ids`), at most one. The same rules as a copy each decide who that is (§ Tasks for everyone): active members only, the Everyone role is the chapter, the Alumni role and an empty role or group are refused. The posting is an `open_tasks` row, not a task.
- **Who hears about it.** Everyone who may claim it but the poster gets a push in the Tasks category, "Up for grabs: Sweep the porch", with the points and due date under it, in batches.
- **Up for grabs.** `GET /v1/open-tasks` lists the open postings the reader may claim now, oldest due first; an officer gets every open posting, each saying whether they may claim it themselves (`viewer_can_claim`) and how many may (`eligible_count`). Who may claim is worked out when it is read, so a member who leaves the role or becomes an alumnus can no longer claim it. Its label reads "Someone in Philanthropy", "Anyone in the chapter" or "Someone among Sam, Alex" (`openTaskScopeLabel`).
- **Claim.** `POST /v1/open-tasks/:id/claim` makes an ordinary task for the claimer, created by the poster (so the poster reviews and pays it as any task they gave), with the posting's checklist. Two claims at once: the first wins and the other gets "Someone already claimed this" (409). The poster gets a push, "Sam claimed: Sweep the porch", unless they claimed it themselves. A member who may not claim it gets a 403.
- **Hand it to someone.** An officer can give a posting to any active member, eligible or not (`POST /v1/open-tasks/:id/assign`); they get the usual assignment push.
- **Give it back.** The claimer can return it before it is finished (`POST /v1/tasks/:id/release`): their task is deleted and the posting is open again, with no push. Once it is marked done, paid, or carries a proof photo it can't go back (409). A task read carries `open_task_id` when it was taken from Up for grabs, which is when "Give it back" shows.
- **Edit and take down.** An officer edits a posting's task fields (`PATCH /v1/open-tasks/:id`) or deletes it (`DELETE`) while it is open; once claimed, it is a task and is changed as one. Who may claim it is set when it is posted: to change it, take it down and post it again.
- **Not yet.** A posting can't repeat or carry reference photos.
- A claimed posting stays as its record (`claimed_task_id`, `claimed_by`, `claimed_at`), even after its task is deleted.

## Status words

Every surface names a task's state with the same words, from `TASK_STATUS_LABELS` and `taskStatusLabel` in `packages/hooks/src/task-card.ts`: **To do**, **In progress**, **Overdue**, **Needs changes**, **Waiting on an officer** (COMPLETED, not yet confirmed), **Confirmed** (COMPLETED with `points_awarded`) and **Done**. A completion that waits on an officer is never called "Done": the member's half is done, but the points aren't theirs until an officer confirms, and mobile saying "Done" where web said "Awaiting confirmation" read as two different states (UI audit 2026-11-06, P6.1). **Done** is only a completion that needed no sign-off (no points, no proof; #4635, decision C), where nothing waits on anyone. **Needs changes** (#4635) is a task an officer sent back: stored IN_PROGRESS with `sent_back_at` set, worked out on read rather than stored (`TASK_NEEDS_CHANGES_LABEL`, `isSentBack`), shown with the officer's reason until the next Mark done. The controls use the shared verbs in `TASK_ACTION_LABELS`: Start, Mark done, Mark not done, Confirm, Send back (called Reject until #4635).

On mobile's task board only the row's checkbox changes a task; a tap elsewhere on the row pushes the task's own screen ([`screens.md` § Task](../ui/mobile/screens.md#task); a detail sheet until #5492, 2026-11-18), whose bar's Mark done is the box's move with a label. Completing offers Undo for a few seconds, and the checkbox unchecks while the completion is unconfirmed, or was signed off as it was marked done (§ Assignee Actions). Web's board offers Undo on the "Marked done" toast and Mark not done on the assignee's own waiting tile; confirmed tasks leave the Waiting on an officer column for a Confirmed list under the board, newest first, the latest few shown with the rest behind Show all. Mobile's board, being personal, keeps a waiting row until it is confirmed and a confirmed row for a week after confirmation.

## Admin Confirmation

- When a task is marked COMPLETED, its creator is notified (same "notify the creator, not every `tasks:manage` holder" rule the OVERDUE notification below uses) — unless the creator is the one who marked it complete, in which case there is nothing to tell them.
- **The review** (#4693, Paul, 2026-11-13: the old review was a full-height sheet around chat's small card, with no proof in view). A task waiting on the viewer's sign-off opens from Activity, and from its push, as a review sized to what was submitted: who handed it in and when ("Submitted today at 9:04 PM", or "Resubmitted" after a send-back), the title with its due date, "on time" or "late", and points; then the proof as big as the panel allows (one photo across it, several in two columns, each opening the full-size viewer) or the link chip; the send-back it answers on a resubmission ("You sent it back: …") and the assignee's reply; and two actions, **Send back** and **Confirm · +10 pts**, the payout named on the button. Edit and Open Tasks sit under the ⋯, so nothing competes with the decision. Send back offers one-tap reasons that fit the proof asked for ("Photo doesn't show it", "Not finished", "Wrong photo"; `sendBackReasonChips` in `packages/hooks/src/task-review.ts`), which fill the field and can be edited. Confirming or sending back opens the next task waiting on a review, longest waiting first, the way Classroom's grader moves to the next student's work (Paul's recommended pick on how several are reviewed, 2026-11-13), and closes the review after the last one; the list re-reads each time. The review says how many more wait after it, and **Skip** leaves this one waiting and opens the next (`activityReviewQueue` in `packages/hooks/src/activity-rows.ts`). The To confirm tile lists them all. The words and order are `taskReview` in the same file; web's review is its only user, and the phone shares `taskConfirmLabel` and `taskAwaitsReview` from it. **Web** has it in Activity's panel. **The phone** reviews on the task's own screen (#5513): every Activity task row, a To review row and a review push included, pushes the screen, which shows the proof above Confirm (the accent pill, "Confirm · +10 pts", the same `taskConfirmLabel`; Edit moves into More, as the panel's ⋯ holds it) and Send back (chat's Send back sheet, without the one-tap reasons yet); the officer stays on the screen after acting, as the phone's review sheet stayed on its card, and goes back to Activity for the next. Any other task in web's Activity still opens as chat's card.
- The admin reviews and confirms completion. On confirmation:
  - If a point reward is attached, a point transaction (category: MANUAL, with task ID in metadata) is created for the assignee.
  - The `confirmed_at` timestamp is set.
- **Atomicity.** Setting `confirmed_at` / `points_awarded` and inserting the point transaction happen
  in a single database transaction (the `confirm_task_completion` RPC), consistent with the
  "Atomic Point Awarding" invariant in [`points.md`](points.md): if either write fails, neither
  persists. The transaction confirms only when the task is still COMPLETED and `points_awarded` is
  `false` (a compare-and-set), so concurrent confirmations cannot award points twice — at most one
  succeeds and the rest are rejected as already awarded.
- **No self-confirmation.** A member cannot confirm a task assigned to themselves, even holding
  `tasks:manage`. Confirmation writes `point_reward` to the assignee's ledger, so a self-confirm
  would move the confirmer's own balance with no second party — the same thing `points:adjust`
  refuses, and the "No self-award" rule in [`points.md`](points.md) binds the ledger rather than
  a single endpoint. The API returns 403; the check runs before the COMPLETED-status guard, so
  the refusal does not depend on the task's state. Clients hide the Confirm control on
  the viewer's own task rather than letting it 403 (`tasks-board.tsx`, chat `task-card.tsx`).
- **Consequence in a single-admin chapter.** Where the assignee is the chapter's only
  `tasks:manage` holder, a self-assigned task with a point reward cannot be confirmed by
  anyone. Rejection only cycles it back to IN_PROGRESS. This is the accepted cost of the
  rule above: the alternative is a single-officer chapter where one member both earns and
  authorises every task award, which is the thing being prevented. The way out is the
  edit: the officer reassigns the task, which starts it over at To do for the new assignee
  (§ Task Lifecycle; resolved 2026-11-11, #4296, closing
  [#1340](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/1340)).
- **Send back needs a reason** (#4635, decision B). The admin can send the completion back (revert to IN_PROGRESS) with `POST /v1/tasks/:id/reject`, whose `comment` is required: one line, up to 200 characters, refused with a 400 when blank. It is stored as `sent_back_reason`, shown on the task to the assignee ("Sent back: Floor is still sticky") and kept beside their reply once they mark it done again, so the officer sees what they asked; a new assignee clears it. The schema still marks `comment` optional, because phone builds installed before #4635 send Reject without a body: they get the 400's sentence, and the officer sends it back from the web or an updated phone. `require_photo: true` ("Ask for a photo next time") raises the task's proof kind to Photo. The write goes through the same compare-and-set as the assignee's take-back, so a send-back racing a confirm can't leave a paid task in progress. Sending back
  is deliberately **not** subject to the rule above: it withholds points rather than awarding
  them, so there is no self-benefit to prevent.
  A send-back stamps `sent_back_at`, so Activity shows the task to its assignee as unread again
  ([`activity.md` § Unread](activity.md#unread)); the assignee's own take-back doesn't.
- **Sign-off only where it means something** (#4635, decision C). Only a task with points or required proof waits on an officer. A task with no `point_reward` and `proof_kind = none` is **Done** when its assignee marks it done: the same write sets `confirmed_at` and `points_awarded` (nothing is paid, since there are no points), so it leaves every waiting list (Activity's Needs you, the board's Waiting on an officer column, the calendar's waiting state) without any of them learning a new rule. Confirm doesn't apply to it (`POST /v1/tasks/:id/confirm` refuses it), and the creator's push says it was finished and asks nothing (`Alex Smith finished: Book the venue` / `Done · send it back if something's off`, [`notifications.md` § What a push says](notifications.md#what-a-push-says)). An officer can still send it back, and its assignee can still take it back: both reopen it from that automatic confirm (`reopenCompletion`'s `pointsAwarded`), since nothing was paid; a no-points task an officer confirmed before this shipped (its `confirmed_at` later than its `completed_at`) stays confirmed. Mark done is a compare-and-set on the status, points and proof kind it read, so an officer's edit or a second Mark done landing first refuses it rather than signing off a task that has since gained points or piling up proof. An officer's edit that leaves a waiting completion with no points and no proof signs it off the same way. Completions already waiting when this shipped with no points and no proof were signed off by the migration.

## Notifications

- Assignee is notified when a task is assigned to them, at creation or when an edit hands it to them.
- Assignee is notified 1 day before the due date if the task is not COMPLETED. A task due at a time is reminded about an hour ahead instead ("Due in 57 minutes", the time actually left), by a five-minute sweep, and flagged overdue within five minutes of its time; the daily sweep leaves timed tasks out (#5279, asked by Paul, 2026-11-17: reminding people "with like an hour left on them"; two hours ahead from #4003 until then). A task marked done, deleted or handed to someone else before then sends the old assignee nothing, since each tick reads only open tasks and their current assignee. Both claim the same `DUE_SOON` / `OVERDUE` keys, so each still goes out once. The assignment push names the time in the assignee's zone ("From Paul · due Fri, Nov 14, 5 PM"), or in the creator's with its zone label when the assignee has reported none.
- Assignee and admin are both notified when a task becomes OVERDUE. The admin notified is the task's creator — the member who assigned it — not every `tasks:manage` holder.
- A followed task's group (§ Following a task) hears both of those too, each member once and never the assignee or creator twice: the same overdue line, and when it is marked done, "Sam finished: Book the venue" / "From Exec board · An officer confirms it", which asks nothing of them. A member of the group who can confirm it (`tasks:manage`) gets the creator's review push instead (#5473). For a task for a group, the overdue line goes to the following group once for the whole group (its own claim, keyed by the group), and each copy's "finished" stacks in one pile on iOS.
- The task's creator is notified when the task is marked COMPLETED (unless they are the one who marked it, or have since left the chapter) — the same creator-not-fan-out rule as the OVERDUE notification above.
- For a task due on a day, the two due-date reminders above are sent by a daily scheduled sweep, in the assignee's own morning ([`notifications.md` § Daily Task and Invoice Reminders](notifications.md#daily-task-and-invoice-reminders)), each at most once per task: delivery is recorded in `scheduled_notification_dispatches`, so re-running a sweep, or running it on several API instances, cannot duplicate a reminder. A task that went overdue more than 7 days ago is not retro-notified.
- Assignee is notified when completion is confirmed (and points are awarded).

## Visibility

- Tasks are chapter-scoped.
- The assignee sees their own tasks.
- All admins (users with `tasks:manage`) see all tasks.
- Members without `tasks:manage` only see tasks assigned to themselves, and the tasks a tier, role or group they're in follows, without those tasks' proof (§ Following a task; changed in #5473 by Shawn's ask, 2026-11-19: before it, only their own).
- One exception: anyone who may read posted minutes sees whether each task made from its action items is done, and for a task for everyone how many copies are ("14 of 20 done"), as checkboxes on the minutes. Nothing else of the task reaches them (Paul, 2026-11-18: action items "as checkboxes that update"; [`meetings.md` § Suggested tasks](meetings.md#suggested-tasks)).

## Chat Integration

Tasks are surfaced inline in chat as a [chat integration](integrations.md) (slash command +
rich renderer + server-originated card).

- **Slash command.** `/task "<title>" @assignee <due date> [points]` (gated on the `tasks`
  module) creates and assigns a task, mirroring the existing dashboard create. It is a _heavy_
  command: the client posts an optimistic `loading` placeholder, calls `POST /v1/tasks` with the
  active `channel_id` + `client_message_id`, and the server posts the rich card after the row
  commits. `tasks:manage` is re-checked server-side and the assignee must be a chapter member.
- **Assignment card.** The posted `kind="task"` message is an immutable creation-time snapshot
  (`assigner → assignee`, title, due date, point reward). It is server-originated — a client
  cannot forge it (see `integrations.md` → _Server-originated kinds_).
- **Live status + inline actions.** The card reads the task's _live_ status back through the task
  query (so OVERDUE and lifecycle changes render without mutating the chat row) and carries the
  same lifecycle controls as the dashboard: the assignee can Start / Mark done and a
  `tasks:manage` admin can Confirm / Send back. Actions call the existing task REST endpoints
  (`PATCH /v1/tasks/:id/status`, `POST /v1/tasks/:id/confirm`, `POST /v1/tasks/:id/reject`) with
  optimistic cache updates; the server stays the trust boundary. The optimism itself lives in the
  shared hooks (`packages/hooks/src/use-tasks.ts`), not in the card — so the card, the dashboard
  board, and any later surface get one behaviour rather than a copy each. A `tasks:manage` holder
  also gets **Edit** on an unconfirmed task while Tasks is on, which opens the board's edit form
  (`PATCH /v1/tasks/:id`; § Task Lifecycle, "Edit wherever a task is shown", #4512).

Status changes are not broadcast on the chat channel, so a non-acting viewer sees a card's status
update on their next task-query refetch rather than instantly. Only the assignee and a
`tasks:manage` holder can read a task (`GET /v1/tasks/:id` refuses anyone else, as the list returns a
member only their own), so any other member's card shows the creation-time snapshot with no status
badge and no controls, and never asks for the task. Web and mobile apply the same card rules from
`packages/hooks/src/task-card.ts`.
