# Mobile Interaction Patterns

> Client-side mechanics behind the screens: screen headers, bottom sheets, keyboard avoidance, QR check-in, study sessions, dues payment, chat, push notifications, and the minimum-version check. Server-side rules stay in [`../../behavior/`](../../behavior/README.md) — this doc links, never restates.

Screen ids reference [`screens.md`](screens.md); surface rules (native-feel, Expo Go isolation, styling) are in [`README.md`](README.md).

## Screen headers

A shell screen (`components/screen-shell.tsx`) opens on its title and goes straight into its body. A screen reached from another one draws its back row (`‹ More`, `‹ Events`, `‹ Chat`), a glass capsule pinned above the title, per [`navigation.md` § Back rows](navigation.md#back-rows). There is no line under the title that says what the screen is for: the member is already on it, and the line costs a row above the fold (owner decision 2026-11-04, [#3023](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3023)). The shell's `subtitle` is for a fact about this one item that the title can't carry, and the board draws exactly one: s07 Event detail, whose line under the event's name is its time and place ("Tonight · 6:00 – 7:00 PM · Great Hall"). The app draws both there. The board puts the event's Mandatory and points chips above the title; the app puts them in the row beneath the line, because the shell draws the title first. A state screen (loading, empty, error) passes no subtitle, because its state block already says it. The title is the 24/700 headline role at -0.3 tracking, one level above the card titles on the page, as the board draws s06, s08–s14 and s16 (s07's event name is drawn at 28, off the type scale, and takes the headline too; s04 and s15 draw no page title, and the app gives them one at the same size). `screenTitleStyle` in `components/screen-shell.tsx` holds it. s13 Directory draws its own header because its body is a `FlatList`; it spreads `screenTitleStyle` and follows the same rules.

## Create actions

On the four tabs, create actions live in the tab bar's quick-actions + ([`navigation.md` § Quick actions](navigation.md#quick-actions), 2026-11-06), which took Tasks' New task and Events' New event. A pushed screen's one create action is a floating glass button at the bottom right, where the thumb is (Paul, 2026-11-05: "put it where the user's thumb is gonna be"), not a tile in the header. It is `FloatingCreateButton` (`components/floating-create-button.tsx`): a 56pt glass circle with a `+` in the chapter accent, 16pt in from the right and 14pt above the floating tab bar (or above the bottom inset on a screen without one), passed to `ScreenShell` as `createAction`, which also pads the scroll so the last row clears it. Its accessible label names the action ("Log service hours", "Upload document"). It opens the screen's creation sheet: s20 on Service hours, s21 on s12. s13 Directory's opens a screen instead, Invite members ([`navigation.md` § Inviting members](navigation.md#inviting-members), 2026-11-06), and since s13 is a `FlatList` outside `ScreenShell` it renders the button itself and pads its list by the same `CREATE_BUTTON_SIZE` plus gap. A viewer who can't create sees no button, as before. One per screen: a screen with two creation flows would need a menu, and none has two.

## Bottom sheets

Creation flows are sheets (s19 new task, s20 log service hours, s21 upload document, New event, s23 adjust points, and the s17 Ask presentation). One-tap actions never are — see [`README.md`](README.md). A reaction's one tap is toggling a chip already under the message; a new reaction starts from s05's long-press actions sheet, which is a menu of actions, not a creation flow (changed 2026-11-05, per the s05 board).

- Library: `@gorhom/bottom-sheet` **v5**. Sheets are components hosted by their parent screen, not routes. The quick actions' sheets (s19 and New event, and s20 and s21 when opened from the +) are hosted by the tab bar instead, so they open over whichever tab the member is on.
- Detents: snap to content height (`enableDynamicSizing`) for a sheet whose body is bounded. **A sheet with a scrollable body takes a fixed detent instead** — dynamic sizing plus `maxDynamicContentSize` is exactly the combination that breaks a nested scrollable (see the bullet on sheet-aware scrollables below, and the note at the second modal in `apps/mobile/components/tasks/new-task-sheet.tsx`). s17 is the worked case: its body grows without bound as answers land, so it ships the drawn cap as a fixed `["78%"]` snap point rather than a dynamic sheet that would resize under every answer. **The 78% is the cap the reference draws** (`max-height:78%` on s17, `canvas-screens.dc.html`); this bullet used to read "snap to content height; tall sheets (s17, s20) cap at roughly 78%", which taken literally prescribes the one combination that cannot work here. **A sheet holding a whole record or a roster is a tall sheet** (added 2026-11-05): `useTallSheetProps()` (full snap, dynamic sizing off, a top inset) with a sticky header, full height under the status bar, closed by the header's icon X because no scrim is left to tap ([`../design-system/components.md`](../design-system/components.md) § Surface size). s13's member sheet and s19's Assign to picker were the first two (they used fixed 65% and 70% detents that cut the profile off and squeezed the roster under the keyboard), and s05's channel member list (#3536) opened as one; the callers of `useTallSheetProps` are the current list.
- Sheet chrome uses the sheet radius and elevated surface from [`../design-system/foundations.md`](../design-system/foundations.md), with a drag handle and title row. **Which sheets carry a Cancel** is [`components.md` § Chrome weight](../design-system/components.md#chrome-weight)'s: forms do, pickers and read-only sheets draw the title alone (`SheetHeader` without `onCancel`), and the message long-press menu draws no header. A tall sheet is the exception: no scrim is left to tap, so its header carries the icon X (`TallSheetHeader`, [`components.md` § Surface size](../design-system/components.md#surface-size)) and never a word. A **trailing Cancel where the reference draws one** — s19, s20, s21, s23 do; **s17 does not**, and the reference wins: its header row is ✦ + "Ask Frapp" with a trailing caption where Cancel would sit, and the grabber and scrim are the dismissal ([`../design-system/components.md`](../design-system/components.md) §9).
- **Scrim.** A presented sheet draws a `BottomSheetBackdrop` at `rgba(0,0,0,.55)` as drawn, appearing at the first detent and gone at dismissal (`appearsOnIndex={0}` / `disappearsOnIndex={-1}`), so nothing dims a screen with no sheet over it. It is `SheetScrim` in `apps/mobile/components/sheet-scaffold.tsx`, passed as `backdropComponent`, and every sheet passes it (s17, which drew it first, since 2026-11-05). It is named "Close" for a screen reader, not gorhom's "Bottom sheet backdrop". `apps/mobile/lib/sheet-scrim.spec.ts` fails on a `BottomSheetModal` that passes no backdrop. A stacked sheet (s19's Assign to picker) carries its own, which dims the sheet beneath it (*corrected 2026-11-09: this used to say gorhom's default `switch` stacking lowers that sheet; in 5.2.14 it dismisses it, [#3790](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3790)*).
- **A sheet opened from a sheet closes back to it** (#3790, Paul 2026-11-09). Closing the sheet on top by its X, a swipe or Android back shows the sheet that opened it, as it was left; only closing the last sheet shows the screen. Every `BottomSheetModal` passes `stackBehavior={SHEET_STACK_BEHAVIOR}` (`push`, from `sheet-scaffold.tsx`), which leaves the sheet underneath presented. gorhom's default `switch` would lower it and bring it back, but in 5.2.14 it dismisses it outright, so the member landed on the page (the mechanism is at `SHEET_STACK_BEHAVIOR`). The paths today: s13's member sheet to its roles editor; s05's channel member list to a member's sheet, and on to roles; s19's Assign to picker; New event's Who's coming. The exception is the s05 long-press menu, whose emoji picker and report form pass `"replace"`: the menu is an action menu, done once an action is chosen, so closing either returns to the thread (kept on purpose, Paul 2026-11-09). `sheet-scrim.spec.ts` fails a sheet that passes neither.
- **Back and escape close every sheet.** gorhom wires neither Android back nor VoiceOver's escape, and a sheet with no Cancel (a picker, a read-only sheet, the message menu) has no other sure way out for a screen reader: activating the scrim taps its centre, which a tall sheet covers. So every `BottomSheetModal` renders `<SheetBackToClose onClose={close} />` as its first child and passes the same `close` as `onAccessibilityEscape` on its content root, where `close` is `useSheetClose(ref)`. A form closes this way like a swipe, keeping its draft. `sheet-scrim.spec.ts` fails on a sheet that skips either. The rule is [`components.md` § Chrome weight](../design-system/components.md#chrome-weight)'s.
- **A draft outlives a close, not a Cancel.** s19 New task, s20 Log service hours, s21 Upload document and New event keep their draft across a close: a swipe or a scrim tap puts the sheet away and the next visit opens on what was entered, because tapping the dimmed screen to put the keyboard away is a common habit and mustn't cost the member what they typed. Cancel gives the draft up, and so does a submit that lands. While a submit runs, Cancel is held (`SheetHeader`'s `cancelDisabled`, and an early return in the sheet's `cancel` for a tap handled in the same batch as the submit, before the held state reaches a render), in a visit reopened mid-submit too, since the sheet stays mounted on its host across a close: the write can't be called back, so a Cancel then would clear a draft that gets filed anyway, or one whose failure no visit hears of. A swipe still closes the sheet mid-submit and keeps the draft. Each host mounts a fresh sheet per chapter, so a kept draft never crosses chapters. **s15 Edit profile resets on purpose**: any close outside a save drops its edits. A typed field stays typed over a profile that moves under it (`rebaseEdits`), which is right within one visit and wrong across a close: a bio typed on Monday and swiped away would still fill the field after the member rewrote it on web on Tuesday, and Save would write Monday's over Tuesday's. What each sheet adds to this is in its [`screens.md`](screens.md) row.
- Text fields inside a sheet MUST use `BottomSheetTextInput` (plain `TextInput` breaks the sheet's keyboard coordination), with `keyboardBehavior="interactive"` on the sheet.
- `react-native-keyboard-controller` does not run in Expo Go. It lives behind an execution-environment check (isolation module per [`README.md`](README.md)).
- Sheet chrome is styled with typed `StyleSheet` token factories. **NativeWind on sheet chrome is a hard ban** (research-derived; the full ban list is §2 "De-Google guardrails" in [`../design-system/README.md`](../design-system/README.md)).
- **The shared chrome implementation is `apps/mobile/components/sheet-scaffold.tsx`** — `SheetGrabber`, `SheetHeader`, `SheetPrimaryButton`, `SheetScrim`, `SheetBackToClose` and `useSheetClose` (back and escape, above), `useSheetBackgroundStyle`, `sheetFieldStyles` (a form sheet's text field and its accent focus border), and `useSheetVisit`. Reach for it rather than hand-rolling a grabber, title/Cancel row, CTA or field. **Any sheet whose save can settle after the member closed it uses `useSheetVisit`** (search for the hook to find them): calling `dismiss()` on a gorhom modal that is already closed, or was never presented, wedges its next `present()`, so the hook tracks whether the sheet is up and which visit an action was sent from, and a late success closes only that visit. Sheets that still dismiss without it are tracked in [#3405](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3405). Same relationship `state-block.tsx` has to the state family.
- A list inside a sheet MUST use the sheet-aware scrollables (`BottomSheetFlatList`, `BottomSheetScrollView`) **as a direct child of the sheet**, with fixed snap points. Nested inside a `BottomSheetView` under `enableDynamicSizing`, the view writes its frame height and the scrollable writes its content height to the same animated value, so a long list snaps the sheet to full screen and then jumps back. A roster-sized picker therefore gets its own stacked sheet rather than expanding inline (s19).

## Keyboard avoidance

**A screen that rises with the keyboard wraps its content in `KeyboardAvoidingArea`** (`apps/mobile/lib/keyboard.tsx`), never React Native's `KeyboardAvoidingView` directly. On Android the app-wide `KeyboardProvider` stops the window from resizing for the keyboard, so a screen that relies on the resize, or switches the avoiding view off on Android, ends up with the keyboard over its input (#3346, the chat composer on the first Play build). The area uses the library's own avoiding view with `padding` on Android store builds, React Native's `padding` view on iOS, and React Native's view with no behaviour in Expo Go on Android, where the provider isn't mounted. `apps/mobile/lib/keyboard-avoiding-area.spec.tsx` pins each path, and `apps/mobile/lib/keyboard-screens.spec.ts` lists the screens that use it and fails any route that draws a `TextInput` or `OnboardingTextInput` on the screen without it (#3354). Settings and s18 check-in keep no field on the screen: Settings' accent hex is typed in a sheet (`apps/mobile/components/settings/accent-sheet.tsx`), and check-in's manual code in another (`apps/mobile/components/check-in/manual-code-sheet.tsx`), opened from the drawn "Enter code manually" link. A screen whose `ScrollView` sets `automaticallyAdjustKeyboardInsets` passes `iosInsetFromScrollView`, so iOS doesn't count the keyboard twice (sign-in). Sheets don't need it: `BottomSheetTextInput` already coordinates with the keyboard ([§ Bottom sheets](#bottom-sheets)).

## Overlays

An overlay that isn't a sheet (a menu anchored to a control, a full-screen prompt) is drawn in the app's own view tree, never as a React Native `Modal`. A `Modal` is a separate native window (a presented view controller on iOS, a dialog on Android), so it would draw over `ClientPolicyGate`'s update prompt ([§ Minimum version](#minimum-version)) and take touches the gate can't stop.

- **It is the last child of the container it covers.** That puts it on top in paint order and in hit-testing on both platforms, with no `zIndex` and no child overflowing its parent. An absolute child that overflows its parent can draw over a sibling while the sibling still takes the taps ([#2033](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2033)).
- **What it covers leaves the accessibility tree while it is up.** Wrap the covered content in one `View` with `collapsable={false}`, `accessibilityElementsHidden` and `importantForAccessibility="no-hide-descendants"`, as `ClientPolicyGate` does. `accessibilityViewIsModal` is iOS-only, so without the wrapper TalkBack reaches controls under the overlay. Without `collapsable={false}`, toggling the two props flattens and un-flattens the wrapper, moving every child between native parents.
- **One opened from a control takes a screen reader's focus when it appears.** Hiding the control that opened it clears that focus rather than moving it, so the overlay sends it to itself with `AccessibilityInfo.sendAccessibilityEvent(ref, "focus")`, from a later task than the commit that mounts it: on Android an event sent from that commit's effect arrives before the view exists and is dropped. The update gate, which appears on its own, announces its title instead. Whether that focus lands on a device, returning focus to the control on close, and whether the gate should move focus too are [#2641](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2641).
- **Android's back button closes it** while it is up, rather than popping the navigator underneath, and nothing holds the button while it is closed. (The update gate holds back without closing, because it has nothing to close to.)
- **An overlay that can close takes both from `apps/mobile/lib/overlay.ts`:** `useBackToClose(open, close)` and `useFocusOnOpen(ref, open)`. It doesn't write its own effects, so a change to either, such as #2641's, reaches every such overlay. The update gate is the exception: it can't close, so it holds back and announces with effects of its own (`components/client-policy-gate.tsx`), and a change to the shared hooks doesn't reach it.
- The worked examples are the chat thread's mute menu (`components/chat/notification-level-control.tsx`) and image viewer (`components/chat/image-viewer.tsx`), both placed and wrapped in `app/(app)/chat-thread.tsx`.

## QR check-in (s18 member scanner, s22 host display)

- **Token design:** the host screen renders a server-signed rotating token — HMAC-signed, ~30-second TTL. The server accepts the current and the immediately previous window, so a scan racing a rotation still lands. This is a signed nonce scheme, **not TOTP** — there is no shared secret on member devices to leak.
- **"Single-use" is per member, not per code.** This bullet used to read "single-use on redemption", which cannot be literally true: one host code is scanned by everyone in the room inside the same 30-second window, so a globally single-use token would admit exactly one member. What is enforced is one check-in per member, by the unique `(event_id, user_id)` index inside `check_in_event` — there is no redemption ledger over tokens, deliberately. Server rules: [`../../behavior/events.md`](../../behavior/events.md).
- **The token is verified when supplied, never required.** The chat event card checks in with no token, by design (`../../behavior/events.md` § Chat Integration). So the rotating code raises the effort of passing a code around; it is not an access control, and the geofence below is what actually gates presence.
- **Geofence is the real anti-proxy layer.** Any displayed code can be screenshotted and forwarded; the check that defeats proxy check-ins is the server's zone check on the scanner's location. The rotating code raises effort; the geofence enforces presence.
- **The zone is per event and opt-in** (`events.check_in_zone`), so s18 must only claim a location check when the event actually carries one — an unconditional "the geofence is the real check" line would be a security claim about something not running. The screen requests location only for a zoned event, and names the zone from `check_in_zone_name` ("Inside the Great Hall zone"). Foreground permission only; the "no background location" ban below is binding here too.
- **Scanner:** `expo-camera`'s `CameraView`. `onBarcodeScanned` fires repeatedly for one physical code — the client MUST latch the first read and debounce until the server responds, or one scan becomes N redemption attempts.
- **QR rendering is always dark-on-white** (s22: white card on the dark screen), even in dark UI. Scan reliability beats theme purity.
- **Fallbacks:** a short manual rotating code on the host screen (s22 "Show code") for members whose camera fails, and an officer manual override — every override is audit-logged.
- Check-in window, role gates, and the atomic attendance-plus-points award are server rules: [`../../behavior/events.md`](../../behavior/events.md) and [`../../behavior/points.md`](../../behavior/points.md).

## Study sessions (s10)

Client obligations for the geofenced study timer. The session state machine, pause/grace semantics, and expiry rules are owned by [`../../behavior/study-sessions.md`](../../behavior/study-sessions.md).

- **Foreground-only.** The app never requests background location and never the "Always" permission — While Using only. "No background location" is a binding ban, not an optimization.
- **Wall-clock timestamps.** Credited time derives from server-recorded timestamps, never an accumulated client-side timer — a clock the client owns is a clock the client can inflate. Concretely: the displayed figure is `total_foreground_minutes` plus the gap since `last_heartbeat_at`, which is the same span the next heartbeat converts into whole minutes, so the number never runs ahead of what the member will be paid for. `apps/mobile/lib/study/session.ts` owns that derivation.
- **AppState pause/resume:** backgrounding triggers the pause call; returning within the grace window (default 5 minutes, chapter-configurable per zone — s10 surfaces it as "a 5-min grace window", reading the number off the selected zone rather than hard-coding the drawn five) resumes without losing time. The client tracks the last foreground state it *reported*, and rolls that back when a pause or resume call fails, so a dropped call is retried instead of silently permitting background accrual. Exact semantics: behavior spec above.
- **Heartbeat:** every 5 minutes in the foreground, at `Location.Accuracy.Balanced` — precise enough for a building-scale polygon, cheap enough to not drain the battery.
- **Stale sessions die server-side.** A client that crashes or goes silent cannot bank time: the server voids sessions via its stale-heartbeat rule and resolves lapsed pauses on every entry point. The client never needs cleanup logic of its own — but it does need to *read* the outcome: a session ends by **returning 200**, not by throwing, so pause, resume, heartbeat and stop each have to be checked for a terminal status. A client that only inspects its `catch` block keeps a dead session ticking on screen.
- **Permission primer:** the location primer is contextual — shown on the first Start-session tap, explaining the zone check, never at app launch.

## Dues payment (s11)

- Payment runs through **Stripe PaymentSheet** confirming the existing PaymentIntent flow (`POST /v1/invoices/:id/payment-intent` returns the client secret). No new payment backend for mobile. A PaymentIntent is per invoice, so the CTA settles **one** invoice — the earliest due, or, while a payment is confirming, that invoice — and says which when more than one is open.
- **The webhook is the source of truth.** PaymentSheet success is provisional: the client shows the invoice as processing and flips it to Paid only when the server (webhook-driven) says so. Idempotency, reconciliation, and invoice states: [`../../behavior/billing.md`](../../behavior/billing.md).
- **Copy says "chapter dues", never "subscription".** Member dues and the chapter's own Frapp subscription are different billing concepts; the UI must never blur them. General copy rules: [`../design-system/writing.md`](../design-system/writing.md).
- The whole payment path sits behind a runtime `isStripeAvailable` guard (Stripe RN does not run in Expo Go — isolation module at `apps/mobile/lib/payments/stripe.ts`, per [`README.md`](README.md)). The guard folds in the publishable key (`EXPO_PUBLIC_STRIPE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`), because a member cannot tell a missing native module from a missing key and neither one lets them pay. When unavailable, the screen still renders balance and history; the pay CTA is **disabled with the reason stated**, not hidden. `stripeUnavailableReason()` returns a *different sentence per cause*: Expo Go is fixed by an installed build, the web target never takes a payment, an installed build whose module failed to load is a broken build that an update may fix (never told to install the build it already is), and a missing key is a deployment setting the member cannot change. A broken module with no key gets the key sentence, since an update can't switch payment on. The strings are in [`../design-system/writing.md`](../design-system/writing.md#dues-mobile-s11) § Dues.

## Chat (s04 list, s05 thread)

Message semantics, read receipts, unread, and mention resolution are owned by [`../../behavior/chat/README.md`](../../behavior/chat/README.md). The hot path itself is shared code — `@repo/chat-core`, which web consumes too — reached through three injected platform ports. Client rules:

- **Inject `NetworkState`. Always.** It is _optional_ on both `ManagerContext` and `ChatActionContext`, and omitting it falls back to the browser implementation, whose probe is `navigator.onLine === false`. React Native defines `navigator` but never sets `onLine`, so that evaluates `undefined === false` and the adapter reports **permanently online** — every offline gate stops queueing and sends are attempted against a dead network. The fallback is silent, so a missed injection throws nothing; `lib/chat/adapters.spec.ts` pins it.
- **Inject `KeyValueStore` too, and respect its seam.** The port is synchronous while AsyncStorage is not, so the mobile adapter serves reads from a boot-hydrated in-memory mirror and persists behind them. That is sound only for reads that may miss. The one key it hydrates is the `chat:lastSeen:<channelId>` backfill cursor, where a miss costs a re-read rather than a message: the backfill reads the newest page instead, and what that page doesn't reach is read on scroll-back ([`message-delivery.md` § Receiving Messages](../resilience/message-delivery.md#receiving-messages-realtime)). The heavy-command notice store (`packages/chat-core/src/heavy-command-notices.ts`) also reads through the port, from the shared hydrate and the realtime eviction, and that is harmless only because mobile never writes a notice: it has no slash dispatch. A mobile slash replay path (#1910) needs those keys hydrated, or a durable port, before it ships, or its Retry will not survive an app restart. A consumer needing real durability needs a different port.
- **`OutboxStore` has no default, by design.** Constructing a `ChatActionContext` without one is a compile error rather than a silent no-op, because dropping queued sends is the failure it exists to prevent.
- **Await the adapter boot before first read.** Both the mirror and the connectivity cache are synchronous ports over asynchronous platform APIs, so they need one hydrate/prime pass before the manager subscribes.
- **The viewer id is `users.id`, not the auth uid.** `chat_messages.sender_id` references `users(id)`, so it comes from `useCurrentUser()` — not the Supabase auth uid in `lib/auth-session.tsx`. The wrong id renders and sends without error but breaks own-message styling and the RLS-scoped delete behind `unreact`.
- **Wire chat from a hook, not a provider.** `app/_layout.tsx` is frozen ([`navigation.md`](navigation.md) § Hotspot freeze), so the runtime is a hook the chat screens call. `chatRealtime` is a module singleton that already refcounts per channel, so `configure` is an idempotent rebind and no screen calls `destroy()` — with no single owning provider, one screen unmounting must not tear the manager out from under another.
- **Never re-derive unread.** Counts come from `GET /v1/channels/unread`; [`../../behavior/chat/README.md`](../../behavior/chat/README.md) § Read Receipts is explicit that clients MUST NOT compute them.
- **s04's previews come from `GET /v1/channels/latest`, never from a thread's cache.** `useChannelLatestMessages()` (`@repo/hooks`) reads it under `["channels", "latest"]`, so the read receipt's `["channels"]` invalidation refreshes it with the badges, and a confirmed block or unblock re-reads it as it does the counts (`use-chat-safety.ts`); the row's wording is `channelPreviewLine` (`@repo/chat-core/channel-preview`), with names from the roster projection like the thread's ([`../../behavior/chat/README.md`](../../behavior/chat/README.md) § Channel list previews).
- **Import `@repo/chat-core` by subpath** ([its package row](../../architecture/README.md#4-shared-packages)), so a screen pulls in only what it uses. Slash commands (`/dispatch`, `/integrations`) are not wired on mobile.
- **There is no `crypto` global in React Native — never call `crypto.randomUUID()`.** No JS-level polyfill exists in the graph this app bundles: Expo's WinterCG runtime (`node_modules/expo/src/winter/runtime.native.ts`) installs `TextDecoder`, `URL`, `DOMException`, `structuredClone`, and `fetch` but _not_ `crypto`; `react-native` installs none; and neither `expo-crypto` nor `react-native-get-random-values` is a dependency. (That Hermes itself exposes no `crypto` is a documented engine property, not something this repo has executed — nothing has run on a device, #938.) `client_message_id` therefore comes from `randomClientId()` (`@repo/api-sdk`), which prefers `crypto.randomUUID`, then `crypto.getRandomValues`, then `Math.random`, so it upgrades itself automatically if `expo-crypto` is ever added. This is the class of defect neither test suite can catch: vitest runs on Node, where `crypto.randomUUID` exists, _and_ aliases `react-native` to `react-native-web`. Grepping an exported bundle is not a valid re-check — globals come from the native VM, not the bundle.
- **The thread must not use `ScreenShell`.** The shell wraps its children in a `ScrollView` (`components/screen-shell.tsx`), and a `FlatList` nested in a `ScrollView` loses windowing entirely — every message ever loaded mounts at once. s05 hand-rolls `SafeAreaView` + `FlatList` + `KeyboardAvoidingArea`; `app/(auth)/chapter-picker.tsx` is the other screen that opts out. s04 legitimately keeps the shell: a channel list is tens of rows, and the shell owns the `headerAction` slot the ✦ Ask pill needs.
- **Apply the block list before rendering, from provenance, failing closed.** The server masks what it serves, but a `postgres_changes` echo never passes through that mask, so s05 classifies every row through `useThreadBlockList` (`apps/mobile/lib/chat/use-thread-block-list.ts`, over `@repo/chat-core/blocks`, which web's timeline classifies through too) from three inputs: whether the row arrived server-evaluated (`_blockEvaluated`, set by chat-core's normalizer), the tri-state `useBlockedUserIds()` with this client's confirmed blocks and unblocks applied, and the rows this session already showed — against a `ready` list, or as server-cleared rows while it was not ready (`blockClearance` in `@repo/chat-core/blocks`). An unevaluated row from someone else renders against a `ready` list, or through a later outage if this session already showed it (including as a server-cleared REST row that an UPDATE echo has since replaced) or this client just unblocked its sender — unless it still carries the server's mask: an echo that overwrote a server-masked row keeps `sender_blocked` (chat-core's `mergeServerRow`) and stays a tombstone, whatever the list says, until this client confirms unblocking its sender; any other row that first arrives while the list is loading or unavailable is held off screen and `BlockListNotice` says so (aloud on iOS too, where live regions do not exist). Never key off the server's masking sentinel or compare `created_at` — [`../../behavior/chat/README.md`](../../behavior/chat/README.md) § The masking contract has the why. A new row renderer goes through `components/chat/thread-message-row.tsx`, which checks the tombstone before any card kind and is also where reactions (`visibleReactions`) and a reply's quoted parent are filtered, so a message or card never sees what the list hides.
- **A message's long-press reaches every inner control.** Attachment rows, reaction chips, poll options, a reply's quote and a link in the body all claim the touch, so each forwards the row's `onLongPress`; the screen-reader twin ("Message actions") sits on an accessible `View` around the message's header and text, never on a `Text`, and carries the body's links and a quote's jump as named actions too (#2775).
- **Key the list by `client_message_id`, not `id`.** It is always present and is stable across the optimistic → confirmed transition; the server id is not, so keying on it remounts every row the moment a send confirms.
- **Opening a channel marks it read.** `POST /v1/channels/{id}/read` stamps the cursor to server `now()` — there is no mark-read-to-a-message API, so this fires on channel entry, not on scroll. Its invalidation of `["channels"]` prefix-matches the `["channels", "unread"]` key, so the s04 badges refresh without a second call.
- **Drafts are AsyncStorage, not the `KeyValueStore` port.** `OutboxStore.clearDraft` removes `chat:v2:draft:<userId>:<channelId>`; the write half is `lib/chat/draft-store.ts` against the same key, derived from the one `draftKey()` rather than a matching literal. It deliberately does not ride the KV mirror, whose synchronous-read seam is sound only for the `chat:lastSeen:` cursor — which is exactly why that mirror's sweep is narrowed to that prefix and must not be widened back to `chat:`.
- **Drafts and the outbox are keyed by member, and that key is a tenant boundary** ([#2228](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2228)). `userId` is the Supabase auth uid, not `users.id`. A new consumer of either store must go through `useChatRuntime()`, never a module-level instance and never a hand-built key string — an unscoped key on a shared phone sends one member's queued message under another's token. The key carries the member and **not** the chapter: mobile's chapter comes from the optional `active_chapter_id` claim, and `sendMessage` enqueues on every send, so keying on it would turn a missing claim into a total send outage. Full argument: `lib/chat/chat-scope.ts`.
- **`ctx.toast` is undefined on mobile**, so every failure toast inside chat-core is a silent no-op. For `sendMessage` that is recoverable: a terminal 4xx also lands as `_status: "failed"` plus `_error` on the cached message, and the thread renders that inline with retry and discard. `react`, `unreact`, and `actOnCard` (inline-card actions — a poll vote today, #528) have no such cache-row fallback — the optimistic rollback carries no explanation on its own — so `ChatActionContext.onError` (#999) is the platform-neutral sink `chat-client.ts` fires alongside `toast` on a rejected reaction or card action. `use-chat-channel.ts` wires `react`/`unreact` into `reactionError` and `actOnCard` into its own `actionError`, both mirroring `sendError`'s shape but kept as separate state so one failure can't dismiss the other's message; `chat-thread.tsx` renders whichever is set as one dismissible banner above the composer, since neither has a message row of its own to attach retry/discard to. Edit and delete (#2775) are the one other shape: they are pessimistic and awaited by the caller, so `use-chat-channel.ts`'s `withFailureMessage` catches the `onError` description and rethrows it as the `Error` their `edit`/`remove` reject with, and the caller shows it (the composer hint for an edit, an alert for a delete). A further failure class reaching for this sink (RSVP, …) extends one of those — route it through `act`/`actionError` if it is a card action, through `withFailureMessage` if its caller awaits it, or add another sink only if neither shape fits; the sink exists precisely because mobile has no toast at all, not because any one action is special. The third shape is a card's own line: the event card's check-in and the task card's Start, Mark done, Confirm and Reject don't go through `actOnCard` but through the attendance and task REST hooks, which they await, so each catches its own refusal and draws it on the card as a `CardNote` (`components/chat/chat-card.tsx`), next to the control it refused (#3185). A card whose write goes through `@repo/hooks` rather than chat-core takes that shape; one that calls `actOnCard` keeps the banner.
- **A chat row draws no name and no marker in the chapter accent** (changed 2026-11-05, [foundations.md](../design-system/foundations.md) §6). The viewer's own name reads "You" in their role color or foreground like anyone's, the Pinned marker is a muted caption beside `(edited)`, and a card's eyebrow is muted. The row's accent is left to controls: the viewer's own reaction, a poll's chosen option and a card's primary button. `useChapterBranding()` reaches for `FrappClientProvider` (#1007), so only a component that draws one of those calls it: every card (`ChatCardShell` in `components/chat/chat-card.tsx`, for its primary button) and a row with attachments (`MessageAttachments`' query) need the provider whoever sent them. *(Corrected 2026-09-29: the own name was the self bubble's `accentPrimary`/`accentOnPrimary` fill until the compact layout, #2873, removed the bubble; from then until 2026-11-05 it was `--signet-accent-text`.)*
- **Resolve display names from the roster projection, never per message.** Both the s05 author labels and the s04 DM row titles go through `useMemberDisplayNames()` (`@repo/hooks`) — one cached `GET /v1/members/roster` per chapter — with the shared rule in `packages/hooks/src/display-names.ts` so web resolves identically instead of growing a second copy. Do **not** reach for `useMembers()`: it returns the full profile, and putting every member's email, bio and city on the device to draw a name is the over-fetch #986 objects to. Resolving by `sender_id` rather than joining onto the message is not a preference — `spec/behavior/chat/README.md` records why no join can reach the live delivery path. Two mobile specifics: `users.display_name` is `NOT NULL DEFAULT ''`, so treat `''` exactly like a missing id, and the id subtracted from a DM's `member_ids` is `users.id` per the viewer-id rule above.

## Push notifications

Server-side routing, preferences, and delivery are owned by [`../../behavior/notifications.md`](../../behavior/notifications.md). Client rules:

- **Android channel before permission.** Create the Android notification channel _before_ requesting permission — on Android 13+ the prompt and delivery behave correctly only when the channel already exists.
- **Onboarding asks, on its own step, never at launch.** The opt-in is a step of onboarding with its own title, `Turn on notifications`: s03's last beat for a member who joined (its only beat when the member already has a photo and a name), and, for the officer who just created a chapter, a step in chapter setup after the live moment (#3351, since that officer never sees s03). It says what a push is for here (announcements, mentions and DMs; events; tasks) before the OS asks in its own generic words, and it is never a cold launch-time OS prompt. It was a card under s03's channel list until 2026-11-07, which a member could scroll past on the way to Go to chat without ever deciding; the owner asked that members be prompted during onboarding, so it became a step. The OS prompt fires only after the member taps `Turn on notifications`. When no prompt can appear any more (iOS after an earlier "Don't Allow", Android after a second one, Android 13+ with the app's notifications blocked, or its channel switched off), the same button reads `Open Settings` and opens the phone's settings for the app (`answerPushAsk()`), because a tap that only asked would do nothing visible. That tap is stored as a `Not now`, since whether the member turned push on out there is only known on return, so the reminder below can still ask. A tap when push turned out to be on already just records the yes. Either answer leaves for chat (or the invite step). A tap on Settings' (s16) push switch counts as accepting too (it goes through `acceptPrimer()`).
- **"Not now" comes back once.** It is recorded with its time, and the chat home asks once more after three days (`shouldOfferReminder`, `PUSH_REMINDER_DELAY_MS`): one compact row under UP NEXT, `Notifications are off`, with `Turn on` (`Open Settings` when no prompt is left) and a close. The same row asks a member who was never asked (one who joined before the step existed). It never follows a `Turn on` that showed the OS prompt, whatever the OS answered, since "Don't Allow" in the system dialog is the member's answer. Answering it or closing it is final; after that, Settings' push row is the only place.
- **"On" means the phone will show a push.** Every surface reads push through `readPushState()` and `pushIsOn()` (`lib/notifications/push-state.ts`): `status`, not `granted`, plus the Android channel, so an Android 13+ phone that reports the permission granted with notifications blocked counts as off everywhere, not only in Settings.
- **The primer is offered only when push can actually be turned on** ([#2299](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2299)). When `isPushAvailable()` is false, `shouldOfferPrimer()` returns false: s03 has no notifications beat, chapter setup skips its step, and the chat home draws no reminder. That covers every build that can't push: Expo Go and web, which never load the native module; an installed build whose module failed to load; and a build with no EAS project id, which can load the module and even read permission but can never register a token. The step's only function is "Turn on", and nothing on s03 can fix any of these, so drawing it there would show a disabled CTA and an apology on the first screen after joining, the placeholder that [`../design-system/README.md`](../design-system/README.md) §5 rule 4 hides, as it hides the ✦ Ask pill. The member who wonders why finds the reason in Settings (s16), on the push row every build keeps.
- **Cold-start dedup.** A notification tap that launches the app is read via `getLastNotificationResponse()`, then cleared with `clearLastNotificationResponse()` — otherwise the same tap re-navigates on every remount of the handler. **These are the synchronous pair.** This bullet named `getLastNotificationResponseAsync` / `clearLastNotificationResponseAsync` while the epic still targeted SDK 56; SDK 57 deprecated both, and the app runs `expo-notifications@57`. Verified against the installed type declarations, not from memory.
- **Token lifecycle follows auth.** Register the push token on sign-in, re-register it when Expo rotates the token mid-session (`addPushTokenListener` via `push.ts`, which re-reads the Expo token and never calls `getDevicePushTokenAsync`), and deregister it on sign-out; a token MUST NOT outlive the session that created it. A rotation while signed out must not POST a row. After a rotated token is persisted, the superseded server row is deleted so one live row remains per device. A permission grant (onboarding's or the reminder's "Turn on", or Settings' push switch) also re-registers, because the sign-in register ran before the prompt; neither platform's token call checks permission, so this is insurance rather than the fix ([#2617](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2617)). Every register POSTs even when the stored row holds the same token: the server prunes rows the device can't see (`DeviceNotRegistered`, another account taking the token, a wipe), and the POST is idempotent.
- **Never call `getDevicePushTokenAsync` inside the token listener.** The listener fires on token changes; fetching a token from inside it can re-trigger the listener and loop. **The stronger form ships:** the function is *absent from the structural type* the app declares for the package (`lib/notifications/push-types.ts`), so the call is unwriteable rather than merely discouraged — nothing in the app can reach it, because that type is the app's only handle on the package. `push.spec.ts` pins its absence. Delivery uses Expo push tokens regardless (`Expo.isExpoPushToken` filters them server-side), so the raw APNs/FCM token has no caller here.
- Remote push does not run in Expo Go — it lives behind an isolation module ([`README.md`](README.md)); in Go, notification UI renders from in-app data only. `expo-notifications` needed the **platform split**, not a bare lazy require: it links native modules at import, so the web/SSR half (`lib/notifications/push-module.ts`) contains no specifier for the package at all and only `push-module.native.ts` requires it. ESLint bans a direct `import` of it **anywhere** in `apps/mobile` — including inside `lib/notifications/`, which is why the native half reaches it through a `require` with an inline `no-require-imports` disable — and the message names `@/lib/notifications/push` as the way in. A dynamic `import()` is banned by the same pair of rules: it is still an import.
- **Push availability depends on the build.** `isPushAvailable()` checks that the native notifications module loads and an EAS `projectId` is configured; it does not verify permission, credentials, or delivery. See [Mobile (EAS) Setup](../../../docs/ops/deployment/mobile.md#61-initial-setup) for the committed project linkage and device-verification prerequisites. Expo Go remains unavailable. `pushUnavailableReason()` returns a *different sentence per cause* — an Expo Go session cannot receive push at all, the web target never can, an installed build whose module failed to load is a broken build that an update may fix (never told to install the build it already is), a missing project id is a deployment setting the member cannot change — and a placeholder project id is never invented ([`../design-system/README.md`](../design-system/README.md) §5 rule 2, *say why, and say what fixes it*). The strings are in [`../design-system/writing.md`](../design-system/writing.md#push-availability-mobile-s16) § Push availability. Settings (s16) is its one reader.
- **Local notifications need the module but not the project id.** They never leave the device, so the study-pause notice (#1065, [`../../behavior/study-sessions.md`](../../behavior/study-sessions.md) § Anti-Distraction) can work independently of remote-push setup in an installed build. It is posted only after `/pause` returns, cleared on resume and on session end, and every call is best-effort — a notification that cannot be posted must never fail the pause that triggered it.
- **Taps resolve through one table.** `lib/notifications/targets.ts` maps `data.target.screen` to a route, and s14's in-app rows resolve their `href` through the same module, so an in-app tap and a push tap cannot name different screens. The vocabulary is the server's ([`../../behavior/notifications.md`](../../behavior/notifications.md) lists the screens; `chat_reports` is deliberately unrouted, since the report queue is web-only); anything unrecognized — or missing its required param — falls back to `/notifications`, which always renders. A cold-start target is **held pending** until the member is authenticated with a chapter, and dropped if they sign out first.
- **`app.json` config, and what is deliberately absent.** The `expo-notifications` plugin sets `color` (`#EFB63B`, house gold) and `defaultChannel` (`"default"`, the one Android channel this app defines) and **no `icon`**: no notification icon asset exists, and pointing the plugin at a wrong asset would be inventing configuration. `UIBackgroundModes` is deliberately not set — that flag is for silent/background pushes, which this app does not send.
- **Implementation map.** `lib/notifications/push.ts` (isolation module and availability), `push-module.ts` / `push-module.native.ts` (the split), `push-types.ts` (structural type), `targets.ts` (payload → route), `push-registration.ts` (the registered row, stored because deregistration is `DELETE /v1/push-tokens/{id}` by **row id** — there is no delete-by-token and no list route, so the id returned at registration is the only handle on the row), `push-token-lifecycle.ts` (register / rotate / deregister without React), `push-state.ts` (the phone's answer, read one way everywhere), `primer.ts` (the rules and the stored answers) + `use-push-primer.ts` (`usePushPrimer` for the onboarding step, `usePushReminder` for the chat home's row) + `components/notifications/push-ask.tsx` (the step's body and answers, hosted by s03's `(auth)/welcome.tsx` and chapter setup's `(auth)/create-chapter.tsx`) and `push-reminder.tsx` (the chat home's row), `study-pause.ts` (#1065), and `use-push-runtime.ts`, mounted once by `components/app-runtime.tsx` above the auth gate because tab screens mount lazily and a runtime on a screen would miss the cold-start read.

## Minimum version

The one lever the server keeps over builds already in the field (#2526, ADR-24 decision 8). Every store install stays as shipped until its owner updates from the store, so a build the API can no longer serve has to be told so, by a check that was in the binary from the start.

- **Every request names the build.** Native builds send `X-Client-Version: <ios|android>/<version>+<build>` on every API call (`lib/client-version.ts`, through `createFrappClient`'s `clientVersion`): the store version and the native build number, the same fields as the Sentry release. Web and Expo Go send nothing, since Expo Go's native version is its own, so a minimum meant for store builds can never lock out a development session.
- **The server decides.** `GET /v1/client-policy` (no auth, because a build too old to sign in is the one that most needs telling) compares that header with the deployment's minimum for the platform and answers `update_required` and `update_url`. The comparison, the minimum and the link all live on the server, so each can change after a binary ships. Those two response fields are read by binaries that can't be updated over the air: add fields, never repurpose one. Setting a minimum: [`ENV_REFERENCE.md`](../../../docs/internal/environment/ENV_REFERENCE.md) § API-Only Settings.
- **It fails open.** `lib/client-policy.ts` asks at launch and again on each return to the foreground once its last answer is stale (`CLIENT_POLICY_STALE_MS`). Only a 2xx whose body says `update_required: true` blocks. A network error, any non-2xx (an empty-bodied 5xx from the CDN included), a timeout, an offline device or a body in any other shape isn't an answer, so it never blocks, and it never lifts a block an earlier answer set either. Only an answer of `update_required: false` lifts it. The answer lives in its own `QueryClient`, not the product cache, because that cache is wiped on sign-out, an account swap and a chapter switch, and an auth-js `SIGNED_OUT` gets there with no tap. An `update_url` that isn't https is dropped rather than handed to the OS.
- **The block is an overlay, not a route.** `ClientPolicyGate` (`components/client-policy-gate.tsx`) wraps the app in `app/_layout.tsx`, just outside `BottomSheetModalProvider` so no open sheet draws over it. That covers everything drawn in the app's own view tree and nothing outside it, which is why an in-app overlay is never a React Native `Modal` ([§ Overlays](#overlays)). A route could be left by a deep link or a push tap. While it blocks, the app underneath stays mounted (push and auth keep their state) but takes no touches and is hidden from screen readers. Android's back button is held, and an open keyboard is dismissed. Its one action hands `update_url` to the OS with `Linking.openURL`, so a store link opens the store app, not an in-app browser. Copy: [`writing.md`](../design-system/writing.md) § Update required.
