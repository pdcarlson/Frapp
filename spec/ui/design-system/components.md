# Components

> Concrete component specs — variants, sizes, radii, token roles, and interaction states — derived from the committed reference board ([signet-design-system.dc.html](reference/signet-design-system.dc.html), panels 4d, 4e, 4f, and the sheet in 4g) and the locked chat/Ask screens of [canvas-screens.dc.html](reference/canvas-screens.dc.html). Tokens, the radius map, and elevation rules live in [foundations.md](foundations.md); the per-chapter accent scale and its role mapping in [accent-engine.md](accent-engine.md).

---

## 1. Reading the reference

The reference board is the committed visual truth, with two locked corrections:

- **Radii.** Panels 4d/4h draw controls at radius 14 and panel 4e draws chat bubbles at radius 20 — drawings that predate the lock. Where a drawing and the canonical map differ, **the map wins**; the map and its deviation note are owned by [foundations.md](foundations.md) §8.
- **Type sizes and spacing, same rule.** The tables below quote sizes measured off the board — 15 and 14.5 for button labels (§3), 16.5 for card and state titles (§8, §10), a 22px tab gap and 11px tab padding (§6). The board draws seventeen distinct font sizes; [foundations.md](foundations.md) §7 locks six and says outright that an off-scale size is a defect, and §9 locks a 4px spacing grid. **The maps win here too**, exactly as they do for radius: an implementation rounds a drawn measurement onto the adjacent scale step (15 → the `label` role's 14, 16.5 → `body` 16 at the weight the drawing distinguishes by, 22 → 24, 11 → 12) rather than reproducing it. Only the weights and the relationships between sizes are transcribed literally.
- **Text tones that miss the contrast gate get lifted one step.** §5 already grants this for badge text on a tint ("implementations MAY lift the text tone for AA contrast, but the hue is the fixed semantic"), and the reference itself does it (`#4BC262`, `#4C93F8`). It generalises: where a *drawn* tone measures below the 4.5:1 text floor [README.md](README.md) §6 sets as a release gate, the implementation moves it one step up the [foundations.md](foundations.md) §4 text ladder — or, for a semantic, to a lifted tone of the same hue — and the surrounding relationships move with it. Measured cases the web implementation carries: `--muted` on the surface ladder was 3.3–4.0:1, so tab labels and input placeholders took `--muted-foreground` (the hover lift moves up to `--foreground` with them), and they keep it; since the 2026-11-09 text ladder ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §4) `--muted` itself clears 4.5:1 on every step; `#f85149` on its own 13% tint is 4.22:1 over card and 3.82:1 over the elevated step, so danger text on a danger tint takes the lifted `#FF7B72`. Fills, borders and the hue itself are unchanged — only text moves.

The `--muted` case generalised further than "placeholders and tab labels": it measured **4.04:1 on `--background`** and lower on every step above it, so it did not clear the gate as text *anywhere* on the ladder. The chat slice therefore lifted every caption it owns — the author line's time, delivery state, rail section headings — to `--muted-foreground`. *(Corrected 2026-09-29: this used to say the sender's name and the time share one tone and differ by weight, as s05 draws. The compact layout ([#2873](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2873), §11) sets the name in `--foreground` at 600 and the time in `--muted-foreground`, two tones, so the name reads first on a row with no bubble to frame it.)* *(Corrected 2026-11-09: the Signet pass this paragraph waited for happened. The text ladder ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §4, [#3826](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3826)) puts `--muted` at 6.2–7.4:1 on every step, so it is a text tone again, the quietest one; the captions above keep `--muted-foreground` for their hierarchy. `--muted` is never a fill: at `#9E9E9E` a `bg-muted` slab puts foreground text at 2.46:1, so a quiet fill takes a ladder step or `--card-hover`.)*

**One pair cannot be lifted, and is a recorded exception rather than a defect.** Mention/DM white-on-`#E5484D` measures **3.91:1**. The text is already white, so §1's remedy is unavailable, and the fill is fixed and semantic — [foundations.md](foundations.md) §5 forbids replacing it, s04 draws it, and mobile ships it, so a web-only hue change would break the one guarantee the token exists for. It ships as drawn, is pinned to its measured value by `apps/web/components/chat/chat-contrast.spec.ts`, and is tracked for a system-level fix in #1190.
- **Tab bar.** Panel 4g's 5-tab bar (with a Home tab) is stale. Four tabs — Chat, Events, Tasks, More — are locked; see [navigation.md](../mobile/navigation.md). Panel 4g's *sheet* drawing remains authoritative and is specced in §9.

Colors below are given by **token role** (`accent-N` per [accent-engine.md](accent-engine.md)). The only literal hexes are the fixed neutral and semantic values, which never vary by tenant, and the role color palette (`ROLE_COLOR_PALETTE` in `packages/validation/src/role-colors.ts`, #3485), a fixed set a chapter picks from per role. The chat message row and the AI sourced-answer card (panel 4e) are the two signature surfaces; they are specced in §11. (Panel 4e and s05 draw chat as bubbles; the owner replaced those with a compact, bubble-free layout on 2026-09-29, recorded in §11, which overrides the drawings for chat.)

- **Transcribe the reference, never lift it.** The board is a *picture* of the design, not an implementation: read its source text — do not render it in a browser or screenshot it — and rebuild what it draws in the target stack. The `x-dc` wrapper and the `dv-*` viewer chrome (`dv-turn`, `dv-thd`, `dv-card`) are export scaffolding, and their geometry is board layout rather than component spec — the `dv-card` panels are pinned to 440px/940px on a `#131211` ground, and panel content is inline-hex styled throughout. Specs are transcribed by token role; the export's own markup and inline values MUST NOT be copied into product code.
- **The exports are not runnable.** Both files load `./support.js`, and `canvas-screens.dc.html` mounts each of its 23 screens through an `x-import` element sourced `from="./ios-frame.jsx"`; neither support file is committed to the repo. Opened in a browser the custom elements never upgrade, so the iOS device frames and panel 4e's accent-seed / bubble-radius controls do not render and the inline-styled screen markup paints unframed. The committed exports are authoritative as **source text**; a browser render is partial, so specs are transcribed from the markup and never from a rendered screenshot.

## 2. Shared rules

- **Every control height in this file is a step of [foundations.md](foundations.md) §9's control size scale**, which owns the numbers; a section names its step. *(Added 2026-11-05.)* The scale's Small step (36) is not §3's Compact button (34, web app bar only).

- **Touch targets MUST be ≥ 44px** on touch surfaces. Compact 34px controls (§7) are web/pointer-only.
- **No drop shadows.** Elevation is a lighter surface fill ([foundations.md](foundations.md)).
- **No pill shapes** except the toggle track (§4) and the proportion meter (§12). The "Ask pill" is a rounded rectangle, not a capsule.
- **Borders are neutral hairlines or tokens** — `rgba(255,255,255,.08)` structural, `rgba(255,255,255,.14)` on inputs, `accent-7` on accent-tinted chrome, low-opacity semantic on status surfaces (§10). Never a fixed grey hex.
- **Focus (keyboard/input):** border swaps to `accent-11` plus a 3px ring of `accent-8` at ~25% opacity. Applies to every focusable control.
  - **The border swap is the half that carries it.** `accent-8` at 25% composites to 1.18–1.31:1 against the step it sits on — 0 of the 19 seeds × 4 steps clear the 3:1 non-text floor [README.md](README.md) §6 sets — so the ring is the halo and the solid border is the signal. A control that drops the border swap has no focus indicator, whatever the ring looks like.
    - **The border is `accent-11`, not `accent-9` (owner decision 2026-09-18, shipped 2026-09-30, [#2398](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2398)).** `accent-11` against the step behind it is 6.81:1 at the worst of the 19 seeds × 4 steps. `accent-9`, which the framework board draws ("gold border"), was the token until then. It failed 3:1 on 9 of 19 seeds over `--popover` until the engine began holding it to the floor ([accent-engine.md](accent-engine.md) §8, [#2541](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2541)), and even then it had no headroom (3.78:1 at the worst). It also drew nothing on a primary button: the fill is `accent-9`, so a border swapped to `accent-9` was the fill's own colour.
    - **A filled control over a transparent border clips its fill to the padding box** (`bg-clip-padding`, on the primary button). By default the fill paints under the border, so focus would only move that 1px from `accent-9` to `accent-11`, 1.09–2.07:1 across the seeds. Clipped, the border shows the surface at rest and `accent-11` on focus. Pinned by `apps/web/components/ui/focus-contrast.spec.ts`, across all three bordered recipes (`FOCUS_RING`, `FOCUS_RING_ALWAYS`, `FOCUS_RING_WITHIN`).
    - **The swap needs a border to swap.** A control with no border class gets the 25% ring alone, which is no indicator. Reserve one (`border border-transparent`), as the button does. [#2965](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2965) tracks the controls that still don't, including the shell chrome's ring-only `FOCUS_RING_SHELL`.
  - **Where the border already means something, move the accent into an offset ring instead.** The toggle's border carries on/off (§4) and a tab's bottom border *is* the selected indicator (§6); repainting either on focus loses the state, or — worse, on tabs — draws the exact visual that means "selected" onto a merely-focused item. Those controls keep their border and take a 2px **`accent-11`** ring, at full opacity, offset from the control by a 2px band of `--background`.
  - **Both recipes draw their signal in `accent-11`, and they differ in which half carries it.** The bordered recipe dilutes its ring to 25% because the solid `accent-11` border is the signal. The offset recipe has no border to swap — that is why a control lands on it — so its ring is the *entire* indicator and must clear the 3:1 floor unaided. Measured against `--background` across all 19 seeded chapter accents:

    | Step | Worst seed | Clears 3:1 |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `accent-9` | 4.70:1 (`#800000`) | yes, but with none of `accent-11`'s headroom: the engine's floor guarantees 3:1 and no more, and the worst, `#800000`'s generated `#F42F22`, is a fill it leaves alone, at 3.78:1 on `--popover` |
    | `accent-8` | 2.98:1 (`#000000`) | no — fails on 3 (`#000000` / `#C0C0C0` / `#FFFFFF`, which all derive the same ring) |
    | `accent-11` | 8.48:1 (`#BF0A30`) | yes — all 19 |

    Before [#2541](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2541)'s fill floor, `accent-9` failed on 4 seeds (worst 1.87:1, `#8B0000`) and `accent-8` on 4 (`#4B0082` too). `accent-8` held this role until the greenfield ladder ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §2) lifted `--background` to `#131211`; its margin was 3.05:1 against a 3.0 floor and the lighter base consumed it. `accent-11` is the engine's text role, gated at 4.5:1 as text ([accent-engine.md](accent-engine.md) §8), so it has real headroom as non-text UI. Pinned by `apps/web/components/ui/focus-contrast.spec.ts`.
  - **The offset band fixes the surface the measurement assumes.** The ring's inner edge abuts a band of `--background`, which is what the table above measures against; its outer edge abuts whatever the control sits on. While the ring drew in `accent-8` this was load-bearing for conformance — that step fails 3:1 on every deeper rung (worst 2.77 on `--surface-1`, 2.64 on `--card`, 2.39 on `--popover`). On `accent-11` it no longer is: the ring clears 3:1 on every rung by itself (worst 7.89 / 7.52 / 6.81). Keep the offset anyway — it fixes one comparison surface instead of letting conformance vary by host, and keeps the ring off the control — but the margin is what makes the recipe robust, not a budget to spend.
> **The hexes in this document are transcriptions, not the source.** Every ladder and text value here is owned by [foundations.md](foundations.md) §§2–5; they appear inline because the component recipes were written that way. When a token value moves, this file has to move with it — the greenfield ladder ([#2143](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2143)) is the case in point. Prefer naming the token over pasting its value in anything new here.

- **Semantic colors are status-only** (success `#3fb950`, warning `#e5a000`, danger `#f85149`, info `#2f81f7`) — never decorative.
- **No component references the chapter hex.** Every accent role resolves through the generated scale ([accent-engine.md](accent-engine.md) §1; §8 says which roles are contrast-gated).
- Undrawn primitives (select, radio, dropdown menu, popover) MUST compose from the same tokens: elevated `#2A2621` fill, hairline border, radius 12, `accent-8` ring. A row inside a menu or select is the Row step, 32px with a 14px label to a fine pointer, and 44px with a 16px label to a coarse one (added 2026-11-05: at 44/16 everywhere, a two-item chapter menu stood taller than the sidebar block it opens from, which the owner called out).
- **A select is the app's own, never the browser's** (added 2026-11-06, owner's call on the Discord import wizard: native dropdowns "should feel part of our UI"). Web composes `components/ui/select.tsx` (Radix), never a native `<select>`, wherever the list is the app's own; the last native ones moved in [#3827](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3827) (2026-11-11), and `apps/web` holds none. Options of two kinds split with `SelectSeparator` and a `SelectLabel` heading over the second group, as the import's role picker puts New role and Skip above a "Your roles" group. A select that sets many things at once shows their shared value, or "Mixed" when they differ, never a placeholder it snaps back to: Radix reports only a change, so a control that reset after each pick hid that the pick applied, and re-picking the shown value did nothing. An action picker over a selection (the import's "Map to…" on ticked rows) is the exception: the pick acts on the rows and clears the selection, so the control leaves with it. Keep a Radix select controlled, `""` for its placeholder: given `undefined` it falls back to its last pick. Inside a dense list row or a toolbar it takes `dashboardToolbarHeightClassName` (§3).
- **A hairline's alpha is not a free parameter.** `--border` is `rgba(255,255,255,.08)` and a diluted `border-border/70` composites to 1.169:1 on `--card` against the token's 1.253:1. Neither clears §6's 3:1 non-text floor and at this ladder neither can, which is exactly why the remaining margin is not available to spend: once elevation is ~1.12:1 the hairline is the only edge there is. Five Chapter Ops row lists had invented that value; [README.md](README.md) §3 rule 4 already bans one-offing a token value at the call site, and this is what it costs.
- **The chat thread is `--background` (or a chat background measured AA against it, #3973), and a card in it is `--card`.** The web dashboard's chat panes are `--background` for the thread and `--surface-1` for the rails, and a hovered message row fills `--surface-1` (§11). A card posted into the thread (poll, task, event) is `--card` with a hairline, and there **the hairline is load-bearing**: one ladder step is ~1.12:1 and the composited hairline reaches ~1.4:1, both under the 3:1 non-text floor, so a card that drops its border is delineated by 1.12:1 and effectively has no edge. *(Corrected 2026-09-29: this rule was written for the incoming chat bubble, which painted `--card` with a hairline, so wrapping the thread in a card made the bubble `#211E1A` on `#211E1A`. The compact layout ([#2873](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2873)) removed the bubble; the reasoning carries over to the cards.)*
- **A highlighted row inside one of those primitives takes `--card-hover`, never a surface step and never the accent tint** (changed 2026-11-05, owner's call; [foundations.md](foundations.md) §6, "Where the accent goes"). The elevated fill above is the *top* of the surface ladder ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §2), so there is no ladder step above it to highlight with. The ShadCN scaffold's `--accent` held that same `#2A2621`, so a menu that highlighted with it painted the hovered row in its own background. It and `--secondary` (an alias of `--card`) are deleted ([#3036](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3036)), along with their `-foreground` pairs and the Tailwind `secondary` and `accent` colour keys. [`apps/web/components/shared/elevation-call-sites.spec.ts`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/elevation-call-sites.spec.ts) still bans both names across the two Next apps, because with the variables gone `hover:bg-accent` compiles to nothing, which is the silent no-colour failure of [#1145](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/1145). It scans each spelling its fixtures list (utilities, `var()` reads, Tailwind shorthands, config keys, `theme()`). A rest fill names its ladder step instead, and a hover takes a state above it. `--card-hover` is that state: it sits above every ladder step (1.199:1 on `--popover`, 1.324:1 on `--card`, §3), so hover and keyboard highlight inside menus, command palettes, selects, tables and lists all take it, with `--foreground` text. Until 2026-11-05 they took the §5 accent-tint recipe (`accent-3` fill, `accent-11` text), which separated by hue rather than luminance and lit every hovered row in the chapter color.
  - **A selected row is not told apart by its fill.** No fill on this ladder clears the 3:1 non-text floor, the accent tint included (measured 2026-10-01, it sat 1.008–1.107:1 from `--card` across the seeded chapters, straddling the neutral step's 1.105:1 with 17 of 19 seeds at or below it), so a state that carries *information* rather than pointer feedback was always redundant with something that is not a fill. A selected table or list row takes `--popover`, one step under the hover so a button inside it still lifts; its checked checkbox (accent, a control the member switched on) and the "N selected" bar above the table (`--popover`, `--foreground` text) carry the selection. The neutral fill's contrast and the call sites that use it are pinned in [`apps/web/components/shared/table-contrast.spec.ts`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/table-contrast.spec.ts). The open item of a list-and-detail rail (the folder you are in, the role you are editing) is different: it is where the member is, so it keeps `accent-4` plus `accent-11` text ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §6, item 6), and its neighbours hover neutral.

### Chrome weight

Owner rule, 2026-11-05: chrome is as light as the job allows, on web and mobile alike. Anything that frames, labels or closes content has to earn its place, and when the content, a gesture or a lighter control already does the job, the heavier one goes. It came out of the chat image viewer rebuild ([#3506](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/pull/3506)) and an app-wide audit of the same pattern.

- **Media draws bare.** An image in a chat message draws at its own shape with the control radius (12) and no frame, padding or hover fill, so the thread shows the picture, not a box holding one. Both platforms cap a preview at 256 tall (web's `max-h-64`) and keep the photo's shape under it: on mobile the row takes the column's width at the photo's aspect ratio, narrowed to the width at which it reaches the cap, so a portrait, square or 4:3 photo never sits in a full-width box with empty sides. A photo with no stored size (sent before sizes were recorded) holds a fixed-height box until it loads, then takes the same box from the image's own size, so it sits at the start of the column under its text, never centred (2026-11-07). The loading tile it replaces is bare too. The framed row (hairline, `--surface-1`, filename and size) is for files that aren't images. Web: `apps/web/components/chat/message-attachments.tsx`; mobile: `apps/mobile/components/chat/message-attachments.tsx`.
- **Chat photos** (owner decision 2026-11-07, before and after: <https://claude.ai/artifact/YYsb51eUiKDcQKXKEsaZdC>). A staged photo is a picture, never a filename and size: mobile's composer draws each as a 64pt square thumbnail from the file on the phone, with a dark × disc on its corner, in a tray above the input. It shows the moment the picker closes; the upload runs behind it, so an uploading thumbnail is faded in place (the pending rule, §10), a failed one is dimmed under a try-again glyph that retries on tap, and Send never waits for the tray: the message goes up at once with the sender's own photos and its time in gray until the server confirms. A photo that fails after Send turns the message into a failed row in its channel, the reason under it, with Retry (uploads the failed photos again, then sends) and Discard; the composer is never touched, so whatever the member typed since stays. Two or more photos in a message draw as one grid, two across in squares a 2pt gap apart (three: the third spans the row), the fourth tile carrying "+N" over a dark scrim when there are more; one photo keeps its own shape as above. Web's composer draws the same 64px thumbnail for a staged image (other files keep the named chip), takes several files in one pick, at most 10 a message on both apps, and fades each until its upload lands; there Send waits for the tray instead, since a desktop upload is a second or two and the web outbox has no pending-upload row yet. A received photo (2026-11-07, both apps) takes its own shape at once from its stored size, filled with a soft blur of its colours (its ThumbHash, stretched to a lone photo's box, cropped like the photo in a grid tile) until the photo paints over it: a 1280 px copy for a JPEG larger than that, the photo itself otherwise; the full-size original is what the viewer opens, drawn over the preview already on screen. On mobile both are cached on disk per photo, so a photo seen once draws at once on every later visit. A photo sent before then has no size or blur and draws as it always did ([`chat/README.md` § Photo previews](../../behavior/chat/README.md)). Mobile: `components/chat/chat-composer.tsx`, `components/chat/message-attachments.tsx`, `lib/chat/use-chat-channel.ts`, `lib/chat/photo-preview.ts`. Web: `components/chat/composer.tsx`, `components/chat/message-attachments.tsx`, `lib/chat/photo-preview.ts`.
- **An action menu has no title and no Cancel.** The mobile long-press sheet opens on the line that names the message (sender and time), then the quick reactions and the action rows. The grabber, the scrim, Android back and VoiceOver's escape gesture close it. gorhom wires neither of the last two, so every mobile sheet renders `SheetBackToClose` as its first child and passes `useSheetClose` as its content's `onAccessibilityEscape` ([`sheet-scaffold.tsx`](../../../apps/mobile/components/sheet-scaffold.tsx)); [`lib/sheet-scrim.spec.ts`](../../../apps/mobile/lib/sheet-scrim.spec.ts) fails the build on a sheet without the first. A screen reader can't rely on the scrim: gorhom closes it on a touch gesture, and activating it taps its centre, which a tall sheet covers. The same holds for an anchored menu like the mute menu: a quiet sentence-case caption may name it, with no rule under it and no Cancel row.
- **A sheet's header follows what the sheet is.** A **form** (a sheet with a commit button: New task, Log service hours, Upload document, Edit profile, Report message, Chapter accent, Enter code) keeps its title and Cancel, because Cancel gives the draft up and a swipe keeps it (§9, [patterns.md](../mobile/patterns.md) § Bottom sheets). A **picker or a read-only sheet** (Study zone, Blocked members) draws its title alone: the pick, the grabber, the scrim, Android back and VoiceOver's escape close it, so a Cancel or Done there is a second close button. A **tall sheet** (Assign to, a member's profile, a channel's Members) leaves no scrim to tap, so its header carries the icon X and never a word (§ Surface size); the member sheet, headed by the member's name, draws the X with, on another member's profile, the `⋯` before it (§ Shared rules, 2026-11-11). A sheet never draws a header Done and a primary Done at once.
- **A chevron promises somewhere to go.** A list row that opens another screen or sheet draws `›`. A row that acts in place (Reply, Edit, Delete, Block, Unblock, Sign out, Delete account) draws none (`ListRow`'s `action`). An action menu's rows draw none either, even Report message, which opens its form: a menu row is a verb, as in Discord's long-press.
- **A control says what it does in its label, and nothing under it explains it** (owner, 2026-11-07: "you don't need to explain how each button works, in the button"). No second line under a menu row, a toggle or a button saying what it will do ("Hides their messages from you…", "Your chapter's officers will be able to see it.", "Notifications arrive silently."). A line under a row is for state the member can't see otherwise: a value, a failure, why a row is unavailable, or progress. What a heavy action does goes in its confirmation, where the member is deciding.
- **A list inside a drawer or popover is flat.** Rows divided by hairlines, transparent on the surface, lifting to `--card-hover` on hover; no bordered card per row. Unread is a dot in `--foreground` plus a bolder title, never a tinted card or a Read/New badge. A row may lead with a picture: the notifications list draws who a notice is about with a small kind badge on its corner (#4346), and a mention of the viewer is that badge in the mention chip's fixed gold, not a tinted row (Shawn's pick, 2026-11-11, when the Discord-style whole-row tint was offered; [foundations.md](foundations.md) §5 records the change). Whether a row mentions the viewer is the server's `data.mentioned`, never the body re-read, as in chat. It is neutral for the reason the chat list's unread is ([foundations.md](foundations.md#5-semantic-colors) §5, "Channel unread is neutral"): red is for direct address and the accent is never a state. The web notification drawer is the reference: a bare title, a worded Mark all read beside the close while anything is unread, no count and no description. (Mark all read was an icon until 2026-11-09, when the owner asked for one that is obvious, #3929; Slack and GitHub word theirs too.) The realtime ping refetches, so the header has no Refresh; a failed load carries its own Try again. This departs from web board `1g`, and [`web-dashboard/README.md`](../web-dashboard/README.md) § Surface decisions records it.
- **An object's actions in a sheet are icons in its header.** Beside the close, in the close's own quiet tone, each with its accessible name and a native tooltip (`title`), 36px under a mouse and 44px under a finger. The web event panel draws Edit and Delete this way, through `PanelHeader`'s `actions` slot (`components/shared/panel.tsx`), which centres them on the title's first line. A form's commit button keeps its words ("Save changes", "Create event").
- **A title is text.** No decorative glyph before a dialog or sheet title that repeats the button just pressed. A mark that names the surface is not decoration: s17's ✦ before "Ask Frapp" is the Ask mark (§11), and the onboarding tutorial's per-slide glyphs name each slide ([`iconography.md`](iconography.md)).
- **Settings live a couple of taps away, not on the header** (owner rule, 2026-11-06). A screen's header names the thing and carries its one or two direct actions; a setting, and the state that setting is in, goes behind the `⋯` menu, and so does everything a member reaches for only now and then. Nothing is exposed just because it exists. The chat thread is the reference: a channel's notification level is a row in the `⋯` menu on both surfaces, and a muted channel's header looks like any other. Mobile drew a speaker glyph there until this rule (with the word "Muted" beside it before that), and web drew a mute mark beside the channel name; both are gone. Where the state still matters at a glance, it shows where the member scans for it: web's chat rail marks a muted channel, and web's menu row reads "Notifications · Muted". Mobile's channel list marks one too since 2026-11-06: a crossed-out bell after the name, and its name and preview drawn as read unless it addresses the member. Its count badge stays, so the number is still there for a member who looks. Chat home's filters follow the same rule on mobile since that day: Unread only and Hide muted sit in a menu behind a filter button at the end of the first section heading, as web keeps them behind its Channels header, instead of two chips above the list. The button shows a dot in the chapter accent while one is on, and a line under the heading says so, with a Show all, so a shortened list never reads as a chapter with fewer channels. Before and after: <https://claude.ai/artifact/BbP85N8J6vtfPnnJiAFVtY>.
- **A destructive action never sits in a record's body or footer, and one aimed at a person lives behind the `⋯`** (owner rule, 2026-11-11, after web drew a full-width red "Remove member" button at the foot of every member profile, his own included: "That's not the action that people go there to do"). A profile, an event or a document leads with what people open it for. A record's own actions sit in its header beside the close, as § Chrome weight has them: the event panel's Delete is an icon there. An action aimed at a person, removing or blocking them, is rarer and heavier, so it is a row in a `⋯` menu in that header, in danger ink, rather than a glyph of its own. Either way it asks before it acts (§ Dialogs). A destructive action that would act on the viewer themselves is not offered at all: no Remove and no Block on your own profile. Where the API decides who may act on whom (removal follows the [rank rules](../../behavior/rbac.md#rank)), the client draws what the API answers for this viewer and this record (`viewer_can_remove`), never a bare permission check, so a chair is never shown a control that can only refuse. A `⋯` with nothing to hold isn't drawn. The member profile is the first record on it: web's `⋯` holds Remove from chapter, mobile's (`TallSheetHeader`'s `actions`) holds Block or Unblock and Remove from chapter; Block was a red row at the foot of the mobile profile until then. Comparable: Discord keeps Kick and Ban in a member's `⋯` and right-click menus, offered only against members whose top role ranks below yours; Slack keeps deactivation out of profiles entirely, in Manage members. Before and after: https://claude.ai/artifact/TcvvsPR4XGsHNUARvnRWs7.
- **"Not yet" is a popover, not a modal.** A feature that can't answer yet says so in a small popover anchored to its control (the web Ask pill), which Escape or a click away closes. A screen-dimming dialog is for content and decisions.

### Going back (mobile)

Owner rule, 2026-11-06 ("On every screen, I should be able to swipe to the left to get back … give the users what they'd expect"): every mobile screen past the four tabs goes back by the platform's own gesture, the edge swipe on iOS and the back button or back gesture on Android, and every drawn way back (the back capsule, a ✕) does exactly what that gesture does. ### Icon-only actions

> **Owner decision, 2026-11-05 (Paul, UI audit).** A button whose icon already says what it does draws the icon alone, on web and mobile. "Download" beside a download arrow, "Dismiss" beside an ×, and "Muted" beside a muted speaker said the same thing twice and made secondary controls louder than the content they act on.

**Which buttons lose their words.** Secondary and row actions with a glyph everyone reads: download, dismiss or close, copy, edit, delete or remove, enable or disable, mark all read. A bare word with no icon counts too when such a glyph exists (mobile Profile's "Edit" is a pencil now, in the back-row slot where Documents already drew its upload glyph).

**Which keep them.**

- A form's primary action (Save changes, Create event, Send invite) and a sheet's commit.
- A choice between outcomes, where the word is the decision: a moderation queue's Mark reviewed / Dismiss / Mark actioned, a dialog's Cancel beside its commit.
- An error state's Retry: it is that state's one action, and it sits on text the eye is already reading.
- A verb with no glyph that says it alone ("Change photo", "Enable Dues"). Don't invent an icon to drop a word.

**What the word becomes.** It never disappears; it moves.

- Web: `aria-label` plus a native `title` tooltip, both from one `label`. `apps/web/components/ui/icon-button.tsx` (`IconButton`) requires the label and sets both. A caller's own `title` (the subscription gate's "Reconnect to make changes.") is added after the label, never in place of it, and a disabled icon button still takes the pointer so its tooltip shows (the shared button recipe turns pointer events off when disabled, which hid both). Use it rather than `Button size="icon"` with hand-written attributes: a `title=` placed before `{...gate.controlProps()}` is wiped by the gate's `title: undefined`.
- Mobile: `accessibilityLabel`, plus `accessibilityHint` for what the label can't say. `apps/mobile/components/icon-button.tsx` (`IconButton`) requires the label.
- Touch has no hover, on the app or on the web dashboard in a phone's browser, so a finger never sees the tooltip. That is why the list above is short: the glyph has to be one a member already knows. If it isn't, the button keeps its word.
- A label names its object when a screen has several of the same control: "Download Bylaws", "Delete North Library", "Revoke the Member invite", never a column of bare "Delete".

**What it looks like.** An icon button is Ghost (above, neutral since 2026-11-05) and takes the Small step of [foundations.md](foundations.md) §9's control size scale, 36px to a mouse and 44 to a finger, in a row or standing alone (`IconButton` sets it). On mobile the glyph is 20pt in a box its own size with 12pt of slop on each side, which reaches the 44pt floor (§2) without taking layout height, so a one-line banner doesn't grow. Every dismiss is the same ×: Lucide `X` on web, `CloseGlyph` on mobile ([iconography.md](iconography.md) §6.1.5). A control that was busy says so in the glyph's place (a spinner) instead of swapping in a word like "Marking…".

## 4. Inputs and selection

**Dashboard filter toolbars take the toolbar height, not the default field height (40 to a mouse, 44 to a finger).** *(2026-11-11: the native `<select>` classes this paragraph used to name are gone with the last native selects, #3827; a toolbar's Radix trigger takes `dashboardToolbarHeightClassName`, and a select in a dialog or form keeps `SelectTrigger`'s own field height.)* The toolbar height is 36 to a mouse and 44 to a finger (§3, corrected 2026-11-05). That is a carve-out for filter chrome specifically: a filter row is secondary chrome sitting above the thing it filters, and mixing §4's field with §3's Inline button in one row renders two visibly different heights, which is what `/members`, `/events`, `/polls` and `/roles` all did. Reading it as the height for every select reproduces the same defect one context over: the event editor once put two filter-height selects in a `sm:grid-cols-2` beside `Input`s, so a *form* row rendered at two heights. On a coarse pointer the row is 44, the touch floor (§2), so nothing a finger hits goes under it.

**The filter chip (web)** *(added 2026-11-10, #3970, asked by `shawnatomklein-eng`; the chip itself dates from the 2026-11-05 `/backwork` rebuild)*. Three toolbars filter with chips: `/backwork`, `/documents` and the Directory. The other toolbars' selects stay under the select rule in §2 and the paragraph above; whether they become chips too is not decided here. `FilterChip` in `apps/web/components/shared/filter-chip.tsx` is a round Button at the toolbar height that opens a list of the values some row actually has, applied the moment one is picked: Secondary at rest, tinted while a value is set, and the chip then names the value. `SortChip` beside it is the order control, never tinted, since an order is always set. The Directory's native selects moved onto them in #3970. **A long list is searchable and still scrollable**: past `FILTER_SEARCH_THRESHOLD` options (the constant in that file) the list opens whole, in a popover with a type-to-filter field above it, focused on a mouse; on touch the field is left unfocused so the on-screen keyboard doesn't cover the list, and focus moves to the list itself so a hardware keyboard still works. The "any" row leads the whole list and steps aside while a query is typed. Typing narrows the list by word start, ignoring case, accents and punctuation (`matchesWordStarts`: "exec" finds "(Exec) Treasurer"); clearing the field brings the whole list back; arrow keys and Enter pick; Esc clears a typed query first and closes on the second press; a query that matches nothing says so in one line. Options are never hidden behind the search. A short list stays a plain menu. Comparable pickers: Slack's "Filter by", Linear's filter menu, Notion's property filters, GitHub's label picker.

### Text input

| State | Fill | Border | Text |
| ----- | ---- | ------ | ---- |
| Rest | surface `#1A1A1A` | 1px `rgba(255,255,255,.14)` | value `#EDEAE3`, placeholder `#78716A` |
| Focus | surface `#1A1A1A` | 1px `accent-11` + 3px ring `accent-8` @ ~25% (§2) | caret 2px `accent-9` |
| Error | surface `#1A1A1A` | 1px `#f85149` + 3px ring danger @ ~25% | caption 12.5 `#f85149` below |
| Disabled | surface `#1A1A1A` | 1px `rgba(255,255,255,.08)` | `#57534C` |

The Field step: 40px to a fine pointer and 44px on touch, padding-x 12 at both, radius 12 (corrected 2026-11-05 from 48px and padding-x 14 everywhere, with §3's button heights, for the same reason). A multi-line field (`Textarea`) takes the same padding and grows. On mobile a single-line field's text sets no line height, because iOS sits a single line at the bottom of its line box and the text then reads low; a multi-line field keeps the body's leading (`fieldTypeRole` and `fieldMultilineText`, `apps/mobile/lib/theme.tsx` and `components/sheet-scaffold.tsx`; 2026-11-06). A web sheet's fields sit one step down, at Small. The value stays 16px at either height, the body floor ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §7). The fill SHOULD sit one layer below the input's container (surface inside cards/sheets; bg `#131211` inside the surface-level app bar). The web top bar's Find field is the named exception: it shares the bar's surface (§7). *(Exception added 2026-11-05.)*

**The value and placeholder sit on the field's vertical center, on every surface.** A single-line field's vertical padding is equal top and bottom, and its text carries no line height beyond the font's own: on mobile the field's text style is `fieldTypeRole` (`apps/mobile/lib/theme.tsx`), the role's size and family without `lineHeight`, never `typeRole`. iOS lays a single-line `TextInput`'s glyphs at the bottom of whatever line box `lineHeight` makes, so `body`'s 25 on its 16pt font put every typed value about 4pt low; that shipped on every mobile field until 2026-11-06. A multi-line field keeps the role's line height, since its lines need the leading, and starts its text at the top (`textAlignVertical: "top"`; `sheetFieldStyles().fieldMultilineText`). The web `Input` already centers: symmetric padding, and the browser centers a single-line `<input>`'s text in its box. *(Rule added 2026-11-06.)*

**Floating label (web sign-in and sign-up).** The pre-auth forms put the label inside the field, where it reads at body size and moves up to caption size on focus, input or autofill, and they draw no placeholder (`apps/web/components/auth/floating-field.tsx`). The field is 52px rather than 48 so the floated 12px label and the 16px value both clear the edge on the grid. It exists so those two forms fit a laptop window without scrolling (`spec/ui/web-dashboard/README.md` § Responsive contract); every other form keeps the label above a 48px field. *(Exception added 2026-11-05.)*

In the Error state the caption MUST be wired to the field, not merely placed under it: `aria-describedby` on the input references the caption's id, and `aria-invalid` marks the input while the error stands.

**When an error shows** *(added 2026-11-06, owner direction: a graduation year field "red until I've typed all four digits")*. A field's error shows once the member leaves the field (blur) or presses the form's submit, and from then on it follows the value, so typing the fix clears it at once. It never shows while the member is still typing a value that more typing could make valid: "2" on the way to "2027", "#4" on the way to "#4F2683", "1" on the way to "1901". One hook decides it on both surfaces, `useFieldReveal` in `@repo/hooks` (`packages/hooks/src/field-reveal.ts`); a form computes validity on every render as before and asks the hook only whether to show it. Three consequences:

- **Submit stays pressable while a field is refused,** so pressing it can reveal why. A submit greyed out over an error the member can't see yet is a dead end. It still sends nothing.
- **An invalid value is kept, not reverted, on blur,** so the error can say what to fix. A field that silently snaps back to its old value on blur (mobile quiet hours did) hides the mistake instead of naming it.
- **A value no further typing can rescue may show at once**: a paste of letters into a number field, a points value past its cap. Every prefix of a valid value is valid there, so nothing is mid-word.

Forms on the hook: mobile Edit profile and the accent picker's hex field (whose hosts, the Chapter accent sheet and the create-chapter wizard, keep their submit pressable and pass `showError` on a press), and the founded year on web Settings → Organization. Web `/profile` and the toast-at-submit forms follow the rule without it: their only checks run at submit (the graduation year's `min`/`max`, which the browser checks on submit, and the server's refusal).

**Grouping and spacing in a form** *(added 2026-11-06)*. A form takes foundations §9's rhythm: 8 between a label and its own field, 16 between fields, 24 before a new group. A group with its own purpose gets a heading (label role, foreground) and, when it needs one, a one-line hint (caption, muted foreground) instead of repeating that purpose in every placeholder; the profile form's City and Company sit under "After graduation, shown in the alumni directory" rather than each saying "Optional, for the alumni directory". Two short related fields share a row when they fit a phone: Major beside a 96-wide Grad year, 16 apart like any two fields, and an error for either shows under the whole row rather than inside its narrow column. A group that doesn't apply to the member yet is left out rather than drawn empty: City and Company show from the member's graduation year on, or whenever either already holds a value (`showsAfterGraduation` in `@repo/validation`, Paul, 2026-11-06: "let's just not show the after grad stuff to undergrads").

### Time and date pickers

*(Added 2026-11-06, from the owner's iOS testing of quiet hours and mobile event times.)* A time or date a member picks is never a typed field and never a home-grown wheel. On mobile it is the platform's own picker, `@react-native-community/datetimepicker`, inside a grouped list row: on iOS the **compact** picker is the row's trailing control (a small pill that opens the wheel in place, `themeVariant="dark"`, `accentColor` the chapter accent), and on Android the row shows the formatted value and a tap opens the system dialog, which saves on OK. On web it is the browser's own input at the Field step: `type="time"` for a time of day (quiet hours), `type="datetime-local"` where a date and time travel together (the event editor). A setting whose times only matter while it is on (quiet hours) shows them only while its switch is on, so the screen carries no controls that govern nothing. A picker reports every position the wheel passes, so a value saved straight to the server is saved once the wheel rests, not on each change. First user: s16's quiet hours (`apps/mobile/components/settings/quiet-hours-section.tsx`).

*(Changed 2026-11-09, #3928, Paul.)* A task's due date on web is the exception to "never a typed field": the browser's date input shows and asks for a full `MM/DD/YYYY`, and a due date should not need the year. ## 5. Badges and chips

The Chip step ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §9): height 24px on every pointer (26–28 until 2026-11-05), radius 8–10, padding-x 8, text 12.5px / 600. On touch a chip that is a control carries the 44px hit area through padding or `hitSlop`, not a taller fill. Mobile's filter chips draw 32 (s13 draws 34).

| Kind | Fill | Border | Text | Use |
| ---- | ---- | ------ | ---- | --- |
| Accent | `accent-3` | 1px `accent-7` | `accent-11` | accent-worthy stats: points, active filters |
| Neutral | `rgba(255,255,255,.14)` | none | `#EDEAE3` | counts and unread markers ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §5) |
| Hairline | transparent | 1px `rgba(255,255,255,.08)` | `#A9A399` | quiet metadata that must not read as a status |
| Semantic | `--*-tint`: the status color @ 13%, as a token rather than an alpha utility ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §5) | none | status color | Paid / Overdue / status only — never decorative |
| Mention / DM | mention/DM red | none | white | unread mentions and DMs only |

- The mention/DM red value, its fixed-and-semantic rule, and the treatment of channel unread markers are owned by [foundations.md](foundations.md) §5; this section specs only the badge geometry. Placement in nav: [navigation.md](../mobile/navigation.md).
- The reference lifts success/info badge text slightly for contrast on the tint (`#4BC262`, `#4C93F8`); implementations MAY lift the text tone for AA contrast, but the hue is the fixed semantic.
- **The Semantic kind ships three hues, and only danger needs the lift.** Measured on its own 13% tint across the whole surface ladder: success **4.79–6.08:1** and warning **5.27–6.81:1** both clear §6's 4.5:1 gate unlifted, so they render in the semantic hue itself; danger is **3.82–4.85:1**, under the gate from `--surface-1` up, which is what `--destructive-text` (**5.07–6.45:1**) exists for. The tint is an `rgba()` token, so these figures hold in every browser (foundations.md §5). Info would need a lift too (3.45:1 on `--popover`) and deliberately has no kind, because it has no call site. The lift is about the tint: on a plain ladder surface the solid danger measures 4.95–5.58:1 up to `--card` and is the correct tone there, but 4.48:1 on `--popover`, so danger text in a dialog takes the lift even untinted. Measured in [`apps/web/components/billing/status-contrast.spec.ts`](../../../apps/web/components/billing/status-contrast.spec.ts) and, reading the alpha from the tokens, [`apps/web/components/shared/status-tint-contrast.spec.ts`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/status-tint-contrast.spec.ts).
- **A status badge is never the chapter accent.** Under a green-seeded chapter an accent badge is indistinguishable from the success badge (1.08:1) and under a red-seeded one from the danger badge (1.13:1) — so a chapter whose brand is red reads `PAID` as overdue. [`writing.md`](writing.md) §5 owns the rule; this is the measurement behind it.
- **The rule is about the whole vocabulary, not one badge.** #1202 reported a single accent-painted status; the Chapter Ops slice found five, across four files, in three spellings — two mappers, two inline ternaries and a bare literal. The same sweep found the mirror error: §5's **Neutral** kind is "counts and unread markers", and it was carrying `LATE`, `PENDING` and `ACTIVE`, i.e. a status rendered in the count badge. So a family is checked by enumerating every state its screens can render, not by grepping for the accent. Web mappers, one per domain vocabulary: [`apps/web/components/events/attendance-status.ts`](../../../apps/web/components/events/attendance-status.ts), [`apps/web/components/service/service-status.ts`](../../../apps/web/components/service/service-status.ts), [`apps/web/components/study/study-status.ts`](../../../apps/web/components/study/study-status.ts), [`apps/web/components/geofences/geofence-status.ts`](../../../apps/web/components/geofences/geofence-status.ts), and [`apps/web/components/billing/invoice-status.ts`](../../../apps/web/components/billing/invoice-status.ts). The shared invariant is asserted in [`apps/web/components/shared/status-kind.spec.ts`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/status-kind.spec.ts). A mapper whose input is a derived boolean rather than a server token — `geofenceStatusKind` (and `pollStatusKind` until `/polls` was retired, #3871) — joins that file's `BOOLEAN_MAPPERS` table instead: the accent and Neutral invariants apply unchanged, and the "unmapped status falls back to Hairline" one has no meaning when there is no third value for the server to add.
- **The Accent kind's other half is a live instruction, not decoration.** §5 names two accent-worthy things and the accent sweeps keep finding only the first. Chapter Ops took the accent off five statuses and had to *give* it to the one badge that deserved it (the `+N pts` chip). The second is **active filters**, and the Resources & Reporting slice found the mirror shape: `/documents` painted its selected folder `bg-primary/10 text-primary` — a raw opacity wash of the chapter accent, which README §2 bans outright as "raw chapter hex painting UI", on a control whose *intent* was correct all along. The recipe is §2's two row states, not §7's sidebar item — §7 defines one active fill (`accent-3`) and a hover that falls back to the card, which a rail already sitting *on* a card cannot use. A filter rail needs two states that are both distinguishable on a card: active takes `accent-4` plus `accent-11` text, and hover the neutral `--card-hover` (§2, changed 2026-11-05); its geometry is a dense rail inside a card rather than §7's 40px sidebar row. So an accent sweep asks both questions — what is wearing the accent that should not, and what should be wearing it that is not — and the answer to the second is spelled in tokens, never in an opacity wash.

## 9. Sheets and dialogs

### Surface size

*(Added 2026-11-05, owner call: "small boxed dialogs for content that should be big" was one of the four patterns he asked to remove.)* The size of the surface follows its content, and the same content gets the same surface on every screen and on both apps.

| Content | Web | Mobile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A question, a confirmation, or a short form that fits without scrolling | **Dialog**: centered, the primitive's 512px, never scrolls | **Sheet** sized to its content, or a confirm alert |
| Anything that scrolls, holds a list that grows, or shows a whole record (an event, a member, a long form, every live invite) | **Panel**: full height from the right edge, 576px, title row and footer pinned while the body scrolls ([`apps/web/components/shared/panel.tsx`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/panel.tsx)) | **Tall sheet**: full height, stopping just under the status bar, with an icon X in the header ([`apps/mobile/components/sheet-scaffold.tsx`](../../../apps/mobile/components/sheet-scaffold.tsx) `useTallSheetProps`, `TallSheetHeader`) |
| Switches you change while watching the page | **Drawer**: the per-page settings drawer, `4c`'s 400px ([`../web-dashboard/README.md`](../web-dashboard/README.md) § Page grammar) | not used |

Rules that go with it:

- **A dialog never scrolls.** A dialog that needs a `max-h` and `overflow-y-auto` to fit is a panel. Before this rule five forms scrolled inside dialogs, with three different width and height caps between them (512 to 672 wide, 80vh to 90vh tall).
- **One panel width.** Every panel is 576px, the width the event and member detail sheets already had. A form that opens from a record (Edit event from the event panel) slides in at the same size, so it reads as the record turning into its editor.
- **Nothing scrolls inside a panel's body.** A list in a panel flows with the body rather than sitting in its own scroll box (`PermissionCheckboxGrid`'s `inPanel`).
- **The primary action is pinned.** A panel's footer and a sheet's primary button stay on screen however long the body is. The member panel's Save role changes used to sit below the fold.
- **A big surface closes with an X, not a word.** The panel's corner X and the tall sheet's header X are icon only. A record does not repeat its kind as a title: the member sheet shows the member's name, not "Member".
- **No decorative glyph before a title.** The title names the surface; a glyph repeating the button just pressed is chrome.
- **Mobile picks match web.** A member's profile is a panel on web and a tall sheet on mobile. Assign to, which lists the whole roster, is a tall sheet with its search pinned above the list.
- **A page's own record opens docked, inside the page** (Paul, 2026-11-11: the panel "feels intrusive and I can't see any other info while I fill it out"). When the page that owns the record opens it (Events opens its events), the panel takes the place of the page in the tool column beside chat: nothing is dimmed, chat stays live, and its header is the tool page header's 48px row with a back arrow instead of a corner X, as chat's thread pane has. Escape and the arrow go back; Edit opened from an event's view goes back to that view. Below the side-by-side width the tool column is the page, so docked is a full page there. A panel opened from somewhere with no page of its own (the chat + card's More options for an event) stays the sheet above, and a yes/no confirmation stays the centred dialog, where blocking the screen is the point. `Panel`'s `docked` prop ([`panel.tsx`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/panel.tsx)) switches it; Events (create, edit, view) is the first page on it, and the other pages' panels follow once the owner has used it. Comparable: Gmail's compose window, Notion's side peek, Slack's thread pane. A docked form's two-up rows wrap by the panel's own width, not the window's, since beside chat it can be 375px wide on a wide screen.
- **A short form started from the composer's + opens in the + card** (Paul, 2026-11-10, [#3983](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3983)). The + card's menu turns into the form in place, anchored above the +: no dialog, no scrim, and the chat stays readable and live behind it. Comparable: Google Calendar's quick create bubble, Todoist's and Asana's Quick Add; it departs from Slack's and Teams' modal for the same job, which block the conversation the form is about. The card is 360px wide in both views, so turning into a form doesn't jump; its fields and buttons are at the Small step (36 to a mouse, 44 to a finger); its header is 48px, a back arrow, the form's title and an icon X; and its footer is a trash icon (Discard draft), any secondary action, then the primary on the right. At its usual length no form scrolls in it; a ten-option poll on a short window scrolls rather than clip, as a safety net. A card form holds only what every item needs (an event's name, date, start and end); everything else is the full editor's, behind More options, with what was typed carried over. Closing the card never loses a draft: a click away, Escape (which first steps back to the menu), the X, Back and a channel switch all keep it, one per form per channel, until it is posted or discarded, and the menu marks a row that holds one with "Draft". The Service and Points pages keep their dialogs; each form is one body in two layouts (`FormLayout` in [`short-form.tsx`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/short-form.tsx)), so the two hosts never drift. ## 10. State family — skeleton / empty / error

This section specs the **anatomy** of the three visual variants. Since 2026-11-07 only error and offline draw a card; empty is one line (§ Empty). *Which* states a surface MUST ship is owned by [README.md](README.md) §4 and is not restated here. Connection banners and retry/backoff behavior are owned by [resilience](../resilience/README.md); state copy voice by [writing.md](writing.md).

### Skeleton (loading)

- **Content-shaped:** the skeleton mirrors the layout it becomes — same blocks, same radii. No spinner-in-a-box. That includes the surface: a flush page's skeleton is flush too, with no card fill or border box the loaded page does not draw. Text the screen already knows renders as itself, not as a bar: on web the route title is the top bar's and stays up through the load, so a page skeleton draws none, and it draws action-row placeholders only for controls every member sees (a placeholder for a permission-gated button draws a row that then vanishes). Web (2026-11-05): every dashboard route ships a `loading.tsx` drawn from [`apps/web/components/shared/page-skeletons.tsx`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/page-skeletons.tsx), and the same component is the page's first-load branch, so navigation and the in-flight query show one picture; `page-skeletons.spec.tsx` fails a new route that ships without one, and fails a new use of the generic loading states outside its shrinking allowlist. Mobile (2026-11-05): each screen draws its own skeleton inside one `SkeletonRegion` (one busy node named for what loads, one pulse), from the same styles its loaded rows use, with `SkeletonText` lines sized to the text they become (Figtree's 1.2em line box, a bar at its 0.7em cap height) so a placeholder row is as tall as the real one. The chat list and thread skeletons are the exception for now: they still draw fixed-height bars, which predate `SkeletonText`. A row or card component owns its placeholder beside it (`ListRowSkeleton`, `HistoryRowsSkeleton`, `TaskRowSkeleton`, `ChoiceRowSkeleton`, `StartCardSkeleton`, `BalanceCardSkeleton`); a caption the data decides is a `SectionHeaderSkeleton` bar, a fixed one renders as itself. `apps/mobile/components/screen-skeletons.spec.tsx` pins the line boxes and row frames, and fails a new `SkeletonLines` (the generic card of bars) outside its shrinking allowlist or a spinner over a "Loading…" caption.
- **Caption:** a mirrored skeleton names nothing on screen; its "Loading …" caption is one `role="status"` line for assistive tech only, kept apart from the blocks so any real content the skeleton renders is not marked busy. On mobile the caption is the region's accessible name (`SkeletonRegion`'s `label`), and the region is one node, so fixed text drawn inside it (Profile's Details caption) is not read until the content lands; React Native has no live status line to keep apart. The visible caption belongs to the generic `LoadingState`, which can't say by its shape what is loading, and which is now left only on regions inside a loaded page (an attendance list, an audit card) not yet given their own skeleton. Those regions are not § Surface size panels.
- Shimmer: linear gradient 90°, elevated `#1E1E1E` at 25%/75% and highlight `#2B2B2B` at 50%; background-size 260px; sweep 1.4s linear infinite, phase-shared across blocks. The highlight moved from `#38312A` with the greenfield shell ([#2141](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2141)) to match the framework board, and to the neutral `#2B2B2B` with the 2026-11-05 neutral ladder ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §2), pitched at 1.18:1 on `--popover` to keep the sweep's pre-greenfield amplitude. **Mobile does not draw the shimmer yet** (2026-11-05 correction): it has no gradient primitive, so `apps/mobile/components/state-block.tsx` pulses a skeleton's blocks together as a stand-in (one opacity animation per skeleton, so the blocks stay in phase), holds it still under reduced motion (followed as the setting changes) and while the skeleton's screen is out of view, and carries a `TODO-DESIGN:` naming these values. Every mobile skeleton shape is a `SkeletonBlock` inside one `SkeletonPulse` (`card-list-skeleton.tsx` moved onto it with [#3453](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3453), and the chat thread's `thread-skeleton.tsx` and host check-in's QR placeholder with it), so the sweep lands there once. When it does draw the sweep it will carry them by hand rather than reading `--skeleton-highlight`, so the two drift unless both move together. The amplitude cost that change carries is measured in [`../web-dashboard/tokens.md`](../web-dashboard/tokens.md) L-01.
- Shapes: text lines 13px tall, radius 6, varied widths (~45–70%); avatars stay circles; control-sized blocks 44px, radius 12. On mobile a text line is sized to the text it becomes instead (the `SkeletonText` line box above), because a fixed 13px bar made every placeholder row a different height from its real one; radius 6 has no token there and `chip` (8) stands in, with a `TODO-DESIGN:`.
- Skeletons are neutral only — never accent, never semantic.
- Show on first load only; background refetches keep stale content in place ([resilience](../resilience/README.md)).

### Pending states

Owner rule, 2026-11-06, on web and mobile alike: **something in flight is a quiet change to the thing itself, never an extra line and never a spinner that moves the layout.** A message being sent draws its text gray until the server confirms it, as Discord does (§11 § What rides the row, "Delivery state"); a card in flight dims to half opacity. A button that is saving swaps its label in place ("Save" to "Saving…") or dims while disabled, inside a box that keeps its size; it does not grow a spinner beside its label. A row being sent, saved or uploaded never adds a caption under itself that appears and disappears, because that pushes everything below it down a line and back. A spinner is for something that has no shape of its own to dim, and only inside a control whose size it does not change (the composer's attach button while the system photo picker is up; a photo uploading in the tray fades instead, § Chat photos). A first load is a skeleton (above), never a spinner over a caption. Failure is the exception: it needs a line, because it needs a control (Retry, Discard), and it stays until the member acts. The chat thread was brought onto this rule first (2026-11-06); the other surfaces' spinners are checked against it as they are touched.

### Empty

**One line, never a card** (2026-11-07, the [empty-states plan](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3429); [writing.md](writing.md) §4 owns the seven rules and the words). An empty list, a search or filter that matched nothing, a done queue, and a blocked state (no chapter picked, a role without access, a module switched off, a link to something gone) render as one muted line where the rows would be. The surrounding layout stays. The way out, when there is one (Clear search, Clear filters, Search all files, Browse channels), is a `link`-variant run of text at the end of the sentence.

- **Web:** `InlineNote` and `InlineNoteAction` ([`apps/web/components/shared/inline-note.tsx`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/inline-note.tsx)). A line whose action goes to another page uses `inlineNoteActionClassName` on a `Link` (Study's Add a study zone).
- **Mobile:** `InlineNote` ([`apps/mobile/components/inline-note.tsx`](../../../apps/mobile/components/inline-note.tsx), body role, muted foreground). Its `action` (`{ label, onPress }`) draws the way out as an underlined run in the chapter accent. It never creates anything; creating lives on the tab bar's + and a screen's floating +, which the sentence names instead (`Tap + to upload one.`). `NoChapterState` in `state-block.tsx` is an `InlineNote` with the picker's path. Its `tone="warning"` is for a list that loaded but is missing a part ("Couldn't load polls. Pull down to try again." on Activity, 2026-11-11, #4173), drawn in the warning hue as web's note is (`text-warning`), so a part that failed never reads as a part that's empty; it is a failure line, not an empty state.

The empty card (icon tile, title, body, CTA) is gone on both clients, with its components (web `EmptyState` and `NestedEmpty`, mobile `EmptyState` and `EmptyStateAction`). Empty is never alarming: muted text and the accent link only, no semantic colour, no blame in copy.

### Error

Error surfaces MUST NOT use the chapter accent. Field-level validation errors use the input error state (§4), not this surface.

### Nested inside a card

A state that replaces a whole screen paints `--card`, as the tables above spec. A state rendered **inside a container it cannot rise above** MUST drop that fill. The obvious case is a `<CardContent>`, where `--card` on `--card` is 1.00:1 and the region disappears outright. The Chapter Ops slice found the other one: a panel inside a `SheetContent` (`--popover`, the **top** of the ladder) painting `--card` is 1.085:1 in the *wrong direction*, so it reads as a hole rather than as elevation, and there is no higher step to raise the sheet to instead. Dropping the fill is not a compromise in either case — measured on `--popover`, the hairline over the container separates at 1.275:1 where the card's own hairline managed 1.155:1, so removing the fill **improves** the boundary while removing the inversion. It keeps everything else — the hairline (which is the load-bearing edge once the fill is gone, §2), the icon tile, the title, the body, the CTA, and the error's sanctioned semantic border — because this family is required to differ **in colour rather than in shape**, and bordering some variants and not others would break exactly that. Web implementation: [`apps/web/components/shared/nested-states.tsx`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/nested-states.tsx), which carries **three** members since the nested empty went on 2026-11-07 — loading, error and the offline state, whose glyph is `WifiOff` rather than the danger triangle exactly as the top-level `OfflineState`'s is. It stopped at three until `/documents` and `/backwork` needed one, and the first two screens to render an offline state inside a card had reached for the error variant, telling a member with a dropped connection that something had failed.

Two of the nested family's compromises assume a nested state is *one of several*: the live region is left to the top-level `LoadingState` that owns the screen, and the title is a `<p>` rather than an `<h2>` because `/billing` renders two from one query and produced two identical headings. Neither holds where the nested state is a page's **only** async state — there the page is silent to a screen reader mid-load, and its error and empty states have no heading at all, since `CardTitle` is a `<div>`. The `sole` prop says which case a call site is; it defaults off, so it changes nothing for the consumers the module was written for.

**The offline state has a third container, and it is not a region at all.** The two above assume the state stands in for a *layout* — a screen (`OfflineState`, painting `--card`) or a card's contents (`NestedOffline`, dropping the fill). `<Can>` produced the third: a gate standing in for a **single control**, an Upload button in a toolbar or a row action in a table cell. Most of the gate's call sites are that shape (`CONSUMERS` in [`apps/web/components/shared/can-fallback.spec.tsx`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/can-fallback.spec.tsx) lists every file that mounts one), and there a 208px card is not a smaller version of the right answer — it is the wrong object, three of them stacked on `/documents` alone. `PermissionsOffline` ([`apps/web/components/shared/async-states.tsx`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/async-states.tsx)) is the same family at control scale: same `WifiOff`, same tone, same Retry, one inline row. It carries **no fill and no border** — the hairline is the load-bearing edge of a region (§2), and this is not one; a bordered chip where a button was reads as a disabled button rather than as a statement about the network. What it may not drop is the **colour**: this family differs in colour rather than in shape, and the first cut painted the whole row `--muted-foreground`, which made it indistinguishable from an ordinary permission-denied stand-in like the attendance sheet's "View only" — the exact conflation of "denied" and "could not check" the gate rule exists to end. The glyph carries the tone and the caption stays neutral, because the caption is a sentence rather than a status. It takes the **solid** `--destructive`, not `--destructive-text`: §5's lift is for a hue on its own 13% tint, and on a plain ladder surface the solid measures 4.717–5.795:1 across all four steps. The glyph is 16px, [iconography.md](iconography.md) §2's inline-metadata size, which is what the connection banner drew for the same condition until it became a dot-and-label pill (2026-11-06); 14 is the badge-companion carve-out and this glyph leads a notice rather than trailing a badge. Its surface-scale twin `PermissionsOfflineSurface` is a thin wrapper over `OfflineState` that exists to hold the shared half of the copy: every screen- or card-scale gate renders it (the `SURFACE_GATES` ledger in [`can-fallback.spec.tsx`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/can-fallback.spec.tsx) lists them, and fails on one that stops), the title is `writing.md` §7's "(global)" row while the descriptions are per-surface, and a retyped copy of one title per gate is a tone pass away from forking — invisibly, since each is ~30 tokens against `check:duplication`'s 50-token floor.

### Chat messages (compact layout)

> **Owner decision, 2026-09-29 (Paul, [#2873](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2873)).** Chat is a compact, bubble-free timeline on web and mobile: no bubbles; a sender's messages within five minutes collapse into one **run** under a single author line; the author line shows the time only; the date appears only in a divider where a new day starts. He approved the layout on a design review page before it was built, including three choices that page put to him: the viewer's own messages sit on the left like everyone else's, the web action bar goes icon-only, and the grouping window stays five minutes. This supersedes s05's and panel 4e's bubble drawings for chat, and the web framework board's ([`web-framework.dc.html`](../web-dashboard/reference/web-framework.dc.html): the `#general` thread, the pending self bubble, the skeleton's "r18 bubbles" and the token sheet's "Bubbles r18 · tail 6"), although that board is rank 1 for web; they remain truth for everything else they draw.

**Why.** The chapter is moving its daily chat off Discord ([#2558](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2558), cutover [#2776](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2776)), and members will judge Frapp against Discord's density. Staging, loaded with imported Tau Nu history, showed what the bubble cost. A one-line message took a 55px bubble (12 + 25 + 12 of padding and line box, two hairlines, a 4px gap) where its text needs 25. Mobile repeated the full avatar and author line on every message, because the board draws no grouped run. And an attachment-only message painted an empty bubble above its image.

**What it replaced, and why the bubble lost.** The bubble is kept here as the rejected alternative, because its reasons were real:

- **Mine versus theirs by side and fill.** The self bubble was right-aligned in `accent-9` with `accent-contrast` text, the one place a message took the chapter accent; incoming bubbles were left-aligned in neutral `--card` with a hairline, so "mine vs theirs" survived any accent.
- **Each message was an object with an edge.** The incoming bubble's hairline was load-bearing, since one ladder step is ~1.12:1.
- **The board drew it.** s05 and panel 4e both draw bubbles at the locked radius, 18 with the tail corner at 6.

What outweighed them:

- **Density, first.** Grouping alone, which web already did, still left a bubble per message at roughly twice the height of its text.
- **The sided layout was a tax on everything built beside it.** The action cluster needed a zero-height `rtl` flex track to hug a shrink-wrapped bubble, the inline editor needed conditional width rules because a `<textarea>` inside a hugging block sizes itself circularly, a deleted message had to keep the side it had, and a rich card needed an exception to the sided rule.
- **The per-tenant self fill was a contrast problem.** The in-bubble mention chip had to be opaque because an alpha tint over `--primary` measured 1.03:1 at worst across the seed corpus. Text selection had to avoid the accent because it vanished on the self bubble.

Alternatives weighed and rejected:

- **Bubbles, grouped.** This is what web shipped until this decision. It keeps every cost above except the repeated author line.
- **Bubbles on mobile only.** Two layouts would have to be kept in step, and the Discord comparison happens on phones first.
- **Own messages right-aligned without a fill.** A right-aligned run with no fill to anchor it reads as a layout bug. The owner chose the left.
- **A density toggle.** Per-user density was [#2252](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2252), parked; the compact layout was the only one. *(Corrected 2026-11-06: the owner asked for the toggle, so members now pick a density, see [§ Density](#density) below. The layout this section describes is the default, **cozy**. Its name here, "compact layout", predates the choice and means bubble-free, not the `compact` density.)*

> *(Reopened 2026-11-10 as an opt-in: Paul, on [#3787](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3787#issuecomment-6771881257), "it's just an opt-in, right? You know, by default it's still Compact … I'm not opposed to that." ("Compact" there means today's bubble-free layout; the default density is cozy.) Bubbles is now a third density beside cozy and compact, see [§ Bubbles](#bubbles). The reasons above stay the record of why it is not the default.)*

#### Density

> **Owner decision, 2026-11-06 (Paul, [#2252](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2252)): "can we let users change their chat density in their settings?"** Two densities, named as Discord names them, on web and mobile alike. The vocabulary lives once, in `@repo/chat-core/density`.
>
> Changed in #3787 by `whewitt-frapp`, 2026-11-10: a third density, bubbles (§ [Bubbles](#bubbles)), on Paul's opt-in OK.

| | Cozy (default) | Compact |
|---|---|---|
| Run start | 16 below the row above | 4 below |
| Avatar | 32px, first row of a run (§ Row anatomy) | None |
| Author line | Name, then time, over the body | None. Web: the time in a 56px gutter, right-aligned caption, 8 from the text; the name leads the body's first line in the body size at 600, floated so the text wraps back under it. Mobile: no gutter; the time (caption) and the name (body size at 600) lead the body's first line inline |
| Follow-on time | Web: revealed in the 32px gutter | Web: revealed in the 56px gutter. Mobile: none, as in cozy |
| A row with no text (a card, a photo, the web editor) | Author line as usual | Time and name on a line of their own above it |
| Reply elbow | From the avatar's centre | From the text column's edge onto the name |

A body that opens with a code block, a list or a quote takes mobile's lead on a line of its own above it (`startsOnOwnLine` in `@repo/chat-core`'s markdown flow), as the trailing markers take their own line after one. Web's float drops such a block below the name by itself.

Everything else on the row (body type, reactions, trailing markers, delivery state, day dividers, grouping) is the same in both, and the name keeps its role color in both. The rows drawn beside messages follow the density too: the thread skeletons on both clients (compact's shapes are a time bar and a name bar leading one body bar), the blocked-member tombstone (its line stays in the body column) and audit lines. A web audit line keeps its shield in compact's time gutter; a mobile one has no gutter in compact, so it draws no shield and its words start at the row's inset with the message text.

Mobile: Settings, a **Chat** section with one **Chat density** row whose value names the current choice and opens a sheet of the same three cards; a tap saves and closes. The thread redraws at once on both.

**Onboarding and Always formal** (2026-11-10, #3787). A new member also picks from the same cards while setting up (web tutorial, mobile s03, and both chapter setups for a founder). A channel set to Always formal draws cozy for everyone, which is the "formal" display: [`chat/README.md` § Always formal](../../behavior/chat/README.md#always-formal).

**Per device, not per account.** Both controls say so ("Applies on this browser", "Applies on this phone"); the rule and its reason are the [chat README's](../../behavior/chat/README.md#chat-density) § Chat density.

The third density, `bubbles` in `@repo/chat-core/density`, on web and mobile alike:

| Part | Spec |
| ---- | ---- |
| Sides | Others' messages on the left, the viewer's own on the right. |

Yours: `accent-primary` with `on-primary` text, the accent engine's AA-by-construction pair ([accent-engine.md](accent-engine.md) §8). #### Row anatomy

| Part | Spec |
| ---- | ---- |
| Thread surface | `--background`, or the channel's chat background drawn behind it ([chat § Chat backgrounds](../../behavior/chat/README.md#chat-backgrounds), #3973): a dark built-in, or a photo blurred under `--background` at a fixed scrim. Every one keeps the thread's text tokens AA, pinned by `apps/web/components/chat/chat-background-contrast.spec.ts`. Rows run the full width of the thread column with no fill at rest. |
| Row padding | 20px horizontal on web, 16pt on mobile (the composer's inset). A row that starts a run sits 16 below the row above; a follow-on sits 2 below. |
| Avatar | On the first row of a run only: 32px circle, initials caption / 700, painted in the member's tint: their hue at 18% over `--popover`, the initials in the full hue (below). A photo, where the author has one, takes the circle in place of the initials; which photo, and on which surface, is the [chat README's](../../behavior/chat/README.md#hot-path-client-behavior) to say. The 32px gutter is held open on every row, and the body sits 12 to its right. **The member's hue** (approved by Paul on [#3860](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3860), 2026-11-10; style A picked by Winston): a member whose role colors their name takes that role color, so avatar and name match and change together; everyone else takes a stable hue, 32-bit FNV-1a over the lowercased user id mod 8, over `ROLE_COLOR_PALETTE` minus Sand and Slate in palette order (`memberAvatarHue` / `memberAvatarTint`, `packages/hooks/src/member-avatar-color.ts`, shared by both apps; its spec pins the hash and every hue's AA contrast). A group DM's title initials and the auth showcase stay neutral. A photo, the presence dot, the offline fade, the mention picker's role rows and the chapter mark tiles are unchanged. *(Changed 2026-11-10: until then the circle was the elevated `--popover` fill with the initials in `--muted-foreground` for everyone.)* |
| Author line | On the first row of a run only, baseline-aligned, 8 apart: the name in the `label` role (14 / 600) in the author's role color (their highest-ranked role that has one, [`rbac.md`](../../behavior/rbac.md) § Roles), or `--foreground` when they have none. The viewer's own name follows the same rule; no name takes the chapter accent ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §6, changed 2026-11-05). Then the time as a caption (12.5) in `--muted-foreground`. **Time only** (`formatTimeOfDay` from `@repo/formatting`, on both surfaces), never a date; `formatClock` prints one and is for the popovers, not the thread. |
| Your own name | Reads "You" (`resolveAuthorLabel`), in your role color or `--foreground` like anyone's name. The word is how a member spots their own run; it replaced the self bubble's accent fill. Until 2026-11-05 it was also drawn in `--accent-text`, which the owner took off with the rest of the decorative accent ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §6). | System posts | A post by the system actor (`SYSTEM_SENDER_ID`) takes its name and avatar from the [chat README](../../behavior/chat/README.md#hot-path-client-behavior) § Hot-path client behavior, not from the Avatar row above. The author line keeps the ordinary `label` style. |
| Body | The `body` role, 16 / 25, `--foreground`. No fill, no border, no padding, no width cap below the column. |
| Follow-on time | Web: the follow-on's own time as a caption in the avatar gutter, revealed with the row's hover, focus and tap state. Hours and minutes with no day period while the run's author line above already says AM or PM, and the full time once the run has crossed noon (`formatTimeOfDayShort` against the run's first message), since a run is measured row to row and can. **Delineation without a bubble.** Rows are told apart by the author line and the run spacing, not by an edge. Under a pointer hover, keyboard focus inside the row, or a tap reveal, the whole row fills `--surface-1`. That fill is pointer feedback, not information, so its ~1.1:1 step is acceptable under §2's rule for fills: nothing is lost if a member cannot see it. The day divider's hairline is the only rule drawn inside the thread.

#### Grouping

A row **starts a run** (and draws the avatar and author line) when any of these holds; otherwise it joins the run above:

| Condition | Why |
| --- | --- |
| It is the first row, or the row above has a different author | Authors compare by `authorGroupingKey`, which namespaces a Frapp user id apart from an imported source id and name, so twenty Discord authors with no Frapp account never merge into one run. A linked imported row groups as its member (#2878); an unlinked one by its source id and name together, since a Discord webhook or bridge posts every persona under one id. |
| Five minutes or more since the row above | A pause reads as a new thought. Five minutes is the window web shipped before this decision. |
| The row above is on another local calendar day | The day divider sits between them, and a run must not inherit the previous day's author line. |
| The row is a reply | The quote needs the author line under it to say who is answering. |
| The row, or the row above, is a card (poll, task, event, and the rest of `CARD_KINDS`) | A card is its own object in the channel, and its author line says who ran the command. An audit line counts as one here, so the message after it names its author, but one audit line under another stays in the stack (§ What rides the row, "Audit lines"). |
| The row above is deleted, or a blocked member's tombstone | Neither shows an author, so the next row has to. |
| The row carries a flag (#3921) | The flag rides the author line ([§ A flagged row](#a-flagged-row), #4288), so a flagged follow-on draws one. A deleted message and a tombstone draw no flag and don't break a run for one; an audit line stays in its stack. The row after a flagged one joins it as usual. |

The window is measured from the previous row, not from the run's first row. The rules live once, in `@repo/chat-core/grouping` (`GROUPING_WINDOW_MS`, `decorateThread`), which web and mobile both call, so the two surfaces cannot drift. Rows the viewer's block list holds back are not drawn, so they neither join nor break a run.

**Day divider.** A hairline (`--border`) on both sides of a centered caption, 12.5 / 600 in `--muted-foreground`: `Today`, `Yesterday`, then the weekday, month and day (`Sunday, Sep 28`), with the year added when it is not the current one (`Tuesday, Mar 3, 2025`: an imported archive spans years, and the divider is the only date there is), by the viewer's local calendar day. It is the only place a date appears in the thread, and it always starts a run. Mobile draws it too; before this decision mobile drew no divider at all. On mobile the divider and its row are one view inside the list cell, never two siblings: an inverted `FlatList` lays a cell out `column-reverse`, so as siblings the divider drew under its row, between a day's first and second messages (fixed 2026-11-06; `thread-message-row.tsx`).

#### What rides the row

- **Trailing markers.** `(edited)` and a pinned marker (`Pinned`, with the pin glyph on web; mobile has no pin glyph yet; `--accent-text` until 2026-11-05) trail the body's last line as captions in `--muted-foreground`, on every row, grouped or not. A card has no text line to trail, so its markers go on a line under the card, on both surfaces. This is what fixed [#2872](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2872): the marker used to live on the author line, which a grouped row does not draw. Neither marker shows on a deleted message. Where the body's last block is a list, a quote or a code block, the markers drop to their own line under it rather than breaking that block. A heading reads as a line of text in a message, since the allowlist unwraps it, so the markers trail it as they trail a paragraph. A divider or an image draws nothing and doesn't count as the last block.
- **Reply.** The quote sits above the author line, starting in the avatar gutter with a 2px `--popover` elbow that runs into the quote and stretches to its height, pointing down into the avatar. The quote is a `--popover` block at radius 8 (`chip`) with 4px × 8px padding that hugs a short quote; web's composer strip draws the same block. Inside it, the parent's author at 600 in `--foreground`, then the preview in `--muted-foreground` on the same line, as a caption, wrapping to at most two lines before an ellipsis and breaking inside a long word or URL rather than running past the row. On web the author's name never splits across the two lines, and a name wider than the quote ends in its own ellipsis. Activating it scrolls to the parent; on mobile the whole quote line is the tap target, not just the block. *(2026-11-10, #4001: the preview was one line with an ellipsis and no block, which Paul read as running off the screen and as too easily mistaken for text. Two lines, not more, as iMessage and WhatsApp clamp a quote while Discord keeps one; the block is their filled quote, the elbow is Discord's.)* A reply always starts a run, so the author line is always under its quote.
- **Attachments** render under the body. An attachment-only message draws **no text row**: the image or file sits directly under the author line (or directly in a follow-on row). Its trailing markers go on a line of their own under the attachment, as a card's go under the card.
- **Reactions** sit under the body, left-aligned, 6px below, gap 6. A deleted message draws none. The reacted chip is the Accent badge recipe (§5) at height 26 / radius 9 carrying emoji + count, and the add-reaction chip is the same geometry in elevated `--popover` with no border and a `--muted-foreground` "+". On mobile the add chip follows existing reactions only and opens the long-press actions sheet, whose top row holds the quick reactions and an add-reaction cell (`AddReactionGlyph`, web's `SmilePlus`) that opens every emoji; a message nobody has reacted to draws no reaction row. Both MUST take a ≥ 44px hit area (§2) despite the 26px chip.
  - **The chip's 44px hit area grows vertically, not on every side.** Chips sit 6px apart, so a hit area overhanging 9px all round has each chip's overlay covering ~3px of the *visible* chip before it, and the later sibling wins the overlap. A control whose right edge cannot be clicked is a worse defect than the one it fixes. The vertical overhang lands in the row's own padding, where there is no sibling to swallow it.
- **Delivery state.** This resolves the TODO-DESIGN that asked where the undrawn pending and failed states go. A pending row draws its body in `--muted-foreground` and nothing else: no caption, no spinner, no line under it, so a send never pushes the thread up a line and drops it back when the server confirms (owner decision, 2026-11-06, after a `Sending…` caption under the body did exactly that on a phone; the same rule is [§ Pending states](#pending-states) for the rest of the app). A card in flight, which has no text to gray, draws at half opacity. A screen reader hears "Sending" (web: a visually hidden line in the status region; mobile: the body's hint). A failed row draws its body in `--muted-foreground` and, under it, the error as a caption in `--destructive-text` (the lifted danger tone, §1) with Retry and Discard (mobile reads the same lift as `semantic.destructiveText`, added 2026-11-05; a caption still drawn in the solid `--destructive` clears 4.5:1 on the plain row, §5), per [resilience](../resilience/README.md). An `unconfirmed` or `recorded` heavy-command row keeps its muted note and never offers Discard. The status region is mounted empty and only speaks when it has something to say.
- **Audit lines.** A `#chapter-audit` entry (a `system_audit` row the audit mirror wrote, `isAuditLine` in `@repo/chat-core/cards`) is not a card and not a post (owner decision, 2026-11-06: the card was too big for a log). It is one line in the caption role: the shield glyph at 14 in `--muted` in the avatar gutter, then the actor's name at 600 in `--foreground`, what they did in words (`auditLineText`: "updated the chapter profile", never the `chapter_profile_updated` key) and, after a `·`, the fields it touched ("accent color"), in `--muted-foreground`, then the time in `--muted`. No avatar, no author line, no frame and no mono: "Frapp" posting it says nothing, and the line already names who acted. Entries stack 2 apart, the first 8 below the row above, and the row after a stack starts a run. Long-press (mobile) and the hover bar (web) still open the actions, and reactions sit under the line. The server's other `system_audit` notices (a poll closing, an invite accepted, the welcome) carry no payload and stay sentences from Frapp. Audit lines arrive silently: they never push, whatever the member's notification level (`decidePush` in `push-rules.ts` refuses a mirror row), and never count as unread ([chat README](../../behavior/chat/README.md#read-receipts) § Read Receipts).
- **Cards** (poll, task, event, loading, and the rest) start a run and keep their card surface in the body column: `--card` with a hairline at radius 14. A card is a thing posted into the channel, so it still gets a frame. The frame belongs to the card, not to the message.
- **A deleted message** draws the placeholder in the body's place, italic, in `--muted-foreground`, with no reactions, quote or attachments. It keeps its author line if it started a run.
- **A blocked member's tombstone** is one muted line in the body column. It names no author, so the row after it always starts a run.
- **Mention/DM red applies unchanged in chat**, including in accent-tinted chapters; the value and its rule are owned by [foundations.md](foundations.md) §5. Badge recipe and placement: §5 and [navigation.md](../mobile/navigation.md).

A flagged message always draws an author line, even inside a run ([§ Grouping](#grouping)), so the flag never needs a line of its own. #### In-body mention chip

`@Name` inside a message body takes a muted amber chip on the **handle alone**: fill `--mention-chip` `#2D2617`, text `--mention-chip-text` `#EED7A8`, radius 5, inline padding, at the weight of the text around it. The chip is the same whoever it names; whether the row is tinted is the next rule's job. **A handle chips only when the send reached someone** (2026-11-07, a tester's `@bigsigma`, a name nobody holds, drew as a mention): a member's handle when that member is in the message's resolved `mentions`, a role's name when the message reached anyone, and nothing on a message that reached nobody (`mentionChipFilter` in `@repo/validation`). (This was a TODO-DESIGN: the reference draws mention red only as a list badge and a notification dot, and drew nothing for the in-body case.) *(Muted 2026-11-06, owner direction "way less intrusive", set beside Discord's chip. It was fill `#4C3A1A` and a saturated `#FAA81A` at weight 600, 5.54:1, which read louder than the message around it. The tone now carries the handle and the weight stays the body's.)*

- **It is not mention red, and that is [foundations.md](foundations.md) §5's own rule, not a departure from it.** Mention red is a badge fill carrying white text, and §5 states outright that a surface rendering that hue *as text* needs a lifted tone which does not exist. This surface renders as text.
- **The fill is opaque where §5's tint recipe would use 13% alpha.** On a plain row the chip sits on `--background`, or on `--surface-1` while its row is hovered, and the opaque pair measures **10.65:1** on either. On a row that mentions you it sits on the chapter's `--accent-subtle` (`--accent-subtle-hover` under the pointer), which differs per chapter, so that pair is checked across every shipped seed rather than quoted as one figure *(2026-11-06)*. *(Corrected 2026-09-29: the opacity was first argued from the self bubble. The chip could land on the per-chapter `--primary` fill, where a 13% tint measured 1.94:1 at best and 1.03:1 at worst across the seed corpus. That surface is gone. Opacity stays because hover changes what is under the chip, and an alpha tint would give it two figures.)* The figures are asserted in `apps/web/components/chat/chat-contrast.spec.ts`.
- **Which run gets the chip is the server's answer, not the renderer's, and it is answered against the server's string.** Mentions resolve server-side from plain `@token` text in the stored body ([chat/README.md](../../behavior/chat/README.md) § Mentions). The chip runs `packages/validation`'s tokenizer over **that raw body** to decide *whether* a handle was addressed, and over the rendered text only to decide *where* the chip sits. The two are different strings, because CommonMark decodes character references before a renderer sees them, and the gap is forgeable: `&#64;PresidentJane` carries no literal `@`, so the API notifies nobody, while a renderer tokenizing its own decoded text would mark her as addressed. Any member can type that, in the one UI whose job is to say who was pinged. Web's composer draws its authored mention in the same chip, so a handle does not change colour on send there.
- **Both clients draw it from one plugin** (`remarkMentionChips`, `packages/chat-core/src/markdown-mentions.ts`), so they agree on which runs are mentions, with one exception: mobile links a bare URL first and a link label is never chipped, so `https://youtube.com/@MrBeast` chips on web and not on mobile until web links bare URLs too (#2862). Web hands the element its `MENTION_CHIP` class (`apps/web/components/chat/chip.ts`). Mobile (since 2026-11-06, `apps/mobile/components/chat/message-markdown.tsx`) draws it as a nested `Text` in the same pair, bold or italic when the run around it is. React Native gives a nested `Text` a fill but no padding or radius, so the mobile chip is the tinted run itself, with square ends. Mobile's composer is a plain field and draws no chip while typing, so on mobile a handle does change colour on send. That is a deliberate departure, not a gap: a chip in a plain `TextInput` would mean replacing it with a rich editor, which nothing else on mobile needs.
- **A chip that names one member opens their profile** (mobile 2026-11-07, web 2026-11-09, [#3726](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/3726)), resolved only against the message's server-resolved `mentions` ([chat/README.md](../../behavior/chat/README.md) § Mentions). A role mention, and a handle that reached nobody as a member, opens nothing. On web an opening chip holds a `<button>` inside its `<mark>` (`MENTION_CHIP_BUTTON` in `apps/web/components/chat/chip.ts`), so it is reached by Tab and pressed by Enter or Space, while the mark keeps painting the same box as any chip: a button is an inline-block whose fill would cover the whole line and stand taller than its neighbours. The button adds a pointer and an underline on hover, and the offset focus ring. Mobile's chip is a nested `Text` with an `onPress`.
- **Open for the Design bar:** the chip's amber sits in the same family as the pinned house gold of the ✦ Ask card below. They are separated by context and geometry today (Ask chips are 27–28px controls in a card's source row; this is an inline run inside a sentence) and by hue and strength (the pale `#EED7A8` against `#EFB63B`), and the Ask tokens are untouched. If the two ever read as the same thing, this is the one to move.

#### Editing (web)

The editor replaces the body **in place**, in the same row, between the same reply quote and attachment list. It is not a dialog, popover or floating card, because none of those is in place: the web board draws Edit as a control in the in-row hover bar ([web-framework.dc.html](../web-dashboard/reference/web-framework.dc.html) pin 12) and draws no separate edit surface anywhere.

- **It fills the body column.** Without a bubble the column has a definite width, so the field takes all of it.
- It is the §4 text input (input hairline, radius 12) drawing the same 16 / 25 body type, so a draft wraps close to where the message wrapped.
- **It grows with the draft** to the composer's cap and scrolls past it, from a 60px floor. The cap is an absolute length, not a fraction of the viewport: the editor sits inside the timeline's scroller, which is always shorter than the viewport, so a `vh` cap can push Save and Cancel below the fold on a short window.
- **It is the composer's editor, and mentions stay chips** (#2236, 2026-11-11). `InlineMessageEditor` (`apps/web/components/chat/inline-message-editor.tsx`) is Tiptap with the composer's own mention extension (`chat-editor.ts`), so `@` opens the same member-and-role list and a pick inserts the same chip, and every token the row painted as a chip opens as that chip (`buildEditDoc`, from the row's `useMentionChips`). Pressing Edit changes nothing on screen, as in Discord, whose edit box is its composer. The list opens only once the member changes the text, so an edit of a message that ends in an `@` handle left as text doesn't open with the list over it. Enter saves, Shift+Enter breaks the line, Escape cancels; with the `@` list open, Enter and Escape act on the list first. It still sends plain text (`chatText`), exactly as typed: no markdown shortcut turns `**x**` or `- ` into formatting that the plain text would drop, and pasted lines arrive as line breaks, not paragraphs (the composer shares all three, `chatTextKit`, `pasteAsLines`, `chatText`). The stored body and the wire contract are unchanged. Until 2026-11-11 it was a plain textarea: every chip dropped to raw `@Name` text on Edit and `@` offered nothing, so a tester adding a mention typed `@shawn` by hand.

Mobile edits through the actions sheet ([screens.md](../mobile/screens.md) s05, #2775).

#### Per-message actions

- **Web: an icon-only action bar on the message's top edge, beside the message.** Quick reactions, the emoji picker, Reply, Save, Edit and Delete, as icons in that order, then on someone else's message a More button (`MoreHorizontal`, named "More actions") whose menu holds Report message and Block <name> (#2687). The two share one button rather than adding two icons to a bar that already wraps, and neither applies to your own message, so it never adds to the nine. Each button carries its accessible name and a native tooltip (`title`), and a toggle (Save) announces its state with `aria-pressed`. The bar is centred on the row's top edge, with its left edge 8px past the right edge of what the message draws (its text, image or card), so it sits beside the message rather than at the far side of the pane, and a short message's own text is never under it. Where that would run past the row (a long message, a narrow pane) it sits flush with the row's right edge instead. Its place is measured on reveal (`placeActionBar` in `message-item.tsx`), since no CSS sets a box at the lesser of two other boxes' edges. It has an opaque `--card` fill, a hairline and radius 10, so it can overlap text without the text showing through. It reserves no height. It rides a zero-height sticky track inside the message: once the row's top scrolls under the top of the timeline, the bar holds a few pixels below it while the message scrolls under, so a tall message keeps its actions in reach, and lets go as the message's bottom reaches the top. The offset fits a one-line bar on either pointer; a bar wrapped to two lines on a narrow coarse-pointer thread still loses part of its first line there. It wraps rather than spilling past the row's left edge: on a coarse pointer each icon is 44px, and the nine on your own message are wider than a 375px thread. It is revealed with the row's hover, a `:focus-visible` inside the row, and tap state, and held while its More menu or emoji picker is open. Not `:focus-within`: a mouse click on a control in the row leaves focus there, and that pinned the bar and the row's lift on screen after the pointer left. While hidden it takes `pointer-events: none`, since `opacity: 0` alone still hit-tests. Icon-only closed [#2247](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2247)'s action-bar item; the labelled chips were wider than a compact row.
  - *(Corrected 2026-11-06, owner report: the bar used to sit 16px in from the row's right edge, which on a wide pane put it hundreds of pixels from a short message or an image, and a row scrolled flush with the top of the viewport clipped it. It was also revealed by `:focus-within`, and a mouse click toggled the tap state, so a click on the message held it open.)*
  - *(Corrected 2026-09-29: the cluster used to attach to the bubble, not the lane. It rode a zero-height `flex-1` track beside a shrink-wrapped bubble, with `rtl` on the incoming side so `justify-end-safe` fell back to the right edge. That existed only because a sided bubble has a variable edge; a full-width row has a fixed corner.)*
- **Per-message actions reach a coarse pointer by tap, not by drawing a second geometry.** `:hover` never fires on a touch device, and the reference draws no touch affordance for this (#1193). Mobile is not a precedent to follow here: its reactions render always visible, and a new reaction, Reply, Edit and Delete live in the long-press actions sheet (`spec/ui/mobile/screens.md` s05, #2775), a native bottom-sheet pattern with no hover to reveal. The web treatment below is this surface's own answer, not a port.
  - Tapping a row toggles that row's bar, and tapping a second row closes the first's. A mouse press anywhere also closes it, since a mouse never opens it. There is one reveal id per list, never a `useState` per row, or two rows could sit open at once.
  - The shared hook is `apps/web/lib/hooks/use-tap-revealed-message.ts`. Its one caller is `apps/web/components/chat/message-timeline.tsx`. The #3785 message-thread panel draws its parent outside that list but hands the list one state it shares with it (`tapRevealed`, #4034), so the parent and a reply never sit open at once. It stays a hook so a second list cannot get this subtly wrong.
  - The toggle is a plain `onClick`, not a touch or press handler, so a scroll gesture cannot trigger it. A click from a mouse does not toggle it: a mouse has hover, which already shows the bar and hides it again on the way out.
  - It bails out on a click whose target is inside a `button`/`a`/form control. That covers every interactive descendant (the reaction chips, the emoji-picker trigger, a card's own Vote/RSVP/checkbox controls) without each one needing its own `stopPropagation`.
  - It checks `window.getSelection()` scoped to the row's own subtree. Finishing a text selection inside the message does not also flip the bar open, and a stale selection left over from a *different* message does not block this row's tap (`apps/web/components/chat/message-item.tsx`).

## 12. Proportion meter

The bar behind a tally — a poll's per-option share, a progress figure. Track and fill are both full-round ([foundations.md](foundations.md) §8's second sanctioned pill); the track carries no border. Web implementation: [`apps/web/components/shared/meter.ts`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/meter.ts), which exports one paint and two heights (6px standalone, 4px in-flow inside a message card) on `table-controls.ts`'s split.

| Slot | Spec |
| ---- | ---- |
| Track | `--background` — the **floor** of the §2 ladder, so the groove recedes rather than rises |
| Fill | `accent-9`, width = the share, `denominator > 0` guarded ([`../../behavior/integrations.md`](../../behavior/integrations.md)) |
| Figure | count and percentage as text beside the bar; the bar itself is `aria-hidden` |

- **This is not the reference being overridden.** The boards draw the track at `--popover` ([`reference/canvas-screens.dc.html`](reference/canvas-screens.dc.html) s10, s22), and [`../README.md`](../README.md)'s precedence rule says references beat docs on visuals. They are not contradicted here, because they are silent on the case that breaks: the Canvas header states that the **demo tenant runs the house-gold accent**, so every meter on the board is drawn against one seed — and house gold is a light accent, where a `--popover` track works. The dark-seeded chapters the accent engine also has to serve are simply not drawn. Where the reference draws a case it wins; where it draws one seed and the engine ships nineteen, the measurement decides the other eighteen. The drawn geometry — full-round track and fill, 8px, accent-filled — is transcribed unchanged.
- **The track recedes, and that is the load-bearing decision.** A meter is the one element that can appear on any container — a card, an elevated `SheetContent`, a dialog — so a track painted with a *raised* tone runs out of ladder exactly as §10's nested states do. Measured worst case across all 19 seeds and both containers: `bg-popover` and the accent tint both wash out to **1.000:1 / 1.001:1 inside a dialog**, which is §2's alias failure in a new place. `--background` is the bottom of the ladder, so it sits below whatever it is placed on. It is also the honest reading of "elevation is luminance" — the filled part of a meter is raised, so the empty part is the floor showing through.
- **The obvious fix was also wrong, and only the second relationship shows it.** A meter has two: fill-against-track and track-against-container. The chat slice shipped `bg-input`, which is a real 1.540:1 groove against `--card` — and collided with the fill at **1.017:1 under `#800000`**, because a white wash at 14% landed almost exactly where a maroon `accent-9` did. A chapter branded maroon shipped a bar whose fill was invisible against its own groove. By [#2541](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2541) `#800000` no longer painted itself (the generator swaps in its own step 9, `#F42F22`); the dark red still colliding was `#8B0000`, at about 1.07:1, until the engine's fill floor ([accent-engine.md](accent-engine.md) §8) lifted it to `#C34437` (`#D75748` since [#2586](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2586)). The ordering held. Worst-case fill-against-track by candidate, measured after [#2586](https://github.com/pdcarlson/Frapp/issues/2586) extended the floor to hover: `bg-input` 2.681, `bg-border` 3.298, `bg-popover` 3.775, accent tint 3.847, **`--background` 4.701**.
- **The bar is never the only signal.** The fill now clears [README.md](README.md) §6's 3:1 non-text floor against the track for every seed, in palettes written since #2541 ([accent-engine.md](accent-engine.md) §4 covers stored ones), but a bar's length cannot carry the exact figure. The count and percentage therefore render as text beside the bar, and the bar is `aria-hidden`. Removing that text on the grounds that the bar shows it is a defect.
- **The fill is the chapter accent and that is sanctioned.** The reference draws both its meters in `accent-9`, and a tally is an accent-worthy *stat* under §5, not a status — so "a status badge is never the chapter accent" does not reach it.
- Measured in [`apps/web/components/shared/meter-contrast.spec.ts`](../../../apps/web/components/shared/meter-contrast.spec.ts), including the rejected candidates, so a later "simplify to the surface ladder" fails loudly rather than reintroducing an invisible track.
