2 · Copy rules and the deck

No em dashes. Extends the web-greenfield product-copy lock to marketing copy. Proposal; record it in the unfrozen landing README.

No numbers that are not commitments. The only figures on the page are $0, $149, 14 days, day 15. No member counts, no chapter counts, no "five minutes".

No testimonials until real ones exist. Then they need a name, a chapter and consent, or the surfaces.md "illustrative" label.

Never a machine that answers. No "AI", "assistant" or "Ask" feature copy on the page, and no ✦ Ask pill in the frames: the shipped pill opens an "Ask isn't ready" notice (apps/web/components/layout/ask-pill.tsx), so drawing it as a working control misrepresents the product. The locked tagline is decision D8.

Check in, never RSVP. spec/behavior/events.md records pre-event RSVP intent as not modelled; the shipped event card carries a Check in action on an immutable snapshot. No Going or Can't make it controls anywhere, and no count that a member could not see.

Dues do not land in chat. spec/behavior/chat/integrations.md lists the dues chat kind as a stub renderer, so the hero names events, check-in and points only. Dues invoicing through Stripe is real on the dashboard and stays in the Treasurer line and the Pro list.

No agent UI, no App Store CTA. Store listings are an Ops step (assets.md §1); until a listing is live, the page sells the web sign-up only.

Sentence case on buttons and links. Both reference boards draw "Sign in", "Create an account", "Join chapter". writing.md §2's example row reads "Get Started"; the code epic amends that row rather than shipping two cases.

Officer roles are the universal three. President, Treasurer, Secretary exist across IFC, NPC, NPHC and MGC councils; Standards, Risk and Recruitment names vary per organization (positioning.md Vocabulary-first).

Frames carry demo data and say so. Caption under each frame: "Demo chapter. Names and messages are illustrative." Chapter, people and events in the frames are invented; nothing from a real chapter.

H1, drawn: Run your chapter where it already talks.

H1, alternates: The chapter already lives in chat. Run it there. · One thread for the whole chapter. · Chat first. Ops inside it.

Eyebrow, drawn: Chapter operations, chat first. Alternates: For chapter officers · Free for chat, Pro for the ops.

Section H2s: Ops happen in the thread. · Check-in that keeps its own books. · Free for chat. Pro for the ops. · Ask your chapter anything.

Meta title and OG: keep layout.tsx as built ("Signet. Ask your chapter anything."); the description sentence should lose "operating system for Greek Life ... billing" and match the hero lead.

3 · Decisions for Paul (each drawn one way; the alternative is a flip)

D1 · Which gold. Drawn: #DDB844 (mark gold, accent seed, web --primary, the Sign in button's color). Alternative: #EFB63B (house gold, mobile primary, Ask pill). Flip: Tweaks → Gold on the Desktop and Phone boards. The brief said #DDB844 is the only accent; brand-identity §2 says the house UI accent is #EFB63B. Both are true statements about different roles, which is why it is a decision. For #DDB844: it is the crest's own gold and what a new chapter sees on its first screen. Against it: continuity only holds for chapters that never change their accent, while #EFB63B is the one gold that is house-owned and never retints, which is the property a storefront wants. In code the choice is one token, so the flip is cheap either way.

D2 · Privacy and Terms in the top nav. Drawn: no; they live in the footer, and the legal pages already cross-link each other in their own header. Alternative: the brief's "Product · Sign in · Privacy · Terms". Flip: Tweaks → Nav on the Desktop board.

D3 · Pricing on the page. Drawn: a full section with Free beside Chapter Pro, because positioning.md calls $149 a commercial commitment the public site sells and the free tier real. Alternative: a one-line pricing sentence in the hero and a /pricing route later. Recommendation: keep the section; it is the only place the free tier is stated honestly.

D4 · Motion. Re-decided after the research round. Drawn: the Motion sheet's spec, which is a signature moment on the crest, once-only reveals below the fold through a 22-line client wrapper, and hover and focus on chrome. It asks for two things in writing: a fourth motion class (signature, 520ms, one moment per load) added to README §7 and foundations §11 in the same commit as the stylesheet, and brand sign-off that a reveal mask and a backing light behind the crest are page treatment rather than mark design under brand-identity §2. Alternative: the still page, hover and focus only, no amendment. The signature moment runs on both page boards now so it can be judged by eye.

D5 · CTA case. Drawn: sentence case ("Get started", "Sign in"), matching both reference boards. Alternative: writing.md §2's "Get Started". Whichever wins, one case everywhere and the other doc amended.

D6 · Phone width. Drawn at 390 (the Design type's phone size). The brief said ~375; the layout has no breakpoint between them, so nothing changes but the frame.

D7 · "Documentation" in the footer. Drawn: dropped; it points at the GitHub docs/guides tree, which is contributor documentation. Restore it if Paul wants a public path to the repo.

D8 · The tagline. Drawn: "Ask your chapter anything." once, at the close, because brand-identity §1 locks it and the sign-in screen the CTA lands on says it. The claim check flagged it: spec/behavior/ai.md says no shipped surface can answer a question today. Alternative: hold the tagline off the storefront until Ask ships and close on "Everything your chapter needs is already in chat." The hero no longer carries it either way.

D9 · Which hero. Four fold boards, identical except for the visual: A, the crest with the signature moment (the Desktop board); B, the officer's chat at the fold with the event card, bleeding off the right edge; C, type as the image, a 96px H1 across twelve columns over a three-row ledger of what ships, the crest a 40px mark on the eyebrow rule; D, the member's phone showing the locked s04 chat home, cropped at the fold. Recommendation: B. It puts the proof where the brief wanted it and answers the question a visitor has in the first second, what is this; the crest keeps the lockup and the closing, and the signature moment moves to the closing crest, playing on view through the same wrapper. C is the strongest editorial statement and the cheapest to build; D is the warmest and the most consumer, but it shows the member view to an officer audience.
